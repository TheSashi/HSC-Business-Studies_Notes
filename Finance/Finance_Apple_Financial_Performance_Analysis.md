# Apple Inc. — Financial Performance Analysis (2024–2025)

## Overview

This case study analyses Apple Inc.'s financial performance over the 2024 and 2025 financial years using three key financial ratios. It also recommends a financial strategy to improve cash flow efficiency.

> **Apple Inc.** is a large global technology company that designs, manufactures and sells consumer electronics, software and digital services worldwide.

---

## Key Financial Ratios

### 1. Profitability — Net Profit Ratio

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Profit | $93,736 million | $112,010 million | ↑ |
| Revenue | $391,035 million | $416,161 million | ↑ |
| **Net Profit Ratio** | **23.97%** | **26.92%** | **↑ Improved** |

**Formula:** `Net Profit Ratio = (Net Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100`

**Analysis:**
- Apple retained approximately **24 cents per $1 of revenue** in 2024, improving to **27 cents per $1** in 2025
- Driven by strong growth in **services revenue** (Apple Music, iCloud, Apple TV+, App Store) — up from $96,169M to $109,158M
- Services generate **higher margins** than physical products due to lower production and distribution costs
- Operating expenses rose more slowly than revenue, allowing greater profit retention

**Benchmark:** Samsung operates at ~10% net profit ratio — Apple's 26.92% demonstrates significantly stronger profitability

**Implication:** Improved profitability supports reinvestment, expansion, and long-term growth

---

### 2. Efficiency — Expense Ratio

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating Expenses | $57,467 million | $62,151 million | ↑ |
| Revenue | $391,035 million | $416,161 million | ↑ |
| **Expense Ratio** | **14.7%** | **14.93%** | **↑ Worsened** |

**Formula:** `Expense Ratio = (Operating Expenses ÷ Revenue) × 100`

**Analysis:**
- Apple spent ~15 cents per $1 of revenue on operating costs in 2024, rising slightly in 2025
- R&D costs increased from $31,370M → $34,550M
- Selling, marketing & admin costs rose from $26,097M → $27,601M
- These investments support innovation and future revenue growth

**Benchmark:** McDonald's has an expense ratio of ~30% due to labour-intensive operations — Apple's 14.93% indicates strong cost efficiency

**Implication:** While higher expenses place short-term pressure on profitability, R&D spending supports long-term competitiveness

---

### 3. Efficiency of Collection — Accounts Receivable Turnover (Days)

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounts Receivable | $33,410 million | $39,777 million | ↑ |
| Revenue | $391,035 million | $416,161 million | ↑ |
| **Collection Period** | **~32 days** | **~35 days** | **↑ Worsened** |

**Formula:** `Accounts Receivable Turnover (Days) = (Accounts Receivable ÷ Revenue) × 365`

**Analysis:**
- **Ideal benchmark:** 30 days
- Apple is taking longer to collect cash from customers
- Linked to expanded global sales and more flexible credit terms in services and international markets
- Cash is tied up for longer periods, reducing short-term cash flow efficiency

**Implication:** Slower receivables collection places pressure on liquidity and requires management attention

---

## Recommended Financial Strategy: Factoring

**Factoring** is a cash flow management strategy where a business sells its accounts receivable to a debt collection agency in exchange for **immediate cash inflow**.

### How It Works
1. Apple sells outstanding invoices to a third-party debt collector
2. Apple receives cash immediately (minus a fee)
3. The debt collector assumes responsibility for collecting from customers

### Benefits for Apple
- **Improved liquidity** — cash received sooner rather than waiting 35 days
- **Reduced admin burden** — staff no longer chase payments; can focus on revenue-generating activities
- **Emergency fund** — acts as short-term cash injection if needed
- **Reduced reliance on short-term borrowing**

### Costs
- Factoring involves a fee (reduces total amount received)
- Does not receive full invoice value

### Additional Strategy: Early Payment Discounts
- Offer customers a small discount for paying before the due date
- Encourages faster settlement of accounts
- Reduces profit margin per sale but provides immediate cash flow injection

## Key Exam Points

- **Profitability improved** due to services revenue growth and higher margins
- **Efficiency slightly worsened** due to increased R&D and operating costs
- **Cash flow management worsened** due to longer collection periods
- **Factoring** is the recommended strategy to improve accounts receivable efficiency
- Apple must balance investment in innovation with cost control to maintain long-term financial stability
