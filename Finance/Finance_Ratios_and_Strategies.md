# Financial Ratios and Strategies

> **HSC Business Studies — Finance** | All 7 financial ratios with formulas, linked strategies, cash flow management, profitability strategies, and cost centres.

---

## 1. Financial Ratios — Complete Reference

| # | Ratio | Formula | Objective | Ideal | Linked Strategies |
|---|-------|---------|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Current Ratio** | Current Assets / Current Liabilities | Liquidity | 2:1 | Control over current assets/liabilities, leasing, sale and leaseback |
| 2 | **Gearing Ratio** | Total Liabilities / Total Equity | Solvency | 0.5:1 to 1:1 | Choose best source of financing for financial situation |
| 3 | **Gross Profit Ratio** | Gross Profit / Sales × 100 | Profitability | Compare to previous periods & industry | Revenue control (marketing mix), cost control (bulk buying, global sourcing, outsourcing, cost centres) |
| 4 | **Net Profit Ratio** | Net Profit / Sales × 100 | Profitability | Compare | Same as gross profit |
| 5 | **Return on Equity** | Net Profit (after tax) / Total Equity × 100 | Profitability | Compare | Same as gross profit |
| 6 | **Expense Ratio** | Expenses / Sales × 100 | Efficiency | Compare | Cost controls |
| 7 | **Accounts Receivable Turnover** | Step 1: Sales / AR → Step 2: 365 / turnover | Efficiency | 30 days | Factoring, discounts for early payment, credit policies |

## 2. Cash Flow Management

Cash flow management assists in achieving the **liquidity** objective. The cash flow statement shows cash inflows and outflows.

### Why Cash Flow Statements Matter
Even with good profit, a business may be in financial difficulty because:
1. **Credit sales** — recorded in P&L before cash received (high AR turnover)
2. **Holding stock** that is hard to sell — may not earn revenue to pay creditors
3. **Negative cash flow** — unable to pay short-term debt as it falls due

### Cash Flow Management Strategies

| Strategy | How It Works |
|----------|-------------|
| **Distribution of Payments** | Spread expenses over the whole year for equal monthly outflow (e.g., monthly insurance instead of yearly) |
| **Prepay Expenses** | Pay rent and interest in advance |
| **Discounts for Early Payment** | Encourage customers to pay early by offering a discount |
| **Factoring** | Sell accounts receivable to debt collection agency for instant cash (minus fee) |

> **Factoring Example:** Business sells $150,000 AR to agency at 10% fee → receives $135,000 instantly. Agency gets $15,000 for collection service.

## 3. Profitability Strategies

Profitability management is split into **cost control** and **revenue control**.

### Revenue Control
- Finance works with **marketing** to generate more revenue
- Changing the marketing mix to increase sales

### Cost Control
- Finance works with **operations** to minimise costs
- Departments have budgets to work within

### Fixed vs Variable Costs

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Fixed Costs** | Don't change with output | Salaries, rent, lease payments |
| **Variable Costs** | Vary with output/sales | Wages, overtime, advertising |

### Strategies to Manage Variable Costs
- Negotiate discounts with suppliers (requires long-term relationships)
- **Supplier rationalisation** — reduce number of suppliers for bulk buying
- Switch to cheaper suppliers (maintain quality)
- Replace labour with equipment/technology
- Outsource to decrease labour costs
- Increase worker productivity (training, machinery)

### Cost Centres
- A department or unit within a business that is responsible for managing its own costs
- Allocate budgets and responsibilities to each centre
- Find ways to minimise costs at the local level

## 4. Profitability Ratios — Worked Example

**Given:** Gross Profit $800,000 | Net Profit $150,000 | Owners Equity $500,000 | Revenue $1,000,000

| Ratio | Calculation | Result |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| Gross Profit Ratio | $800,000 / $1,000,000 × 100 | **80%** |
| Net Profit Ratio | $150,000 / $1,000,000 × 100 | **15%** |
| Return on Equity | $150,000 / $500,000 × 100 | **30%** |

> **No ideal ratio** — depends on industry. Always compare to previous periods and industry averages.

## 5. McDonald's Model Answer — Ratio Analysis

### Current Ratio (Liquidity)
- **2022:** 1.43:1 → **2023:** 1.16:1 — **worsened**
- Measures ability to pay current liabilities with current assets
- Similarity: Inventory ~$52 million both years
- Difference: Huge increase in short-term borrowing of $2 million in 2023
- **Judgement:** Liquidity has deteriorated

### Gearing Ratio (Solvency)
- **2022:** (598)% → **2023:** (869)% — negative due to share buyback
- Still relies more on debt than equity

### Net Profit Ratio (Profitability)
- **2022:** 27% → **2023:** 33% — **improved**
- McDonald's implemented new marketing strategy (Marvel promotional campaign)
- Revenue increased from $23,182M to $25,493M while costs remained steady
- **Judgement:** Profitability strategies effective

### Accounts Receivable Turnover (Efficiency)
- **2022:** 31.74 days → **2023:** 32.88 days — slight increase

### Recommendation
- Use **cost centres** to improve profitability — allocate budgets and responsibilities to minimise costs (e.g., buying raw materials locally instead of controlling from US headquarters)

## 6. Key Equations Students Must Memorise

```
Cash Flow: Opening Balance + Receipts - Payments = Closing Balance
Income Statement: Sales - COGS = Gross Profit; Gross Profit - Expenses = Net Profit
COGS: Opening Stock + Purchases - Closing Stock
Balance Sheet: Owners Equity = Assets - Liabilities
Break-Even (units): Fixed Costs / (Selling Price - Variable Cost per unit)
Working Capital: Current Assets - Current Liabilities
Capacity Utilisation: (Actual Output / Maximum Possible Output) × 100
```
