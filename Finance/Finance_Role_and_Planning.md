# Role of Financial Management & Financial Planning

> **HSC Business Studies — Finance** | Covers the role of FM, financial planning, budgets, record systems, and financial control.

---

## 1. Definition and Strategic Role

**Financial Management** deals with the **analysis, interpretation and evaluation** of all financial records. The financial manager's responsibility is to source finance that enables the business to achieve its strategic goals.

**Strategic Role:**
- Setting objectives throughout the business and deciding what resources are needed
- Developing a strategic plan ensures the business **survives and grows** in a competitive environment
- Provides a **framework for all other business activities** — operations, marketing, and HR all depend on finance

---

## 2. Financial Objectives

### Short-Term Goals (within 12 months)

| Objective | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Liquidity** | Ability to pay back current liabilities with current assets |
| **Profitability** | Ability of the business to maximise profits |
| **Efficiency** | Ability to manage costs and collection of money from customers |

### Long-Term Goals (2–5 years)

| Objective | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Growth** | Ability of the business to grow and expand |
| **Solvency** | How the business is financed (debt vs equity) — ability to meet long-term financial commitments |

> **Key relationship:** Long-term goals depend on profitability in the short term resulting from increased operating efficiency.

---

## 3. Interdependence

Finance is mutually reliant on all other key business functions:

| Function | Interdependence with Finance |
|----------|------------------------------|
| **Operations** | Finance provides funding and budgets; operations produces goods to generate revenue |
| **Marketing** | Finance provides marketing budgets; marketing generates revenue through sales |
| **HR** | Finance allocates payroll budgets; HR manages staffing costs and productivity |

---

## 4. Financial Planning

Financial management is responsible for financial planning in both the **short term** and **long term**.

### Components of Financial Planning
1. Determining the **viability of projects**
2. Making decisions and projections for future **liquidity and performance**
3. Setting **financial goals** and determining/implementing strategies to achieve them

### Financial Needs
- **New business** → determine establishment costs → finance through debt and/or equity
- **Existing business** → determine financing required for future plans

---

## 5. Budgets

Budgets show cash required for planned outlays for a particular period and cost of capital expenditure.

### Types of Budgets

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Operating Budgets** | Main business activities — sales, production, raw materials, labour |
| **Project Budgets** | Capital expenditure and research & development |
| **Financial Budgets** | Forecast funds required to pay for inputs and anticipated inflows from sales |

### Role of Budgets
- **Planning:** Give departments a financial plan to follow
- **Implementation:** Used to identify variances and investigate areas needing attention

---

## 6. Record Systems

Systems to ensure data is recorded accurately, reliably, efficiently and accessible.

- **Legal requirement** to provide information to shareholders and ATO
- Most errors occur due to **human error** in data entry
- **Audits:** All public companies legally require annual audits by qualified independent external auditors to ensure accounts show a true and fair view

---

## 7. Financial Control

Controls ensure financial plans are implemented effectively and achieving goals:

1. **Monitoring and controlling budgets** — ongoing throughout the year
2. **Preparing financial statements** — cash flow, income statement, balance sheet (usually monthly)
3. **Using financial ratios** to monitor financial performance

---

## 8. Financial Risks

Risks that a business is unable to pay its debts as they fall due:

- Theft of goods
- Not meeting sales targets
- Non-payment by accounts receivable
- Interest rate rises

> **Key relationship:** There is a **direct relationship** between borrowing and risk — higher debt (gearing) increases fixed interest costs, reducing financial flexibility and raising insolvency risk.

## 9. HSC Exam Response Structures

### Explain Scaffold (for "Explain" questions)
- **A. Define** — What is it?
- **B. Cause/Relationship** — How does the issue impact the business?
- **C. Effects/Implications** — What does this lead to?
- **D. Link** — What does this mean for the business?

### Key Exam Tips
- Always link theory to a **case study**
- Use **specific data** — ratios, figures, percentages
- Show **interdependence** between functions
- Know your **verbs** — outline (2 marks), explain (3–4 marks), discuss/evaluate (7–8 marks)
