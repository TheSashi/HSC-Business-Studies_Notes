# Finance - Syllabus Summary

## 1. Role of Financial Management

- Strategic role: provide the financial framework for business activities and strategic goals
- Objectives:
  - Short term: liquidity, profitability and efficiency
  - Long term: growth and solvency
- Long-term goal: increase owners' wealth through sustainable profitability, growth and efficiency
- Interdependence:
  - Operations needs budgets and funding; cost control and output affect profit
  - Marketing uses finance budgets and generates sales revenue
  - HR wages, training and rewards require budgeting and productivity review

## 2. Influences on Financial Management

- Internal finance: owner's equity and retained profits
- External debt finance:
  - Short term: overdraft, commercial bill and factoring
  - Long term: mortgage, debenture, unsecured notes/bonds and leasing
- External equity finance:
  - Private equity and public share issues
  - IPOs, rights issues, placements and share purchase plans
- Financial grants: government funding subject to eligibility criteria
- Financial institutions: banks, investment banks, finance/insurance companies, superannuation funds, unit trusts and ASX
- Primary market: new securities; proceeds go to the issuing business
- Secondary market: existing securities; proceeds go to the selling investor
- Government and regulation:
  - RBA monetary policy and interest rates affect borrowing costs
  - ASIC, Corporations Act compliance, disclosure and investor protection
  - Company tax affects distributable and reinvested profit
  - Interest deductibility can make debt more attractive than equity
- Global influences: international interest rates, availability of funds and global economic outlook
- Finance selection considerations: cost, term, risk, control, gearing and purpose of funds

## 3. Processes of Financial Management

- Planning and implementation:
  - Assess project viability and future funding needs
  - Set goals and projections for liquidity and performance
  - Use operating, project and financial budgets
  - Maintain accurate records, controls and independent audits
- Financial risk: sales shortfalls, unpaid receivables, theft and interest-rate rises
- Financial controls: budget variance monitoring, financial statements and ratio analysis
- Financing choices: debt vs equity; match the term of finance to the use of funds
- Cash flow statement:
  - Opening cash balance + receipts − payments = closing cash balance
  - Actual cash movements; distinguish from a forecast cash-flow budget
- Income statement:
  - Net sales = sales − sales returns
  - COGS = opening stock + purchases − closing stock
  - Gross profit = net sales − COGS
  - Net profit = gross profit − other expenses
  - COGS is deducted once to calculate gross profit; do not subtract it again from gross profit
- Balance sheet: assets = liabilities + owners' equity; current and non-current categories
- Financial ratios:
  - Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities; liquidity benchmark 2:1
  - Gearing = total liabilities ÷ owners' equity; course benchmark 0.5:1 to 1:1
  - Gross profit ratio = gross profit ÷ net sales × 100
  - Net profit ratio = net profit ÷ net sales × 100
  - Return on owners' equity = net profit ÷ owners' equity × 100
  - Expense ratio = total expenses ÷ net sales × 100
  - Accounts receivable turnover: sales ÷ accounts receivable, then 365 ÷ result; benchmark 30 days
- Ratio analysis: interpret results, compare across periods and against the industry benchmark; use net sales where returns are provided
- Limitations: normalised earnings, capitalised expenses, asset valuations, timing/cut-off, debt repayments and notes to financial statements
- Ethics: integrity, objectivity and confidentiality; accurate disclosure, independent audits and true-and-fair reporting
- Unethical practices: window dressing, income smoothing, hidden liabilities, fictitious revenue, misuse of funds and misleading asset valuations

## 4. Financial Management Strategies

- Cash flow management:
  - Spread payments to avoid cash shortfalls
  - Offer early-payment discounts and enforce credit terms
  - Factor receivables; increase inflows and control discretionary outflows
  - Shorten the cash-flow cycle
- Working capital management:
  - Working capital = current assets − current liabilities
  - Control cash, receivables and inventory
  - Manage payables, loans and overdrafts
  - Leasing and sale-and-lease-back to release cash
- Profitability management:
  - Fixed and variable cost controls; cost centres; expense minimisation
  - Revenue growth through marketing objectives and the marketing mix
  - Profit = total revenue − total costs
- Break-even analysis:
  - Contribution per unit = selling price − variable cost per unit
  - Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit
  - Break-even dollars = break-even units × selling price
  - Margin of safety = actual/expected sales − break-even sales
  - Target-profit units = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit
- Global financial management:
  - Manage exchange-rate and interest-rate exposure
  - Select international payment methods: payment in advance, letter of credit, bill of exchange and clean payment
  - Hedge risks naturally or through derivatives
  - Forward exchange contracts lock a rate; options provide a choice; swaps enable payment in another currency
