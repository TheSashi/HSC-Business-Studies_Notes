# 💰 Finance 1 · Role of Financial Management

> **Finance** · **① Role** ← you are here · [[Finance_2_Influences|② Influences]] · [[Finance_3_Processes|③ Processes]] · [[Finance_4_Strategies|④ Strategies]] · [[Finance_FULL|📕 Full note]]

> **Scope:** the full HSC Finance module is examinable. This file covers the **Role of financial management** dot points only.

**Definition**: Financial management deals with the **analysis, interpretation and evaluation** of all financial records. It is the financial manager's responsibility to source finance that enables the business to achieve its strategic goals.

---

## 1. Strategic Role of Financial Management

- The process of **setting objectives** throughout the business and deciding **what resources are needed** to achieve them
- Developing a strategic plan as part of financial management ensures the business **survives and grows** in a competitive world
- Financial management **provides a framework for all other business activities** — it does not follow the lead of other managers

> ⚠️ **Exam trap**: A common multiple-choice question asks what the strategic role of finance is. The answer is *"provides a framework for all other business activities"* — **not** "sets short-term goals" (that's tactical/operational).

---

## 2. Objectives of Financial Management

Five objectives, split by time horizon.

| Horizon | Objective | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Short term** (within 12 months) | **Liquidity** | The ability to pay back **current liabilities** with **current assets** |
| **Short term** | **Profitability** | The ability of the business to **maximise profits** |
| **Short term** | **Efficiency** | The ability to manage **costs** and the **collection of money** from customers |
| **Long term** (2–5 years) | **Growth** | The ability of the business to grow and expand |
| **Long term** | **Solvency** | How the business is financed (debt vs equity) — the ability to meet **long-term** financial commitments |

**Long-term goal of the business** is to increase the **wealth of the owners**. Long-term goals depend on **profitability in the short term** resulting from increased operating efficiency.

> ⭐ **Each objective has its own strategy family** — that mapping is in [[Finance_4_Strategies|④ Strategies]] and is the spine of most extended-response questions.

## 3. Interdependence with Other Key Business Functions

**Interdependence** = the mutual reliance between finance and the other key business functions.

| Function | Relationship with Finance |
|---|---|
| **Operations** | Finance provides funding and budgets so products can be made profitably. Operations produces quality products with minimal expense, generating higher revenue margins that flow back to finance |
| **Marketing** | Finance sets the marketing budget. Marketing generates the sales revenue that finance then allocates, monitors and reinvests |
| **Human Resources** | Wages and training are major budget items. Finance sets salary budgets; HR must justify expenditure through productivity gains. Commission and incentive schemes are a **joint HR–finance** decision |

> **Model "Explain" answer (scaffold from class)**
> **Define** — Financial management is interdependent with operations because of the relationship between business revenue and expenditure.
> **Relationship** — Finance provides funding and budgets so the product can be produced profitably, while operations produces quality products with minimal expense to generate a higher revenue margin.
> **Implications** — Both functions supply each other with information and resources that drive growth and profit maximisation.
> **Link** — Hence the business grows in the marketplace and increases revenue margins, leading to increased profitability.

## ⚠️ Confusing pairs in this section

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| **Liquidity** vs **Solvency** | Liquidity = *short*-term, can we pay bills due **now** (current ratio). Solvency = *long*-term, can we meet commitments over **years** (gearing ratio). Both are about paying debts — the difference is purely the **time frame** |
| **Profitability** vs **Efficiency** | Profitability = how much **profit** we make. Efficiency = how well we **manage costs and collect money** |
| **Strategic** vs **tactical** role | Strategic = *provides the framework for all other business activities*. Tactical = short-term goal setting |
| **Profit** vs **cash flow** | A profitable business can still fail from poor liquidity. Profit is accounting; cash flow is money actually in the account |
