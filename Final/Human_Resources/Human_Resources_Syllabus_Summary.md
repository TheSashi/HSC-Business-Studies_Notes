# Human Resources - Syllabus Summary

## 1. Role of Human Resource Management

- Align workforce capability and culture with strategic direction
- Attract, develop and retain employees; manage employee relations and legal compliance
- Treat employees as an asset to be developed
- **Interdependence:**
  - Operations identifies staffing and skill requirements; HR recruits and trains
  - Marketing depends on skilled and customer-facing employees
  - Finance budgets wages and training; HR supports expenditure with productivity outcomes
- **Outsourced HR functions:** recruitment, payroll, training and specialist advice
- **Contractors:** domestic or global; flexibility and expertise against cost, control, communication and legal-jurisdiction risks

## 2. Influences on Human Resources

- **Stakeholders:** employers, employees, employer associations, unions, government organisations and society
- **Stakeholder tensions:** productivity, flexibility and cost vs fair pay, safety, security and development
- **Legal influences:**
  - **Fair Work Act 2009:** workplace relations, minimum wages, NES, unfair dismissal and bargaining
  - **Awards:** industry/occupation minimums; enterprise agreements: workplace-negotiated and subject to BOOT
  - Common-law employment contracts and employer/employee obligations
  - **Work Health and Safety legislation:** duty of care, risk control, training and consultation
  - Anti-discrimination laws and Equal Employment Opportunity (merit-based decisions and removal of barriers)
  - Workers compensation, termination protections and severance entitlements
- **Economic influences:** booms, downturns, unemployment, skills shortages, recruitment difficulty and wage pressure
- **Technological influences:** automation, HRIS, AI recruitment, remote work, changing skill needs and role displacement
- **Social influences:** ageing workforce, women's participation, cultural diversity and work-life balance expectations
- **Ethics and CSR:** fair pay, diversity, inclusion, bullying/harassment prevention, whistleblower protection and safe supply chains

## 3. Human Resource Processes

- **HR cycle:** acquisition → development → maintenance → separation
- **Acquisition:**
  - Identify staffing and skill needs
  - Prepare job descriptions and job specifications
  - Recruit internally or externally
  - Select through interviews, application forms, referee checks and tests
  - Induct new employees into policies, safety, facilities, colleagues and entitlements
- **Development:**
  - **On-the-job training:** coaching, mentoring, rotation, apprenticeships and shadowing
  - **Off-the-job training:** TAFE, university, workshops and conferences
  - **Performance appraisals:** strengths, skill gaps, goals and development needs
- **Maintenance:**
  - Retain and motivate staff through pay, benefits, work conditions and development
  - Participation, teamwork, flexible work, family-friendly programs and diversity
  - Address bullying, harassment, low morale and avoidable turnover
- **Separation:**
  - **Voluntary:** resignation, retirement and voluntary redundancy
  - **Involuntary:** redundancy, dismissal and summary dismissal
  - Manage notice, severance, accrued leave, consultation, fairness and documentation
  - Consider survivor morale, corporate knowledge and unfair-dismissal risk

## 4. Human Resource Strategies

- **Leadership styles:** authoritarian, participative/democratic, delegative/laissez-faire, pacesetting, affiliative, coaching, coercive and transformational
- **Job design:**
  - Job rotation, enlargement and enrichment
  - Semi-autonomous work groups and team structures
  - Flexible work structures
  - Balance efficiency, motivation, autonomy, variety and job satisfaction
- **Recruitment:** right person for the right role; internal/external methods; general and role-specific skills
- **Training and development:** address skills shortages, safety, performance, innovation and retention
- **Performance management:**
  - Set expectations and objectives, check in, give feedback and assess performance
  - **Teacher's distinction:** administrative performance management records/audits skills and informs HR planning; developmental management plans and delivers training
- **Rewards:**
  - Monetary and non-monetary; individual and group
  - Performance-related, job-related and group incentives
  - Align rewards with business strategy, objectives, viability, awards/agreements and employee motivation
- **Global HR:**
  - Consider labour cost and supply, skills, quality, infrastructure, regulations, political stability, language and culture
  - Balance access to skills and lower costs against domestic job losses and coordination risks
- **Workplace disputes:**
  - Maintain a grievance procedure
  - **Resolution sequence:** negotiation → mediation → conciliation → arbitration
  - Distinguish overt action (strikes, bans, lockouts) from covert action (absenteeism, turnover, sabotage, underperformance)

## 5. Effectiveness of Human Resource Management

- Corporate culture
- Benchmarking key variables
- Changes in staff turnover
- Absenteeism
- Accidents
- Levels of disputation
- Worker satisfaction
- **Match strategies to evidence:**
  - **Leadership:** culture, benchmarks, turnover, absenteeism and satisfaction
  - **Job design:** turnover, absenteeism, accidents and satisfaction
  - **Recruitment:** culture, benchmarks, turnover and absenteeism
  - **Training and development:** turnover, absenteeism, accidents, culture and satisfaction
  - **Performance management and rewards:** turnover, absenteeism, accidents and satisfaction
  - **Workplace disputes:** turnover, absenteeism and satisfaction
