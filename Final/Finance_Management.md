# Finance Management — HSC Final Notes

> **Module 5: Finance** | Complete revision document for the HSC Business Studies exam.

## 1. The Role of Finance

Finance management involves **planning, sourcing, and controlling** the financial resources of a business to achieve its goals.

**Key functions:**
- Determining **capital requirements** (short-term and long-term)
- Selecting appropriate **sources of finance**
- Managing **working capital** (day-to-day liquidity)
- Controlling financial performance via **budgets and ratios**

---

## 2. Sources of Finance

### Internal vs External Sources

| Internal Sources | External Sources — Debt | External Sources — Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Retained profits | Bank overdraft / loans | Issuing shares (IPO/secondary) |
| Sale of assets | Debentures | Venture capital |
| Tighter credit control (debtors ↓) | Mortgages / hire purchase | Crowd funding |
| Owner's funds (sole traders) | Leasing | Private equity |

**Exam tip:** Always distinguish between **debt** (must be repaid, interest is tax-deductible) and **equity** (no repayment obligation, dilutes ownership).

### Short-Term vs Long-Term Finance

| Short-Term (≤ 1 year) | Long-Term (> 1 year) |
|---|---|
| Overdraft, trade credit, factoring debtors | Loans, mortgages, debentures, shares, leasing |

## 3. Financial Ratios — All Formulas & What They Mean

### 3.1 Profitability Ratios (Ability to Generate Profit)

| Ratio | Formula | Example | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross Profit Margin** | (Gross Profit ÷ Sales Revenue) × 100 | GP = $200k, Sales = $500k → **40%** | Efficiency of production; higher = better cost control |
| **Net Profit Margin** | (Net Profit ÷ Sales Revenue) × 100 | NP = $80k, Sales = $500k → **16%** | Overall profitability after ALL expenses |
| **Return on Assets (ROA)** | (Net Profit ÷ Total Assets) × 100 | NP = $80k, Assets = $400k → **20%** | How effectively assets generate profit |
| **Return on Equity (ROE)** | (Net Profit ÷ Owner's Equity) × 100 | NP = $80k, Equity = $250k → **32%** | Return earned for shareholders/owners |

### 3.2 Liquidity Ratios (Short-Term Solvency)

| Ratio | Formula | Example | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Current Ratio** | Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities | CA = $150k, CL = $100k → **1.5:1** | Can business meet short-term debts? Ideal ≈ 1.5–2:1 |
| **Quick Ratio (Acid Test)** | (Current Assets − Inventory) ÷ Current Liabilities | CA = $150k, Inv = $50k, CL = $100k → **1:1** | Stricter test — excludes slow-selling inventory. Ideal ≥ 1:1 |

### 3.3 Efficiency Ratios (How Well Resources Are Used)

| Ratio | Formula | Example | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inventory Turnover** | Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Inventory | COGS = $300k, Avg Inv = $50k → **6 times** | How quickly stock sells; higher = better (usually) |
| **Debtor Turnover (Days)** | (Accounts Receivable ÷ Sales Revenue) × 365 | Debtors = $40k, Sales = $500k → **29.2 days** | Average time to collect payment; lower = better |
| **Creditor Turnover (Days)** | (Accounts Payable ÷ Purchases) × 365 | Creditors = $30k, Purchases = $250k → **43.8 days** | Average time to pay suppliers; higher = better cash flow (but not too high!) |

### 3.4 Gearing / Leverage Ratios (Long-Term Solvency)

| Ratio | Formula | Example | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Debt-to-Equity Ratio** | Total Liabilities ÷ Owner's Equity | Debt = $200k, Equity = $250k → **0.8:1** | Financial risk; higher = more debt reliance |
| **Gearing Ratio** | (Non-Current Liabilities ÷ Total Assets) × 100 | NCL = $150k, Assets = $400k → **37.5%** | Proportion of long-term debt funding assets |

### 3.5 Ratio Quick-Reference (Cheat Sheet)

```
Profitability:   GP%  NP%  ROA  ROE
Liquidity:       Current Ratio   Quick Ratio
Efficiency:      Inventory Turn   Debtor Days   Creditor Days
Gearing:         Debt-to-Equity   Gearing %
```

> **Exam trap:** A high current ratio isn't always good — it could mean excess cash not being invested. Always **interpret** the ratio, don't just calculate it.

## 4. Cash Flow Management

### 4.1 Cash Flow Statement vs Budget

| Cash Flow Statement | Cash Flow Budget |
|---|---|
| **Record** of past cash inflows & outflows | **Forecast** of future expected cash flows |
| Historical / actual | Planning tool / projected |
| Shows where cash went | Identifies potential shortfalls in advance |

### 4.2 Strategies to Improve Cash Flow

**Increase Cash Inflows:**
- Offer **trade discounts** for early payment (e.g., 2/10 net 30)
- **Tighten credit terms** — reduce debtor days
- **Factor (sell) debtors** to a factoring company for immediate cash
- **Increase sales revenue** (marketing, pricing strategies)
- **Sell unused assets** (non-current assets)
- **Lease assets** instead of purchasing outright

**Decrease Cash Outflows:**
- Negotiate **longer payment terms** with suppliers (increase creditor days)
- Reduce **inventory levels** (just-in-time)
- Cut **discretionary expenses** (travel, entertainment)
- **Lease instead of buy** — spreads payments over time
- Refinance loans at **lower interest rates**
- Delay non-essential **capital expenditure**

### 4.3 Cash Flow Cycle

```
Cash → Inventory → Production → Sales → Debtors → Cash
                                                      ↑
                                                  (cycle repeats)
```

> The **longer** the cash flow cycle, the more working capital is needed. Businesses aim to **shorten** the cycle.

## 5. Working Capital Management

**Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities**

### 5.1 Components of Working Capital

| Component | What It Is | Management Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash** | Most liquid asset | Maintain adequate levels; not too much (idle), not too little |
| **Inventory** | Raw materials, WIP, finished goods | Balance between too much (storage costs, obsolescence) and too little (stockouts, lost sales) |
| **Debtors** | Money owed BY customers | Minimise collection time; offer early payment discounts |
| **Creditors** | Money owed TO suppliers | Maximise payment time without damaging relationships or credit terms |

### 5.2 Working Capital Cycle (Operating Cycle)

```
Inventory Holding Period + Debtor Collection Period − Creditor Payment Period
= Working Capital Cycle (in days)
```

**Example:**
- Inventory holding: 60 days
- Debtor collection: 30 days
- Creditor payment: 45 days
- **Working Capital Cycle = 60 + 30 − 45 = 45 days**

> A **shorter** cycle means less cash is tied up → better liquidity.

### 5.3 Strategies to Manage Working Capital

| Strategy | Effect |
|---|---|
| Just-in-Time (JIT) inventory | Reduces inventory holding → frees cash |
| Offering early payment discounts | Reduces debtor days → faster cash inflow |
| Negotiating longer supplier terms | Extends creditor days → retains cash longer |
| Factoring debtors | Immediate cash from debtors (at a cost) |
| Cash pooling | Consolidating cash across accounts for efficiency |
| Rolling budgets | Continuously updated budgets for better forecasting |

## 6. Profitability Strategies

### 6.1 Cost Control Strategies (Reduce Expenses)

| Strategy | How It Works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Economies of scale | Increase output → lower average cost | Bulk purchasing discounts |
| Lean production | Eliminate waste in all processes | Toyota Production System |
| Outsource non-core functions | Cheaper external providers | Outsourcing payroll or IT |
| Negotiate supplier discounts | Lower input costs | Long-term contracts |
| Reduce overheads | Cut fixed costs | Move to smaller premises, reduce energy use |
| Technology/automation | Replace labour with machines | Self-checkout in retail |
| Just-in-Time (JIT) | Reduce inventory holding costs | Dell's build-to-order model |

### 6.2 Revenue Control Strategies (Increase Income)

| Strategy | How It Works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product differentiation | Charge premium prices for unique products | Apple's brand positioning |
| Market development | Sell existing products to new markets | Exporting to Asia |
| Product development | New products for existing markets | New menu items in a café |
| Diversification | New products in new markets | Virgin expanding into banking |
| Price skimming | High initial price, lower over time | New tech products |
| Penetration pricing | Low price to gain market share quickly | Aldi entering the Australian market |
| Improve marketing mix | Better promotion, placement, product, price | Social media campaigns |

### 6.3 Profit Formula

```
Profit = Total Revenue − Total Costs
Profit = (Price × Quantity Sold) − (Fixed Costs + Variable Costs)
```

> To improve profit: **increase revenue** (price or quantity) OR **decrease costs** (fixed or variable).

## 7. Break-Even Analysis

### 7.1 Key Formulas

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Total Revenue** | Price per unit × Quantity sold |
| **Total Costs** | Fixed Costs + (Variable Cost per unit × Quantity) |
| **Break-Even Point (units)** | Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price per unit − Variable Cost per unit) |
| **Break-Even Point ($)** | Break-even units × Selling Price per unit |
| **Contribution per unit** | Selling Price per unit − Variable Cost per unit |
| **Margin of Safety** | Actual (or expected) sales − Break-even sales |
| **Target Profit (units)** | (Fixed Costs + Target Profit) ÷ Contribution per unit |

### 7.2 Worked Example

> A café has **fixed costs of $60,000/year**. Each coffee sells for **$5** with **variable cost of $2**.

| Calculation | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution per unit | $5 − $2 | **$3** |
| Break-even (units) | $60,000 ÷ $3 | **20,000 coffees** |
| Break-even ($) | 20,000 × $5 | **$100,000** |
| Margin of safety (if selling 25,000) | 25,000 − 20,000 | **5,000 coffees** |
| Target profit of $15,000 (units) | ($60,000 + $15,000) ÷ $3 | **25,000 coffees** |

### 7.3 Break-Even Chart Interpretation

```
Revenue ($)
  |        ╱ Total Revenue
  |       ╱
  |      ╱
  |     ╱  ← Break-Even Point
  |    ╱╱ Total Costs
  |   ╱╱
  |  ╱╱  ← Fixed Costs line (horizontal)
  | ╱╱
  |╱╱
  +————————————————→ Quantity
     ↑
  Break-Even
  Point
```

**Key zones:**
- **Below break-even:** Loss zone (costs > revenue)
- **At break-even:** No profit, no loss
- **Above break-even:** Profit zone (revenue > costs)

### 7.4 Effects of Changes on Break-Even

| Change | Effect on Break-Even Point |
|---|---|
| ↑ Selling price | Decreases (fewer units needed) |
| ↑ Variable costs | Increases (more units needed) |
| ↑ Fixed costs | Increases (more units needed) |
| ↓ Selling price | Increases |
| ↓ Variable costs | Decreases |
| ↓ Fixed costs | Decreases |

> **Exam application:** If a business increases marketing spend (fixed cost ↑), BEP increases. If they switch to cheaper suppliers (variable cost ↓), BEP decreases.

## 8. Budgeting

### 8.1 Purpose of Budgets
- **Planning** — forces management to set financial targets
- **Coordination** — aligns departments toward common goals
- **Control** — compare actual vs budgeted performance (variance analysis)
- **Communication** — financial expectations shared across organisation

### 8.2 Variance Analysis

| Variance Type | Favourable (F) | Adverse (A) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue variance | Actual revenue > Budget | Actual revenue < Budget |
| Cost variance | Actual costs < Budget | Actual costs > Budget |
| Profit variance | Actual profit > Budget | Actual profit < Budget |

**Variance = Actual − Budget**

> A **favourable** variance on costs means spending was **below** budget.
> An **adverse** variance on revenue means sales were **below** budget.

## 9. HSC Exam Response Structures for Finance Questions

### 9.1 Formula for 2-Mark "Identify" Questions

```
Identify the ratio + State the formula (or what it measures)
```
**Example:** "Identify one profitability ratio."
> The net profit margin ratio measures profitability by comparing net profit to sales revenue. Formula: (Net Profit ÷ Sales Revenue) × 100.

### 9.2 Formula for 3-Mark "Explain" Questions

```
State the concept + Explain how it works + Provide a business example
```
**Example:** "Explain how a business could improve its cash flow."
> One strategy to improve cash flow is to tighten credit terms (CONCEPT). This involves reducing the number of days customers have to pay, such as changing from 60 days to 30 days (EXPLANATION). For example, a retail clothing store could offer a 5% discount for payment within 7 days to encourage faster debtor collection (EXAMPLE).

### 9.3 Formula for 4-Mark "Discuss" / "Analyse" Questions

```
State the strategy/technique + Explain the positive impact on the business + Explain a potential negative/limitation + Link back to the business context
```

### 9.4 Formula for 8-Mark "Evaluate" Questions

```
Introduction: Define the key term, state the judgement/position
Body Paragraph 1: Point + Evidence/Example + Link to question
Body Paragraph 2: Point + Evidence/Example + Link to question
Body Paragraph 3: Counterargument OR another factor + Link
Conclusion: Restate judgement with a qualifying statement (e.g., "however, the effectiveness depends on...")
```

### 9.5 Common Finance Question Verbs & What They Demand

| Verb | What to Do | Marks Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Identify** | Name/state a concept | 1–2 marks |
| **Describe** | Give features/characteristics | 2–3 marks |
| **Explain** | How/why — show understanding with example | 3–4 marks |
| **Analyse** | Break down + examine relationships | 4–5 marks |
| **Discuss** | Present multiple perspectives | 5–6 marks |
| **Evaluate** | Make a judgement with supporting arguments | 7–8 marks |
| **Justify** | Support a position with evidence | 6–8 marks |

### 9.6 Key Finance Keywords to Use in Responses

| Category | Keywords |
|---|---|
| Profitability | Gross/net profit margin, ROA, ROE, cost control, revenue growth |
| Liquidity | Current ratio, quick ratio, cash flow, working capital |
| Efficiency | Inventory turnover, debtor/creditor days, operating cycle |
| Gearing | Debt-to-equity, financial risk, leverage, solvency |
| Cash flow | Inflows, outflows, cash flow cycle, liquidity, overdraft |
| Break-even | Contribution, fixed/variable costs, margin of safety, BEP |

### 9.7 Finance Essay Template (8 marks)

```
[SENTENCE 1 — Define key term from question]
[SENTENCE 2 — State your overall position/thesis]

BODY 1: First reason the strategy is effective
- Identify strategy
- Explain how it works (use ratios/numbers if possible)
- Link to improving business performance

BODY 2: Second reason OR a counterargument
- Identify another strategy or limitation
- Explain with evidence/example
- Acknowledge the complexity

CONCLUSION:
"Ultimately, [strategy] is effective in [context] because... 
However, its success depends on [qualifying factor]. 
Therefore, a balanced approach combining [strategies] would 
be most beneficial for the business."
```

## 10. McDonald's Ratio Analysis — Model Answers (2022 vs 2023)

### Liquidity — Current Ratio
| Year | Current Ratio |
|------|--------------|
| 2022 | 1.43:1 |
| 2023 | 1.16:1 |

**Model comparison:** Current ratio worsened from 1.43:1 to 1.16:1. Measures ability to pay current liabilities with current assets. Similarity: inventory ~$52M both years. Difference: huge increase in short-term borrowing ($2M), explaining the worsened ratio.

### Profitability — Net Profit Ratio
| Year | NP Ratio | Yum Brands |
|------|----------|-----------|
| 2022 | 27% | 19% |
| 2023 | 33% | 23% |

**Model comparison:** NP ratio improved from 27% to 33%. Similarity: operating costs steady ($13,811M → $13,847M). Difference: revenue increased ($23,182M → $25,493M), explaining improvement.

### Efficiency — Accounts Receivable
| Year | Days |
|------|------|
| 2022 | 31.74 days |
| 2023 | 32.88 days |

### Extended Response Scaffold (Assess financial performance)
1. **Intro:** Brief intro about business, state ratios being analysed
2. **Current Ratio:** Cause → How → Effect → Judgement (link to liquidity/solvency)
3. **Gearing Ratio:** Same structure (link to solvency)
4. **Net Profit Ratio:** Same structure (link to profitability)

### Financial Strategies Scenario — PEEL Example
**Scenario:** XYZ Retail with high inventory, low cash, high receivables.

**JIT Strategy PEEL:**
- **P:** JIT inventory management can control high inventory levels
- **E:** High inventory increases storage costs and risk of obsolescence
- **E:** JIT reduces storage costs, minimises expenses, improves profitability. Risk: may not have enough stock to meet demand
- **L:** By reducing inventory costs, XYZ Retail maximises profit for reinvestment and business growth

**Key insight:** Top students integrate strategies from other topics (operations) and link them back to finance impact.

## 11. Common Exam Pitfalls

1. **Don't just calculate — interpret.** A ratio means nothing without a comment on whether it's good/bad and why.
2. **Compare ratios over time** or to industry benchmarks when possible.
3. **Use specific numbers** from stimulus material — don't be vague.
4. **Link everything back** to the business in the question.
5. **Know the difference** between cash flow and profit — a profitable business can still have poor cash flow.
6. **Don't confuse** current ratio with quick ratio — quick ratio excludes inventory.
7. **Break-even:** Always check if the question asks for units or dollars.
8. **Variance:** Favourable on costs ≠ Favourable on revenue. Context matters.
