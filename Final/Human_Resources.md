# Human Resources Management — HSC Final Notes

> **Module 6: Human Resources** | Complete revision document for the HSC Business Studies exam.

## 1. The Role of HRM

Human Resource Management is the **managing of people** to achieve business objectives. It is one of the four key business functions alongside operations, marketing, and finance.

**HRM as a strategic function:**
- Attracts, develops, and retains talented employees
- Aligns workforce capabilities with business goals
- Creates positive workplace culture
- Manages employee relations and compliance

**Link to business performance:**
- Skilled, motivated employees → higher productivity → better operations
- Good customer service training → improved marketing effectiveness
- Efficient staffing → cost control → better financial performance

---

## 2. Relationship Between HRM and Other Functions

| Function | Relationship with HRM |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Operations** | HR recruits and trains production staff. Operations determines skill needs and staffing levels. Productivity depends on workforce quality. |
| **Marketing** | HR recruits marketing team. Marketing effectiveness depends on staff creativity and skills. Customer-facing staff need service training. |
| **Finance** | HR costs (wages, training) are major budget items. Finance sets salary budgets. HR must justify expenditure through productivity gains. |

> **Exam tip:** Always show how HR connects to the other three functions — this demonstrates **interdependence**, which is a key exam concept.

## 4. Legal Framework for Employment

### Key Legislation

| Law | Purpose |
|-----|---------|
| **Fair Work Act 2009** | National workplace relations system — minimum wages, unfair dismissal, enterprise bargaining |
| **WHS Act 2011** | Work health and safety — employer duty of care, hazard identification, risk management |
| **Anti-Discrimination Act** | Prohibits discrimination based on gender, race, age, disability, sexual orientation |
| **Modern Awards** | Industry-specific minimum pay and conditions |
| **National Employment Standards (NES)** | 11 minimum entitlements (annual leave, sick leave, hours, notice of termination) |

### Employment Types

| Type | Description | Key Feature |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Full-time** | Regular hours (38 hrs/week) | Full entitlements |
| **Part-time** | Regular hours but fewer than full-time | Pro-rata entitlements |
| **Casual** | No guaranteed hours | Higher hourly rate (loading), no leave |
| **Contract** | Fixed-term or project-based | Specific terms in contract |

## 5. HRM Strategies

### 5.1 Recruitment and Selection

**Steps in the recruitment process:**

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Job analysis** | Identify duties, responsibilities, skills needed |
| **2. Job description** | Written document outlining the role |
| **3. Person specification** | Ideal candidate profile |
| **4. Job advertisement** | Post via Seek, LinkedIn, company website, social media |
| **5. Application review** | Screen resumes against selection criteria |
| **6. Selection methods** | Interviews, psychometric testing, skills tests, assessment centres, reference checks |
| **7. Appointment** | Offer letter, contract, negotiate terms |

**Internal vs External Recruitment:**

| | Internal | External |
|--|----------|----------|
| **Pros** | Cheaper, faster, motivates staff, known performance | Fresh ideas, larger pool, new perspectives |
| **Cons** | Limited pool, creates vacancy, inbreeding | Expensive, unknown performance, longer adjustment |

### 5.2 Training and Development

| Type | Description | Pros | Cons |
|------|-------------|------|------|
| **Induction** | Introduction to organisation, policies, safety (first week) | Reduces anxiety, speeds productivity | Time away from productive work |
| **On-the-job** | Learning while working (mentoring, coaching, job rotation, shadowing) | Practical, relevant, immediate application | Can disrupt production |
| **Off-the-job** | External courses, workshops, TAFE/university | Expert instruction, dedicated environment | Expensive, time away from work |
| **Development** | Long-term career growth (leadership programs, succession planning) | Retains talent, prepares for future roles | Costly, no guarantee of ROI |

### 5.3 Performance Management

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **KPIs** | Measurable targets (sales, production output, customer satisfaction) |
| **Performance appraisals** | Formal review meetings — set goals, review achievements, identify development needs |
| **360-degree feedback** | Feedback from supervisor, peers, subordinates, self-assessment |
| **Balanced scorecard** | Measures 4 perspectives: financial, customer, internal processes, learning & growth |

### 5.4 Dismissal and Redundancy

**Dismissal types:** Termination (poor performance/misconduct), Constructive dismissal (employee resigns due to hostile actions), Unfair dismissal (harsh/unjust — claimable through Fair Work Commission)

**Redundancy types:** Voluntary (employee chooses with package), Involuntary (position no longer needed — restructuring/technology)

**Legal requirements:** Minimum notice periods, redundancy pay (based on years of service), consultation obligations

### 5.5 Employee Relations

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Enterprise agreements** | Negotiated between employer and employees/union — pay and conditions above minimum |
| **Awards** | Industry-specific minimum pay and conditions set by Fair Work Commission |
| **Unions** | Organisations representing workers' interests |
| **Dispute resolution** | Mediation, Fair Work Commission, arbitration |

**Industrial action:** Strike (employees stop work), Lockout (employer prevents employees from working) — protected vs unprotected under Fair Work Act

### 5.6 Workplace Diversity

**Benefits:** Wider talent pool, better decision-making, improved creativity, better understanding of diverse customers, enhanced employer reputation

**Challenges:** Communication barriers, potential for discrimination/bias, different work styles, training costs, resistance to change

### 5.7 Flexible Work Practices

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Part-time** | Fewer hours than full-time |
| **Job sharing** | Two employees share one full-time role |
| **Telecommuting** | Working from home or off-site |
| **Flextime** | Flexible start/finish times (core hours required) |
| **Compressed work week** | Full hours in fewer days (e.g. 4 × 10hr days) |

**Benefits:** Improved work-life balance → higher morale and retention; access to wider talent pool; reduced absenteeism; lower office costs

**Challenges:** Coordination difficulties; reduced team cohesion; harder to monitor performance; not suitable for all roles

## 6. Motivation Theories

### Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

```
        Self-Actualisation
       (reach full potential)
      ────────────────────
       Esteem Needs
     (recognition, status)
    ────────────────────
     Social/Belonging Needs
      (teamwork, friendship)
     ────────────────────
      Safety Needs
   (job security, safe workplace)
  ────────────────────
   Physiological Needs
  (salary, basic conditions)
```

**Application:** Ensure lower-level needs are met before expecting motivation from higher-level rewards. Pay and safety first, then belonging, then recognition, then growth opportunities.

### Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory

| Factor | Description | Examples |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Hygiene factors** | Cause dissatisfaction if absent, don't motivate when present | Salary, working conditions, job security, company policy |
| **Motivators** | Actually motivate and satisfy employees | Achievement, recognition, responsibility, advancement, personal growth |

**Application:** Fix hygiene factors to prevent dissatisfaction, then use motivators to engage employees.

### McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y

| Theory X (authoritarian) | Theory Y (participative) |
|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Workers are lazy and avoid work | Workers are self-motivated and enjoy work |
| Need close supervision and control | Need autonomy and responsibility |
| Motivated by punishment/rewards | Motivated by intrinsic factors |
| Management style: command and control | Management style: empowerment and trust |

## 7. Organisational Structures

| Structure | Description | Pros | Cons |
|-----------|-------------|------|------|
| **Flat** | Few management layers, wide span of control | Faster communication, empowerment | Manager overload, unclear reporting |
| **Tall** | Many management layers, narrow span of control | Clear career path, close supervision | Slow communication, bureaucracy |
| **Centralised** | Decision-making at top | Consistency, control | Slow response, disempowerment |
| **Decentralised** | Decision-making pushed to lower levels | Faster response, empowerment | Inconsistency, coordination challenges |
| **Functional** | Grouped by function (marketing, finance, etc.) | Specialisation, efficiency | Silos, poor cross-functional communication |
| **Matrix** | Dual reporting (function + project) | Flexible, cross-functional collaboration | Conflicting priorities, complex reporting |

## 8. HSC Exam Response Structures for HR

### HR Strategy Answer Structure (4-6 marks)
1. **Define** the strategy — "Recruitment is the process of attracting and selecting suitable candidates..."
2. **Explain** how it works — describe the steps or methods
3. **Apply** to the business — link to specific case study
4. **Evaluate** effectiveness — discuss pros/cons, impact on business performance

### Recommendation Structure (8 marks)
1. **Recommend** — "It is recommended that [business] implement [strategy]"
2. **Define** — explain what the strategy involves
3. **Reason 1** — how it addresses the identified problem
4. **Reason 2** — additional benefit
5. **Link** — restate recommendation with justification

### Common HR Exam Verbs

| Verb | What to Do | Marks |
|------|-----------|-------|
| **Identify** | Name/state a concept | 1-2 |
| **Describe** | Give features/characteristics | 2-3 |
| **Explain** | How/why with example | 3-4 |
| **Analyse** | Break down + examine relationships | 4-5 |
| **Discuss** | Present multiple perspectives | 5-6 |
| **Evaluate** | Make judgement with supporting arguments | 7-8 |

### Key HR Keywords to Use

| Category | Keywords |
|----------|----------|
| Recruitment | Internal/external, selection criteria, job analysis, person specification |
| Training | Induction, on-the-job, off-the-job, mentoring, succession planning |
| Performance | KPIs, appraisals, 360-degree feedback, balanced scorecard |
| Motivation | Maslow, Herzberg, McGregor, hygiene factors, motivators, empowerment |
| Structure | Flat/tall, centralised/decentralised, span of control, delegation |
| Legal | Fair Work Act, NES, Modern Awards, WHS, anti-discrimination |
