## 0. Business Profile

```
Apple Inc. — large global technology company designing, manufacturing
and selling consumer electronics, software and digital services worldwide.
Financial year ends late September.
```

**Revenue by segment:** iPhone **50.4%** · Services **26.2%** · Wearables **8.6%** · Mac **8.1%** · iPad **6.7%**

> **The strategic tension to name:** roughly **half of all revenue depends on one product**. Every operations, marketing and finance question about risk can hang off that.

## 1. Finance — FY2024 to FY2025

> Your assessment dataset. Use this window for anything about **recent** performance.

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $391,035m | $416,161m | ↑ |
| Net profit | $93,736m | $112,010m | ↑ |
| **Net profit ratio** | **23.97%** | **26.92%** | ✅ improved |
| Operating expenses | $57,467m | $62,151m | ↑ |
| **Expense ratio** | **14.7%** | **14.93%** | ⚠️ worsened |
| Accounts receivable | $33,410m | $39,777m | ↑ |
| **AR turnover (days)** | **31 days** | **35 days** | ⚠️ worsened *(ideal 30)* |
| Services revenue | $96,169m | $109,158m | ↑ |
| R&D expense | $31,370m | $34,550m | ↑ |
| Selling, general & admin | $26,097m | $27,601m | ↑ |

**The three sentences that carry an answer:**

- *"Apple retained approximately **24 cents per $1 of revenue in 2024, improving to 27 cents in 2025**."*
- *"Growth was driven by **services revenue rising from $96,169m to $109,158m**, which carries higher margins than physical products due to lower production and distribution costs."*
- *"Against **Samsung's net profit ratio of approximately 10%**, Apple's 26.92% demonstrates significantly stronger profitability."*

**The recommendation that scored:** **factoring** to address the deteriorating accounts receivable turnover, converting receivables to immediate cash and transferring collection to a debt collection agency.

> ⚠️ **Correction to your assessment.** You wrote 2024 AR turnover as "approximately 32 days" in one place and "approximately 31 days" in another. The calculation gives **31.2 days**, so **31** is correct.
> `365 ÷ (391,035 ÷ 33,410) = 31.2` · 2025: `365 ÷ (416,161 ÷ 39,777) = 34.9 → 35`
> The two-step method: **Step 1** Sales ÷ AR. **Step 2** 365 ÷ Step 1.

**The story these numbers tell — memorise this arc:**

```
1. Retained profits were ~102% of owners' equity in 2019,
   fell below 9% by 2021, and went into DEFICIT in 2022–2023
   (overdrawn to pay dividends).
2. Apple exploited a decade of record-low interest rates by
   loading up on DEBT — cheaper than equity finance.
3. A share buy-back scheme from 2015 shrank the shareholder
   base to lift return on owners' equity.
4. Result: gearing rose from 2.74:1 (2019) to 5.96:1 (2022).
5. 2022 US Federal Reserve contractionary policy → higher rates
   → higher interest expense → Apple began reducing long-term debt.
6. 2023: total liabilities fell and owners' equity rose
   FOR THE FIRST TIME IN A DECADE.
```

> **Why ROE looks absurd (196% in 2022):** Apple's owners' equity is tiny relative to profit because of buybacks and negative retained earnings. A high ROE here reflects **financial structure**, not just performance. Saying this is a Band 6 observation.

## 3. Operations

- **Transformation:** design in the US, component sourcing globally, assembly outsourced — principally to **Foxconn**
- **Global sourcing** of components; **outsourcing** of assembly to low-cost economies
- **Inventory valuation: FIFO.** Apple **pre-pays suppliers** for some stock to guarantee availability
- **Key inventory challenge:** forecasting demand for each new iPhone model — over- or under-supply causes cost or delay
- **2021 global chip shortage** — Tim Cook **reduced the number of suppliers and warehouses** in response
- **Quality management failures worth quoting:**
  - **2013** — iPhones returned due to inadequate staff training, **$1.6 billion cost**
  - **2012** — defective **Apple Maps** launch
  - **2018** — facial recognition failure on **iPhone X**
- **Strategic role:** **good/service differentiation**, not cost leadership. Premium positioning, design, ecosystem lock-in

> **The interdependence line:** operations quality failures (2013 returns, $1.6bn) hit finance directly through warranty and replacement costs, and marketing through reputational damage.

## 4. Marketing

- **Positioning:** high-end, quality and design. Premium price, never discounted
- **Branding:** logo changed three times since **1979**. Bitten apple, globally recognisable. Packaging minimalist — white with silver logos
- **Market share vs Samsung:** **17% vs 20% (Q2 2023)** → **23% vs 16% (Q4 2024)**
- **Threats:** low-cost Asian competitors — **Huawei, Xiaomi**
- **Criticism to concede:** innovation questioned in recent years, with focus narrowing to camera improvements
- **Pricing — iPhone X, 64GB, US$:** Russia **$1,390**, showing **price discrimination** across markets. Latest launch **$2,199 (Pro Max)**
- **Approach shift:** early iPhones used a **sales approach** (educating customers on features); as expectations grew, shifted to a **marketing approach** (research-driven, persuasive)
- **Market research:** monthly country-by-country surveys of iPhone buyers, plus post-purchase feedback surveys. *"Every month, Apple surveys iPhone buyers"* — reveals purchase drivers versus Android
- **Product life cycle responses:**
  - **GFC** → declining sales → low-cost **iPhone 5c**, older models kept on sale
  - **COVID-19** → **iPhone SE (2020)** and "Mini" versions for price-conscious buyers
  - **iPhone 5c** (plastic colours) **failed** — quality expectations outweighed colour preference
  - **iPhone 5s** (metallic colours) **succeeded** — colour associated with quality
- **Global expansion:** saturated developed markets → pivot to China and India. **2014** contract with **China Mobile**, the world's largest carrier
- **Regulatory friction:** iPhone 6 launch delayed in China over privacy; India blocked store expansion for years, eased **2020**; India's **$6.6 billion incentive program** boosted local manufacturing
- **Legal:** fined **$9 million by the ACCC** for misrepresentations regarding iPhone and iPad repairs

## 5. Influences — Economic and Government

- **2022:** US Federal Reserve **contractionary monetary policy** in response to inflation → higher interest rates → pressure on Apple's short-term debt obligations and gearing
- **Post-COVID inflation** rose above target for the first time in decades, driving materially higher rates in 2023–2024
- **IMF forecast low growth** for advanced economies (North America, Europe, Japan) across 2023–2025
- Apple is taxed in the US and in countries hosting subsidiaries, notably **Ireland** — regulatory change in any of them affects profitability

## 6. Human Resources

> 🚨 **Trial scope (5 Aug 2026):** HR **strategies** and **effectiveness indicators** are **not assessed**. So this material is only for **Role of HR**, **Influences on HR**, and **the HR cycle** (acquisition → development → maintenance → separation). Don't build an answer around rewards, performance management, staff turnover or absenteeism.

### Workforce

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-time equivalent employees** | ~**164,000** | ~**166,000** |

*Source: Apple 10-K, FY ending 28 Sep 2024 and 27 Sep 2025.*

### Acquisition

- Apple is an **Equal Employment Opportunity employer**, taking affirmative action to offer employment and advancement to all applicants including minorities, women, protected veterans and people with disabilities
- Recruitment emphasises specialist skills — engineering, design, silicon — competing directly with other technology firms for a limited talent pool

### Development

- **Over 30 million supplier employees trained** on workplace rights across the life of the program
- **Supplier Employee Development Fund** — launched at **$50 million in 2022**, since expanded — funds worker voice programs, rights training, education and skill development
- **2013 failure worth quoting:** the iPhone returns episode was caused by **inadequate staff training**, costing **$1.6 billion**. A direct HR failure with operations and finance consequences

### Maintenance and separation

- **Supplier Code of Conduct** sets labour standards across the supply chain. **Updated in 2025** with stronger standards developed with experts, suppliers and Apple teams
- Labour conditions at contract manufacturers, notably **Foxconn**, remain a recurring **ethics and CSR** issue — working hours, underage labour and living conditions have all drawn scrutiny
- Apple audits suppliers against the Code and publishes findings annually in its **Supply Chain Progress Report**

> **The HR interdependence line that works in any essay:** *"Apple's 2013 iPhone returns, caused by inadequate staff training, cost the business $1.6 billion — demonstrating that human resource failures translate directly into operations quality failures and finance losses."*

> **The CSR tension to name:** Apple's own workforce of ~166,000 is well paid and highly skilled, but the **majority of people making its products are not employed by Apple at all**. They work for contract manufacturers. That gap between legal responsibility and ethical responsibility is the strongest CSR argument available in this case study.

## 8. How to Drop a Stat Into a Sentence

```
CLAIM      [Apple's X] [improved/worsened] between [YEAR] and [YEAR],
DATA       from [FIGURE] to [FIGURE],
CAUSE      driven by [REASON].
BENCHMARK  Against [COMPETITOR / INDUSTRY / IDEAL], this indicates [JUDGEMENT].
IMPLICATION Consequently, [LONG-TERM EFFECT ON THE BUSINESS].
```

**Worked:**

> Apple's accounts receivable turnover worsened between 2024 and 2025, from **32 days to 35 days**, driven by expanded global sales and more flexible credit terms in services and international markets. Against the **ideal benchmark of 30 days**, this indicates deteriorating cash flow efficiency. Consequently, cash is tied up for longer, placing pressure on liquidity and increasing reliance on short-term borrowing — which is why **factoring** is recommended.

> **Never state a figure without the year, and never state a figure without a comparison.** Those two habits are the difference between Band 5 and Band 6.
