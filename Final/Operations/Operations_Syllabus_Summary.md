# Operations - Syllabus Summary

## 1. Role of Operations Management

- **Strategic contribution:** cost leadership and goods/services differentiation
- **Transformation:** inputs → transformation processes → outputs; value added through production or service delivery
- **Goods and services:** tangible/storable goods; intangible services produced and consumed together; quality measured against different expectations
- Goods with service components, pure goods and pure services
- **Interdependence:**
  - Marketing forecasts demand and communicates customer preferences; operations supplies products for sale
  - Finance funds equipment, technology and inputs; operations controls production costs
  - HR supplies and trains staff; operations identifies skill and staffing needs

## 2. Influences on Operations

- **Globalisation:** global markets, sourcing and production; economies of scale; stronger competition
- **Technology:** administrative and processing technology; efficiency, consistency, productivity and cost effects
- **Quality expectations:** design, fitness for purpose, durability, professionalism and reliability
- **Cost-based competition:** waste reduction, standardisation, automation, bulk buying and economies of scale
- **Government policy:** tax, industrial relations, environmental and training requirements; compliance costs
- **Legal regulation:**
  - **Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW):** duty of care, hazard control, training and worker consultation
  - **Workers Compensation Act 1987 (NSW):** compulsory workers compensation insurance
  - **Protection of the Environment Operations Act 1997 (NSW):** environment protection licences and pollution controls
  - **Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (Cth):** federal approval for significant environmental impacts
  - **Waste Avoidance and Resource Recovery Act 2001 (NSW):** waste reduction and landfill levy
  - **Food Act 2003 (NSW):** food safety supervision and safe handling
  - **Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth):** consumer guarantees, product safety, recalls and fair trading
  - **Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth):** minimum employment conditions, awards and workplace relations
  - **Modern Slavery Act 2018 (Cth):** supply-chain risk reporting for qualifying large businesses
  - **Income Tax Assessment Acts and A New Tax System (GST) Act 1999 (Cth):** tax and GST obligations
- **Environmental sustainability:** reduce waste, recycle, lower emissions and protect future resources
- **CSR and triple bottom line:** financial, social and environmental performance; short-term cost trade-offs against reputation and long-term risk

## 3. Operations Processes

- **Inputs:**
  - **Transformed resources:** materials, information and customers
  - **Transforming resources:** human resources and facilities
- **Transformation process and the four Vs:**
  - **Volume:** production quantity and scale
  - **Variety:** product range and process flexibility
  - **Variation in demand:** fluctuation and capacity planning
  - **Visibility:** customer contact and service requirements
- **Technology choices:** leading-edge or established; cost, reliability, quality and competitive effects
- **Task design:** duties, skills, time and role requirements
- **Process layouts:** process/functional, product/assembly line, fixed-position and office
- **Monitoring:** actual results against plans using KPIs
- **Controlling:** investigate variances and take corrective action
- **Improvement:** reduce waste, inefficiency and bottlenecks; involve staff in continuous improvement
- **Operations KPIs:** lead times, inventory turnover, defects, capacity utilisation, unit costs, waste and warranty claims
- **Outputs:** goods/services, customer service, warranties and quality feedback
- **Sequencing:** activity order; scheduling: activity duration
- **Gantt charts:** task order, duration, concurrency, progress and scheduling conflicts
- **Critical path analysis:** dependencies, critical activities, project duration and float/slack
- **Bottleneck:** lowest-capacity point that limits total output

## 4. Operations Strategies

- **Performance objectives:** quality, speed, dependability, flexibility, customisation and cost
- **New product/service design:**
  - Consumer-driven or technology-driven design
  - Research → prototype → testing → refinement → production/launch
  - Supply, quality, capacity and cost considerations
- **Supply chain management:**
  - **Supplier choice:** demand, quality, flexibility, timeliness and cost
  - Supplier rationalisation, backward vertical integration and responsive supply chains
  - **Global sourcing:** cost and expertise benefits against quality, communication and supply risks
  - E-commerce and e-procurement; B2B and B2C ordering
  - **Logistics:** transport, storage, distribution, materials handling and packaging
- **Outsourcing:** cost, efficiency, specialist skills and focus on core work; quality-control, security, dependency and skill-loss risks
- **Technology:** CAD, CAM, robotics and automation
- **Inventory management:**
  - **Just in Case (JIC):** availability and bulk savings against holding costs, tied-up capital and obsolescence
  - **Just in Time (JIT):** lower storage costs and freed working capital against supplier and interruption risk
  - FIFO, LIFO and weighted average for inventory valuation; EOQ balances ordering and holding costs
- **Quality management:**
  - **Quality control:** inspection and corrective action
  - **Quality assurance:** systems that prevent defects
  - **TQM:** whole-business responsibility, customer focus and continuous improvement
  - **Six Sigma:** DMAIC and defect reduction
  - **Kaizen:** incremental improvement and waste elimination
  - **Quality circles:** employee groups solving quality problems
- **Resistance to change:** equipment, redundancy and retraining costs; fear, uncertainty and inertia
- **Managing change:** assess need, communicate, build support, use change agents and apply change models
- **Global factors:** global sourcing, economies of scale, scanning and learning, research and development
