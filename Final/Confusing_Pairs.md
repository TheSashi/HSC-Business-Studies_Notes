# Confusing Pairs — MCQ Drill Sheet

> **Why this file exists.** Across two trials (Killara and Knox), seven of eight MCQ errors were the **right syllabus dot point, wrong member of the pair**. You aren't missing content — you're picking the neighbour.
>
> **How to use it.** Cover the right column. Say the tell out loud. Anything you hesitate on, star it. Twenty minutes beats re-reading a module.
>
> Theory: [[Operations]] | [[Marketing]] | [[Finance_Management]] | [[Human_Resources]] · Marked papers: [[Trial_Practice_Killara_2022]]

## 1. ⚠️ The Eight You Have Actually Got Wrong

> Drill these first. Every one cost a real mark.

| Paper | Pair | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| Killara 4 | Resource / Industrial market | **Resource** = where raw materials are **sold** (farmers, miners). **Industrial** = businesses that **buy** inputs to make other goods. Farmers *operate in* resource, *sell to* industrial |
| Killara 6 | Unfair dismissal / Performance appraisal | **Dismissal** ends employment. **Appraisal** evaluates it. An appraisal is not a termination |
| Killara 10 | Common law contract / Enterprise agreement | **Common law** = individually negotiated, one person. **Enterprise agreement** = collective, must pass the **BOOT** |
| Killara 17 | Key business function / anything else | There are exactly **four**: operations, marketing, finance, HR. "Employees", "customers", "outsourcing" are **not** functions |
| Knox 5 | Financial cost of change / Inertia | The syllabus lists them **separately**. **Financial** = redundancy payments, retraining, new equipment, plant layout. **Inertia** = behavioural resistance, costs nothing directly |
| Knox 10 | Administrative / Developmental performance management | **Administrative** = measuring output to decide pay, promotion, retention. **Developmental** = feedback used to build skills. Data collection with no training attached is **administrative** |
| Knox 16 | Market share analysis / Marketing profitability analysis | Only **profitability** involves **cost**. Market share compares your sales to **industry** sales |
| Knox 17 | FIFO / LIFO | When prices rise, **FIFO** leaves the **fresh, expensive** stock on the balance sheet → higher inventory, higher profit |

## 2. Operations

### Processes

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Transformed / Transforming resources | **Transformed** = what gets **changed** (materials, information, customers). **Transforming** = what **does** the changing (human resources, facilities) |
| Sequencing / Scheduling | **Order** vs **duration** |
| Gantt chart / Critical path analysis | **Gantt** shows what and how long, including **concurrent** tasks. **CPA** shows what must finish before something else can start, giving the **critical path** and **float** |
| Critical path / Longest task list | It's the longest path by **time**, not by number of tasks |
| Float / Critical activity | Float is spare time. Critical activities have **zero** float |
| Lead time / Cycle time | **Lead** = order to receipt. **Cycle** = time to produce **one unit** |
| Bottleneck / Defect | **Bottleneck** = lowest-capacity point limiting total output. A defect is a quality failure. **Not the same thing** |

### Quality and layout

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Quality control / Quality assurance | **Control** finds defects after the fact. **Assurance** designs the process so they don't occur |
| Quality assurance / TQM | **Assurance** = a system (ISO 9000). **TQM** = a whole-of-business philosophy, everyone responsible |
| Design quality / Conformance quality | **Design** = does it match what consumers understand the product to be. **Conformance** = does it meet the specification |
| Process layout / Product layout | **Process** groups machines by **function** (job shop). **Product** arranges them in the **sequence of production** (assembly line) |
| Fixed position layout | The product **doesn't move** — shipbuilding, construction |

### Inventory

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| JIT / FIFO and LIFO | **JIT** is inventory **management** (when stock arrives). **FIFO/LIFO** are inventory **valuation** (what it's worth on the balance sheet) |
| FIFO / LIFO — rising prices | **FIFO**: higher closing inventory, lower COGS, **higher profit**. **LIFO**: lower closing inventory, higher COGS, **lower profit** |
| EOQ / Reorder point | **EOQ** = how **much** to order. **Reorder point** = **when** to order |
| Obsolescence / Spoilage | **Obsolescence** = outdated. **Spoilage** = physically deteriorated |

### Strategy and influences

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Speed / Dependability | **Speed** = how fast. **Dependability** = how reliably, **when you said you would** |
| Cost leadership / Lowest price | A low-cost producer may still charge a **premium**. Cost leadership is about your **costs**, not your prices |
| Outsourcing / Offshoring | **Outsourcing** = external provider. **Offshoring** = moved overseas, possibly still in-house |
| Outsourcing / Global sourcing | **Outsourcing** contracts out an **activity**. **Global sourcing** buys **inputs** from anywhere |
| Legal compliance / Ethical responsibility | **Compliance** = letter of the law, the minimum. **Ethics/CSR** = spirit of the law, voluntary. You can be fully compliant and still unethical |
| Compliance cost / Penalty | **Compliance cost** = the expense of **meeting** the law. A penalty is what you pay for **breaching** it |
| Economies of scale | Lower cost **per unit**, not lower total cost. Total cost still rises |
| Explicit / Implicit service | **Explicit** = tangible elements (time, expertise). **Implicit** = intangible feelings (atmosphere, status) |

## 3. Marketing

### Markets and segmentation

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Resource / Industrial / Intermediate / Consumer market | **Resource** = raw materials sold (primary producers). **Industrial** = buy inputs to produce other goods. **Intermediate** = retailers and wholesalers buying to **resell**. **Consumer** = individuals for personal use |
| Mass / Niche market | **Mass** = one product for everyone. **Niche** = one narrow segment |
| Market segment / Target market | A **segment** is a group with shared characteristics. The **target market** is the segment you decide to pursue |
| Demographic / Psychographic / Geographic / Behavioural | **Demographic** = who they are (age, income, gender). **Psychographic** = how they think (lifestyle, values, personality). **Geographic** = where they are. **Behavioural** = what they do (usage rate, loyalty, benefits sought) |
| Positioning / Branding / Differentiation | **Differentiation** = making the product distinct. **Branding** = the name and identity. **Positioning** = the **image relative to competitors** in the consumer's mind |

### Influences on consumer choice

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Psychological / Sociocultural | **Psychological** = inside the individual (perception, motives, attitudes, personality, learning). **Sociocultural** = other people (family, peers, reference groups, social class, culture) |
| Influences on choice / Marketing strategies | **Influences** = why the customer buys. **Strategies** = what the business does. Branding and price are **strategies**, not influences |

### Process and KPIs

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Situational analysis / Market research | **Situational** = SWOT and product life cycle, where the business stands **now**. **Research** = collecting data about customers and the market |
| Primary / Secondary data | **Primary** = you collect it (surveys, observation, focus groups) — specific, current, expensive. **Secondary** = someone else did (census, industry reports, internet) — cheap, fast, possibly outdated |
| Sales analysis / Market share analysis / Marketing profitability analysis | **Sales** = your sales against objectives. **Market share** = your sales against **industry** sales. **Profitability** = **cost** of a marketing activity against the revenue it generated |
| Monitoring / Controlling | **Monitoring** measures. **Controlling** compares to planned, investigates the **variance**, and takes **corrective action** |

### Strategies

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Price skimming / Price penetration | **Skimming** = launch **high**, lower over time (new tech). **Penetration** = launch **low** to gain share, then raise |
| Cost-plus / Market / Competition-based pricing | **Cost-plus** = cost + mark-up. **Market** = what the market will bear. **Competition-based** = at, below or above rivals |
| Above the line / Below the line promotion | **Above** = mass media the business **pays** for (TV, radio, print). **Below** = everything else (personal selling, sales promotion, PR, direct mail) |
| Publicity / Public relations | **Publicity** = free media coverage, **not controlled** by the business. **PR** = deliberate activity the business **plans** to build image |
| Push / Pull strategy | **Push** = promote to the **retailer** so they stock it. **Pull** = promote to the **consumer** so they demand it |
| Standardisation / Customisation | **Standardisation** = same product everywhere (economies of scale). **Customisation** = adapted to local tastes |
| Global branding / Global pricing | **Branding** = one identity worldwide. **Pricing** = one price strategy worldwide |

### Consumer law — the five

| Law | The tell |
|---|---|
| **Deceptive and misleading advertising** | Claims that mislead — false comparisons, fine print, altered product demonstrations |
| **Price discrimination** | Charging **different prices to different buyers** for the same good without justification |
| **Implied conditions** | Unstated but legally assumed — the good is fit for purpose and matches its description |
| **Warranties** | A promise to repair or replace. Cannot be fake or misleading |
| **Resale price maintenance** | A supplier **forcing** a retailer to sell at a set minimum price. **Illegal** |

> Citation: **Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth)**, containing the **Australian Consumer Law**, enforced by the **ACCC**.

## 4. Finance

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Liquidity / Solvency | **Time frame only.** Liquidity = short term (current ratio). Solvency = long term (gearing ratio) |
| Gearing / Leverage | **The same thing** — proportion funded by debt rather than equity |
| Control of current assets / current liabilities | **Assets** = cash, receivables, inventory (JIT, credit checks, cash budgets). **Liabilities** = payables, loans, overdrafts (paying on time, seeking discounts, minimising overdraft) |
| Debenture / Mortgage | **Debenture** = long-term debt to the public, **unsecured**. **Mortgage** = secured against property |
| Factoring / Leasing | **Factoring** = selling receivables for cash now. **Leasing** = renting the use of an asset |
| Primary / Secondary market | **Primary** = new shares, the **company** gets the money. **Secondary** = existing shares traded, company gets **nothing** |
| Overdraft / Bank loan | **Overdraft** = flexible, short term, on a current account. **Loan** = fixed amount, fixed term |
| Operating / Financial lease | **Operating** = short term, **owner maintains**. **Financial** = runs for the asset's useful life, lessee maintains |
| Income statement / Balance sheet | **Income statement** = performance **over a period**. **Balance sheet** = position at a **point in time** |
| Cash flow statement / Cash flow budget | **Statement** = record of the **past**. **Budget** = forecast of the **future** |
| Capitalising an expense / Expensing it | **Capitalising** puts the cost on the balance sheet as an asset, written off later as **depreciation**. This **understates expenses and overstates profit** now |
| Normalised earnings / Window dressing | **Normalised** = one-off items removed to show underlying performance, but **management decides what's unusual**. **Window dressing** = legal techniques to make the position look better at reporting date |
| Window dressing / Fraud | **Window dressing is legal.** Fraud is not |
| Hedging / Derivative | **Hedging** = any strategy reducing financial risk. A **derivative** is a specific type of hedging contract (futures, options, swaps, forwards) |
| Spot rate / Forward rate | **Spot** = today's exchange rate. **Forward** = locked in now for a future date |
| Merger / Takeover | **Merger** = both agree to combine. **Takeover** = one buys the other, which ceases to exist |
| Cost centre / Profit centre | **Cost centre** only incurs costs. **Profit centre** also generates revenue |
| Appreciation / Depreciation (currency) | **Appreciation** = AUD rises → exports dearer, imports cheaper. **Depreciation** = AUD falls → exports cheaper, imports dearer |

### The seven ratios

```
LIQUIDITY      Current Ratio     = Current Assets / Current Liabilities      ideal 2:1
SOLVENCY       Gearing Ratio     = Total Liabilities / Owners' Equity        ideal 0.5:1 to 1:1
PROFITABILITY  Gross Profit      = (Gross Profit / Sales) x 100
               Net Profit        = (Net Profit / Sales) x 100
               Return on Equity  = (Net Profit / Owners' Equity) x 100
EFFICIENCY     Expense Ratio     = (Total Expenses / Sales) x 100            lowest possible
               Accounts Receivable  Step 1: Sales / AR   Step 2: 365 / answer   ideal 30 days
```

> **A ratio without a comparison is a number, not analysis.** Always answer *compared to what?* — over time, industry average, or target. **If the paper gives you an industry average, use it.**

## 5. Human Resources

### Acquisition

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Job description / Job specification | **De-S-cription** = the po-**S**-ition. **Spec**-ification = the **spec**-ial qualities of the **person** |
| Recruitment / Selection | **Recruitment** builds the **pool**. **Selection** chooses from it |
| Internal / External recruitment | **Internal** = cheaper, faster, known quantity, but no fresh ideas. **External** = new skills and perspectives, but costly and slower to settle |
| Employee / Contractor | **Employee** = contract **of** service, business controls how and when, owes leave and super. **Contractor** = contract **for** service, controls own methods, invoices for a result |

### Development and maintenance

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Induction / Training | **Induction** introduces to the **business**. **Training** improves **job performance** |
| Training / Development | **Training** = the **current** role. **Development** = **future** responsibilities |
| Administrative / Developmental performance management | **Administrative** = evaluate output to decide pay, promotion, retention, termination. **Developmental** = feedback to build skills |
| Monetary / Non-monetary rewards | **Monetary** = cash or cash-equivalent (salary, bonus, performance pay). **Non-monetary** = flexible hours, recognition, leave, career development |
| Intrinsic / Extrinsic rewards | **Intrinsic** comes from **within** the person (satisfaction). **Extrinsic** is **given** by the business |
| Piece rate / Commission | **Piece rate** = pay per **unit produced**. **Commission** = pay per **sale** |
| Hygiene factors / Motivators | **Hygiene** prevents dissatisfaction but doesn't motivate (pay, conditions). **Motivators** actively create satisfaction (achievement, recognition, responsibility) |

### Separation

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Voluntary / Involuntary separation | **Voluntary** = the employee chooses (resignation, retirement, voluntary redundancy). **Involuntary** = the business decides (retrenchment, dismissal, forced redundancy) |
| Redundancy / Dismissal | **Redundancy** = the **JOB** no longer exists. **Dismissal** = the **PERSON** is removed. Redundancy attracts severance; dismissal for misconduct does not |
| Retrenchment / Retirement | **Retrenchment** is involuntary — the business no longer needs the role. **Retirement** is voluntary |
| Summary / Unfair dismissal | **Summary** = immediate, for serious misconduct. **Unfair** = harsh, unjust or unreasonable |
| Unfair dismissal / Unlawful termination | **Unfair** = harsh, unjust or unreasonable. **Unlawful** = for a **prohibited reason** (pregnancy, race, union membership, illness) |
| Constructive dismissal | Employee **resigns** because the employer's conduct left them no choice. Still counts as dismissal |

### Legal and disputes

| Pair | The tell |
|---|---|
| Award / Enterprise agreement | **Award** = industry-wide safety net, applies by default. **Enterprise agreement** = workplace-negotiated, must pass the **BOOT** |
| Enterprise agreement / Common law contract | **Agreement** = collective. **Common law** = individual |
| NES / Awards | **NES** = 11 legislated minimums for **all** employees. **Awards** = industry-specific, on top |
| Mediation / Conciliation / Arbitration | **Mediation** facilitates only. **Conciliation** suggests solutions. **Arbitration** imposes a **binding** decision |
| Strike / Lockout | **Strike** = employees stop work. **Lockout** = employer shuts them out |
| EEO / Anti-discrimination | **EEO** requires decisions on **merit** and removal of barriers. **Anti-discrimination** **prohibits** specified conduct |
| Turnover / Absenteeism | **Turnover** = staff leaving and being replaced. **Absenteeism** = unplanned days off |
| Span of control / Chain of command | **Span** = how many people a manager supervises. **Chain** = the reporting lines |

## 6. Same Word, Different Topic

> These catch people because the word is genuinely used twice with different meanings. **Check which module the question is in.**

| Word | In Operations | In Finance |
|---|---|---|
| **Control** | Compare actual to planned, investigate the **variance**, take **corrective action** | Manage the **level** you hold of current assets and current liabilities |
| **Quality control** | Inspecting output to find defects | — |

| Word | Meaning A | Meaning B |
|---|---|---|
| **Depreciation** | Writing off an asset's cost over its useful life (Finance) | The AUD falling in value (Global) |
| **Matching principle** | Match the **term of finance** to the **use** of funds | Match revenue to the expenses incurred earning it |
| **Differentiation** | Operations: distinguishing the good or service itself | Marketing: the strategy of making the product distinct from competitors |
| **Efficiency** | Operations: productivity, output per input | Finance: the **expense ratio** and **accounts receivable turnover** |

## 8. MCQ Method

> Four minutes lost to careless reading is four marks. Same as a full extended-response paragraph.

```
1. UNDERLINE the exact noun the question asks for.
   "which key business FUNCTION"  →  only four options qualify
   "control of current ASSETS"    →  liabilities options are out
   "FINANCIAL cost"               →  behavioural options are out

2. Check for a directional word that flips the answer.
   SEEKING a discount (you pay)  vs  OFFERING a discount (you're paid)
   sell IN a market              vs  sell TO a market
   an INCREASE in economic activity (boom only)

3. If two options are from the same pair, ONE of them is the trap.
   Ask which member the question actually named.

4. On calculations, compare the given figures before deciding.
   Inventory $15 000 sold for $15 000 = no profit = no change.
```
