# Operations Management — Exam-Ready Notes

---

## 1. ROLE OF OPERATIONS

Operations = business process of **transformation** and **value adding**.
- Transformation: converting inputs into outputs
- Value adding: creating extra value as inputs become outputs (manufacturing: physical changes; services: knowledge/skills)

**Strategic Role**: Contribute to strategic direction. Two key strategies:
- **Cost Leadership**: Lowest costs, most price competitive while profitable. Achieved via economies of scale, automation, bulk buying, eliminating waste.
- **Product Differentiation**: Distinguishing goods/services from competitors. Goods: vary features, quality, augmented features (warranty, delivery). Services: vary time, expertise, qualifications.

**Goods vs Services**:
| Goods | Services |
|-------|----------|
| Tangible, can be stored | Intangible, produced & consumed simultaneously |
| Standardised or customised | Standardised (fast food) or customised (legal) |
| Quality measured by durability/fitness | Quality measured by professionalism/reliability |

---

## 2. INPUTS → TRANSFORMATION → OUTPUTS

**Transformed Resources** (changed by the process):
- Materials/intermediate goods, Information, Customers

**Transforming Resources** (carry out the change):
- Human Resources (skills, training, motivation), Facilities (plant, machinery, layout)

**Outputs**: Final goods/services, Customer service, Warranties

---

## 3. THE 4Vs

| V | What it means | High | Low |
|---|--------------|------|-----|
| **Volume** | How much is made | Efficient, automated, low unit cost | Manual, skilled, high unit cost |
| **Variety** | Range of products | Complex, flexible, expensive | Simple, efficient, standardised |
| **Variation** | Demand fluctuation | Need flexible resources, accurate forecasting | Stable planning, consistent resources |
| **Visibility** | Customer contact | Skilled staff, responsive, quality interaction | Speed, consistency, cost control |

---

## 4. TECHNOLOGY, TASK DESIGN & LAYOUT

**Technology**: Leading edge (new, untested) vs Established (proven). Benefits: faster production, fewer errors, consistent quality, labour cost savings long-term.

**Task Design**: Classifying job activities for easy employee performance. Steps: define → analyse into duties → allocate difficulty/time → match to awards → articulate via job descriptors.

**Layout Types**:
| Type | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **Process** | High variety, low volume; grouped by function | Hospital, bank, printery |
| **Product** | High volume, consistent quality; assembly line | Car factory, Foxconn |
| **Fixed Position** | Large immovable products; workers come to site | Construction |

---

## 5. MONITORING, CONTROLLING & IMPROVEMENT

**Monitoring**: Measuring actual vs planned performance via KPIs (lead times, inventory turnover, defect rates, capacity, costs).

**Controlling**: Assessing KPIs against targets, investigating variations, taking corrective action.

**Improvement**: Systematic reduction of inefficiencies/waste/bottlenecks. Areas: time, process flows, quality, cost, efficiency. **Continuous improvement** = ongoing commitment to perfection; requires staff involvement.

---

## 6. PERFORMANCE OBJECTIVES

| Objective | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Quality** | Design quality (consumer understanding) + conformance quality (meeting specifications) |
| **Speed** | Fast response to market changes; reduced lead/wait times |
| **Dependability** | Consistency and reliability of products |
| **Flexibility** | Ability to adjust to changes quickly |
| **Customisation** | Individualised products; mass customisation |
| **Cost** | Minimising expenses for maximum competitiveness |

---

## 7. INFLUENCES ON OPERATIONS

**Globalisation**: Removal of trade barriers; operations structured around global production facilities; opportunity (global markets, economies of scale) and threat (intensified competition).

**Technology**: Increased efficiency, reduced costs, consistent quality, higher productivity.

**Quality Expectations**: Goods — design, fitness for purpose, durability. Services — professionalism, reliability, customisation. Failure = customer dissatisfaction, returns, damaged reputation.

**Cost-Based Competition**: Pressure to lower production costs. Methods: eliminate waste, standardise, high volume, automate, economies of scale, bulk buy.

**Government Policies/Legal**: WHS Act, Fair Work Act, Environmental Protection Act, Competition & Consumer Act, Food Safety Act. Compliance costs = expenses of meeting requirements.

**Environmental Sustainability**: Operating without compromising future resources. Methods: minimise waste, recycle, reduce carbon footprint. **CSR**: triple bottom line (financial + social + environmental). **Legal compliance** (letter of law) vs **ethical responsibility** (spirit of law).

---

## 8. SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT

Integration and management of flow of supplies from inputs through transformation to outputs.

**Sourcing factors**: Consumer demand, quality of inputs, flexibility/timeliness, cost.

**Trends**: Supplier rationalisation, backwards vertical integration, cost minimisation (offshore), flexible/responsive supply chains.

**E-Commerce**: E-procurement systems; B2B (automatic restocking) and B2C (direct to consumer).

**Global Sourcing**: Purchasing without location constraint. Advantages: lower costs, reduced lead times, access to unavailable materials. Disadvantages: quality risk, impact on domestic businesses.

**Logistics**: Distribution, transportation, storage (warehousing + distribution centres), materials handling and packaging.

---

## 9. INVENTORY MANAGEMENT

**Holding Stock (JIC)**:
- Adv: Meet demand, alternatives if out of stock, reduce lead times, immediate revenue, bulk cost savings
- Disadv: Holding costs (storage, insurance, theft, spoilage), capital tied up, obsolescence

**Just in Time (JIT)**: Produce only to meet demand.
- Adv: Reduced storage costs, no theft/damage risk, no obsolescence
- Disadv: Lead time increases with demand variation, supplier delivery issues, production shutdown risk

**Inventory Valuation**:
| | Costs Increasing | Costs Decreasing |
|--|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Minimise profit** (less tax) | LIFO | FIFO |
| **Maximise profit** (investors) | FIFO | LIFO |

**Weighted Average**: Average cost of all inventory; smooths price fluctuations.

**EOQ**: Optimal order quantity minimising total costs (ordering + holding).

*If Q asks about inventory MANAGEMENT → JIC or JIT. If Q asks about inventory VALUATION → FIFO or LIFO.*

## 10. QUALITY MANAGEMENT

| Approach | Key Feature |
|----------|-------------|
| **Quality Control** | Inspection at various production points; define standards, test, corrective action |
| **Quality Assurance** | System to prevent defects; "right the first time"; ISO 9000 standards |
| **TQM** | Holistic; every employee responsible for quality; manage total business |
| **Six Sigma** | Data-driven; 3.4 defects per million; DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control) |
| **Kaizen** | Continuous incremental improvement; all employees; eliminate waste (muda) |
| **Quality Circles** | Small employee groups meet to identify and solve problems |

## 11. OUTSOURCING

Use of external providers for business activities (domestic or global).

**Advantages**: Cost savings, efficiency, access to specialist skills, focus on core business, increased accountability via contracts, simplification.

**Disadvantages**: Communication/language barriers, loss of quality control, security risks, dependency on partner, loss of corporate memory, reputation affected by partner.

---

## 12. GLOBAL FACTORS

1. **Global Sourcing**: Inputs sourced for cost advantage
2. **Economies of Scale**: High volumes → lower per-unit costs (capital investment, technology, global branding, training)
3. **Scanning and Learning**: Learn from global best practices
4. **R&D**: Leading-edge technologies; global expertise increases opportunities

---

## 13. RESISTANCE TO CHANGE

**Two sources**:
- **Financial**: New equipment costs, redundancy payouts, retraining costs, reorganising plant layout
- **Psychological (Inertia)**: Fear of uncertainty → less motivated → lower productivity → quality issues → increased lead time/defects → lost customers

**Managing Change**:
1. Identify sources of change and assess need
2. Communicate with employees; get widespread support
3. Use change agents
4. Apply change models

---

## 14. NEW PRODUCT DESIGN

**Approaches**: Consumer-driven (preferences determine design) vs Technology-driven (innovation enables new products).

**Steps**: Market research → Design & prototype → Testing → Refinement → Production & launch

**Key Considerations**: Supply chain (new suppliers?), Quality (maintain standards?), Capacity (new resources/tech needed?), Costs.

**Service Design**: Explicit service (tangible: time, expertise) + Implicit service (intangible: wellbeing, feeling) → then cost determined.

---

## 15. SEQUENCING & SCHEDULING

**Sequencing**: Order of activities. **Scheduling**: Duration of activities.

**GANTT Charts**: Visual timeline showing activities, order, and duration. Advantages: shows concurrent activities, identifies bottlenecks, easy communication.

**Critical Path Analysis**: Shows tasks, durations, and order. **Critical path** = shortest time to complete all tasks. Identifies which tasks cannot be delayed (critical) vs those with float.

## 16. HSC VERB FRAMEWORKS

| Verb | What to do |
|------|-----------|
| **Describe** | Features/characteristics; define + give 2+ features |
| **Explain** | Cause and effect; sentence answer → how → why → link to goals |
| **Distinguish** | Differences between two things; def 1 → def 2 → differences → link |
| **Outline** | Sketch in general terms; answer + brief details |
| **Assess** | Judgement based on criteria; judge → impact → effect → implication → restate |
| **Analyse** | Show relationship + implications; define → impact → effect → link to objectives |
| **Discuss** | Issues + advantages + disadvantages; identify issue → prove adv/disadv with links |

### Past Paper Answer Structures

**"Assess the impact of customised products on the operations process" (Q21c)**
- Judgement: It has a large impact on the operational process...
- Inputs: Customers become a transformed resource
- Process: Technology, task design, and layout all affected
- Effect: Flexible production needed, skilled workers, process layouts
- Restate judgement

**"Analyse the use of outsourcing in achieving the strategic role of Operations"**
- Define outsourcing
- How does outsourcing impact operations (cost reduction, access to expertise)
- Effect: link to performance objectives (cost, quality, flexibility)
- Implication: linking to cost leadership OR product differentiation
- Use relational verbs: therefore, as a result, consequently, this means

## KEY CASE STUDY: APPLE

| Topic | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Inputs** | Transformed: screens, chips, circuitry, customer feedback. Transforming: Foxconn factories, Chinese labour |
| **4Vs** | High volume at Foxconn; wide variety (iPhone, iPad, Mac); variation risky (over/under ordering); high visibility (customer-driven design) |
| **Quality** | 5M iPhones returned (2013) — lack of training, $1.6bn cost; defective Apple Maps (2012); facial recognition failure on iPhone X (2018) |
| **Outsourcing** | Production to Foxconn: lower cost, expertise, economies of scale; but loss of quality control, security risks, cultural barriers |
| **Supply Chain** | 785 suppliers, 31 countries; COVID delayed iPhone 12; chip shortage (2021); Tim Cook reduced suppliers/warehouses |
| **Cost Objectives** | iPhone SE cheaper components; in-house chip development; Samsung→TSMC supplier switch; China→India production; purchased Intel 5G division |
| **Global** | Components from USA, Korea, Taiwan, Europe, China; global branding; R&D investment growing |
