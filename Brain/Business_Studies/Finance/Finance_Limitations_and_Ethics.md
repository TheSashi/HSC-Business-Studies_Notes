# Limitations on Financial Statements & Ethical Issues

> **HSC Business Studies — Finance** | Normalised earnings, capitalised expenses, asset valuation, timing issues, debt repayments, notes to financial statements, and ethical practices.

---

## 1. Normalised Earnings

- Earnings adjusted for **cyclical ups and downs** of the economy
- On P&L and Balance Sheet, earnings are adjusted to remove **unusual or one-off influences**
- **Example:** Sale of land/property where business made a capital gain
- Shows a **true reflection** of earnings from operations

### Ethical Risk
- Can be used to **mislead stakeholders**
- Revenues could be delayed or accelerated
- Costs may be manipulated to suit the business
- Aim: give an inaccurate picture of earnings

---

## 2. Capitalised Expenses

- Purchase price of non-current assets is added to setup costs and recorded as the **asset value** in the balance sheet
- This value is then **written off as depreciation** over the asset's useful life

### Example: Equipment Purchase

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Purchase Price | $1,000,000 |
| Delivery Costs | $500 |
| Installation Costs | $1,500 |
| Training Costs | $5,000 |
| **Value in Balance Sheet** | **$1,007,000** |

### Ethical Risk
- Inappropriate capitalisation — taking ordinary expenses and capitalising them to mislead stakeholders
- Only legitimate for purchasing non-current assets (depreciation reflects gradual loss)

---

## 3. Valuing Assets

### Historical Cost Concept
- Assets recorded at **purchase price** in the Balance Sheet
- In reality, asset values change over time

### Appreciation
- Land and property tend to **appreciate** in value
- Business should revalue to show true reflection
- Requires at least **two independent valuations**

### Depreciation (Straight-Line)

**Formula:** (Historical Cost - Disposal Value) / Useful Lifetime

**Example:** $1,000,000 equipment, 10-year life, $1,000 disposal value
- Annual depreciation = ($1,000,000 - $1,000) / 10 = **$99,900/year**

| Year | Income Statement (Expense) | Balance Sheet (Asset Value) |
|------|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | $99,900 | $1,000,000 - $99,900 = $900,100 |
| 2 | $99,900 | $1,000,000 - $199,800 = $800,200 |

### Ethical Risk
- **Intangible assets** (goodwill, trademarks) are impossible to value accurately
- **Example:** Facebook bought WhatsApp for $19 billion — never made a profit but had 45+ million users. Value at purchase price or potential future value?
- Businesses can get multiple valuations and **choose the one that suits** their desired financial position

---

## 4. Timing Issues

- Financial reports cover activities over **one year**
- Business's financial position may not be true if experienced **seasonal fluctuations**
- **Matching principle:** Revenue must be matched to all expenses incurred in earning that revenue in a particular period (even if cash not yet paid/received)

### Ethical Risk
- Recording transactions outside current financial year to make business appear more profitable
- **Income smoothing:** Recording expenses in wrong period to reduce tax

**Example:** XYZ company pays $10,000 insurance in advance (January 2022 for the year). Records all $10,000 as expense in June 2021/2022 — but only 6 months ($5,000) was incurred in that year. Reduces profit and tax payable.

---

## 5. Debt Repayments

- Reports cannot disclose specific information about some debt repayments
- **Example:** Money owed for annual leave/sick leave — difficult to predict when workers will take leave
- Cannot recognise exactly when this debt will be paid

---

## 6. Notes to Financial Statements

- Additional details left out of main reporting documents (balance sheet, income statements)
- Normally appear in **appendices** of Annual Reports and/or Prospectus
- **Not regulated as strictly** as the financial report itself — may be used to mislead

---

## 7. Ethical Issues — Overview

Accountants must display:
- **Integrity** — honest and transparent
- **Objectivity** — unbiased
- **Confidentiality** — protect sensitive information
- **Professional competence** — high level of technical ability

### Standards and Oversight
- **IFRS** (International Financial Reporting Standards)
- **AAS** (Australian Accounting Standards) — adapt IFRS to Australian market
- Business accounts must be **audited** by independent organisations (ASIC)
- Ensures accounts present a **true and fair** representation

## 8. Unethical Financial Practices

| Practice | Description | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Window Dressing** | Legal techniques to show better financial position | Selling bulk stock to valued customer at end of year at discount to show improved cash flow and low stock |
| **Income Smoothing** | Reducing taxation by reducing Net Profit | Recording prepaid insurance in wrong financial year |
| **Continuous Disclosure** | Not legally required to inform shareholders of potential losses after positive forecasts | Ethically should provide information for informed decisions |
| **Valuing Intangible Assets** | Difficult to place value on goodwill, patents, licences | WhatsApp valuation dispute |
| **Asset Valuation** | Getting multiple valuations and choosing the most favourable | Legally requires independent valuation but can choose which one |
| **Corporate Raiding** | Buying undervalued company, increasing profit or selling assets | Increases owner's personal wealth at cost of employees who lose jobs |
| **Misuse of Funds** | Using business credit cards/money for personal use | — |
| **Fictitious Revenues** | Creating fake sales to inflate profits | — |
| **Hidden Liabilities** | Concealing debts from reports | — |

## 9. HSC Exam Response Structure — Limitations Questions

When answering questions about limitations of financial reports:
1. **Identify** the limitation (normalised earnings, capitalised expenses, etc.)
2. **Explain** how it creates a misleading picture
3. **Provide an example** to illustrate
4. **Link** to ethical implications
