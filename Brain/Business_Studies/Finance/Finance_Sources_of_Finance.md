# Sources of Finance

> **HSC Business Studies — Finance** | Internal vs external, debt vs equity, all major sources with advantages and disadvantages.

---

## 1. Overview

A business can source money from **inside** or **outside**:
- **Internal sources** — from within the business (equity in balance sheet)
- **External sources** — from external organisations (debt or equity)

Finding the appropriate source involves financial decision making — identifying, collecting and analysing relevant information.

---

## 2. Internal Sources of Finance

Recorded under **equity** in the balance sheet.

| Source | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Owner's Equity** | Funds contributed by owners or partners to establish and build the business |
| **Retained Profit** | Net profit reinvested in the business — increases owner's claim |

> **Balance Sheet:** Capital + Retained Profit = Owners' Equity

---

## 3. External Sources — Debt Finance (Short-Term)

**Debt** = any borrowed money. Short-term = repayable within 12 months.

| Source | Description | Key Features |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| **Overdraft** | Flexibility to borrow from bank at short notice through cheque/current account | Interest payable; tax deductible; more flexible than loans; assists with short-term liquidity |
| **Commercial Bill** | Written order for loan guaranteed by business's bank | 30–180 days; usually $100,000+; borrowed from businesses with surplus funds |
| **Factoring** | Selling accounts receivable to a specialist factoring firm | Receives up to 90% of receivables within 48 hours; factoring company takes over collection; riskiest due to unpaid debts (risk borne by debt collection agency) |

---

## 4. External Sources — Debt Finance (Long-Term)

| Source | Description | Key Features |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| **Mortgage** | Loan repaid over set years with interest | Used for land/property; property becomes security; bank takes property if defaults |
| **Debenture** | Long-term debt via prospectus on securities exchange | Set interest rate for fixed time; more risky than mortgages (no specific property security); rate not affected by profit level |
| **Unsecured Notes (Bonds)** | Notes issued by finance companies | Higher interest rates reflecting greater risk; no specific security |
| **Leasing** | Leasing non-current assets (cars, equipment) for payments | Reduces acquisition cost; tax advantages (lease payments tax deductible); reduces net profit and company tax |

### Types of Leases

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Operating Lease** | Short-term (shorter than asset life); owner carries out maintenance |
| **Financial Lease** | Leasor purchases asset on behalf of leasee; covers asset's lifetime |

**Why lease rather than buy?** Tax deductible, flexibility for short-term needs, distributes cost over time (better for cash flow).

## 5. External Sources — Equity Finance

Equity = owner's financial claim on business assets.

| Legal Structure | Equity Term |
|----------------|-------------|
| Sole Trader/Partnership | Owners' Equity |
| Public/Private Company | Shareholders' Funds |

### Private Equity
- Current owners invite people/organisations to invest
- Normally **proprietary companies** (Pty Ltd)
- Shareholders don't necessarily have control
- No guarantee of dividends or profit share

### Public Equity
Companies listed on **ASX** — anyone can invest.

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Ordinary Shares** | Issued on ASX, may or may not have voting rights at AGM |
| **New Share Issues** | Shares sold for first time (IPO or new offering for more finance) |
| **Rights Issue** | Offer to existing shareholders to buy more shares |
| **Placements** | Additional shares offered to specific institutions (venture capitalists) and investors |
| **Share Purchase Plans** | Offer to existing shareholders to purchase more shares without brokerage fees; below normal prices; requires ASIC permission |

### Advantages vs Disadvantages of Equity

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|-----------|---------------|
| No debt servicing costs (interest) | Time to arrange |
| No dividend obligation in loss years | Dilutes ownership |
| (However, no dividends may cause shareholder uncertainty) | |

## 6. Financing Grants

Financial gifts from government to assist business establishment. Must meet strict criteria:
- Location of business
- Personal characteristics of owner (e.g., indigenous people)
- Type of industry (e.g., IT, innovative products)
- Essential services to underserved areas

> **Government does not get repaid** and does not get ownership share. Return is through increased employment, growth and taxes.

## 7. Budgets and Financial Control

### Budgets
- Plan for achieving set outcomes
- Establishing standards for monitoring
- Types: Operating, Project, Financial

### Record Systems
- Ensure data is accurate, reliable, efficient, accessible
- Legal requirement for shareholders and ATO
- Annual audits required for all public companies

### Financial Risks
- Theft, missed sales targets, non-payment by receivables, interest rate rises
- **Direct relationship** between borrowing level and risk

## 8. Key Formulas

```
Cash Flow Statement: Opening Balance + Receipts - Payments = Closing Balance
Income Statement: Sales - COGS = Gross Profit; Gross Profit - Expenses = Net Profit
Balance Sheet: Owners Equity = Assets - Liabilities
```
