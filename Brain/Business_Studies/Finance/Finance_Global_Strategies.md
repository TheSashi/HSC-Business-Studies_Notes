# Global Financial Strategies

> **HSC Business Studies — Finance** | Exchange rates, interest rates, international payments, hedging, and derivatives.

---

## 1. Exchange Rates

**Exchange rate** = the value of a country's money calculated using another country's money.

### Key Exchange Rates (approximate)

| Currency | AUD$1 = |
|----------|---------|
| US Dollar | US$0.72 |
| Euro | €0.62 |
| British Pound | £0.53 |
| Chinese Yuan | ¥4.93 |

### Impact of Exchange Rate Movements

| Scenario | Impact on Australian Business |
|----------|-------------------------------|
| Imports from China + AUD **depreciates** vs Yuan | Costs **more** to import |
| Borrowed from US + AUD **appreciates** vs USD | Pay **less** back |
| Lent to UK + AUD **depreciates** vs Pound | Receive **less** back |
| Exports to France + AUD **appreciates** vs Euro | Costs the business **less** |

> **Appreciation** = AUD gets stronger (buy more foreign currency per AUD)
> **Depreciation** = AUD gets weaker (buy less foreign currency per AUD)

## 2. Interest Rates

- Important to find the **lowest interest rates** globally
- Exchange rate fluctuations can make repayments more costly

### Advantages of Borrowing Overseas
1. Interest rate can be **cheaper**
2. Fewer **restrictions**
3. Finance may be acquired **more quickly and easily**

## 3. Methods of International Payments

| Method | How It Works | Risk Borne By |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| **Payment in Advance** | Exporter receives payment before sending goods | Importer (goods may never be sent) |
| **Letter of Credit** | Bank document authorising seller to draw specific amount from buyer's bank under certain conditions | Shared (bank mediates) |
| **Bills of Exchange** | Written order from seller requesting buyer pay specified amount at specified time; bank ensures goods delivered and exporter paid | Shared (bank mediates) |
| **Clean Payment** | Goods shipped before payment received | Exporter (significant risk; only with trusted partners) |

## 4. Hedging

**Hedging** = any strategy used to reduce financial risks. Like buying insurance to protect against unforeseen risks.

### Natural Hedging
- Setting up **subsidiaries** in other countries to pay for expenses/goods from that country
- Setting up **bank accounts** in countries where the business buys/sells goods

---

## 5. Derivatives

Special contracts between global businesses and suppliers.

### Forward Exchange Contract
- Agreement with FOREX business guaranteeing a **certain exchange rate on a certain day**
- **Advantage:** If spot rate is unfavourable (AUD depreciated), the contract rate is cheaper
- **Disadvantage:** If spot rate is favourable (AUD appreciated), the contract rate is more expensive

### Currency Options Contract
- Business has the **option** to buy/sell foreign currency when exchange rate movement is advantageous
- Can choose to use the **spot rate** (rate on the day) instead
- **No obligation** to take up the option
- Protected against unfavourable fluctuations while maintaining opportunity for gain

### Swap Contract
- Written contract allowing an Australian business to pay an overseas supplier in **another currency** at a spot rate
- **Example:** Australian business has excess USD; Chinese supplier agrees to accept USD (which they can use to pay their American supplier)

---

## 6. Hedging Comparison Table

| Type | Mechanism | Flexibility | Risk Level |
|------|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Forward Exchange Contract | Locks in fixed rate | Low — must use agreed rate | Moderate |
| Currency Options | Choice of contract or spot rate | High — can choose best rate | Low |
| Swap Contract | Pay in different currency | Medium — depends on agreement | Moderate |

---

## 7. HSC Exam Tips

- **Appreciation** helps importers (cheaper imports), hurts exporters (more expensive for foreign buyers)
- **Depreciation** helps exporters (cheaper for foreign buyers), hurts importers (more expensive imports)
- Always link exchange rate impacts to a **specific business scenario**
- Know the **difference** between forward contracts, options, and swaps
