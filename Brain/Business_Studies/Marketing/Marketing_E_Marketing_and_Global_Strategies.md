# E-Marketing & Global Marketing Strategies

> **HSC Business Studies — Marketing** | Internet marketing technologies, impact on the marketing mix, global branding, standardisation vs customisation, and global pricing strategies.

---

## 1. E-Marketing

**Definition**: Internet marketing — allows a business with online operations to reach a global audience.

### 1.1 E-Marketing Technologies

| Technology | Description | Marketing Use |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Web pages** | Combination of text, graphics, animation and video | Physical evidence (branding/packaging), process (ordering/communication), place (distribution), promotion (advertising on other sites) |
| **Podcasts** | Distribution of digital audio/video files over internet | Publicity — promoting companies through talking/showcasing. Effective if aimed at same audience as target market |
| **SMS** | Short message service — text messages between mobile phones | Direct promotion — cannot be ignored unless blocked. Used for sales notifications, tracking info, status updates, incentives |
| **Blogs** | Online diary allowing communication between business and customers | Building relationships, feedback, product information |
| **Social media advertising** | Use of Facebook and Twitter to promote business | Cost efficiency, vast global reach, higher market share |

### 1.2 Social Media Advertising — Advantages & Disadvantages
### 1.2 Social Media Advertising — Advantages & Disadvantages
| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|-----------|--------------|
| Cost efficient — cheaper than traditional media | Anyone can comment — even without trying the product |
| Vast global reach — connect to all consumers | Can worsen brand reputation through negative reviews |
| Higher market share potential | |

### 1.4 Apple's E-Marketing — Impact on the Marketing Mix

| P | How E-Marketing Influences It |
|---|------------------------------|
| **Product** | Detailed specifications online; displayed alongside accessories; FAQs and technical specs reduce customer service costs |
| **Price** | Price transparency — comparison with competitors; price comparison websites; increased profit margins by selling online (no retail store costs) |
| **Place** | Global platform without retail costs; sell directly to customers; tailor websites for languages/cultures. Limitation: some customers prefer viewing before purchasing |
| **Promotion** | Images, audio, video, detailed info on website; new product launches published online; social media ads; database of existing/potential customers for email/social media promotion |

## 2. Global Marketing Strategies

Many businesses operate in countries beyond their domestic operations. These are called **TNCs (Transnational Corporations)**.

### 2.1 Why Businesses Go Global
- Increase sales
- Further brand awareness
- Establish markets in new countries

### 2.2 Global Branding

**Definition**: The worldwide use of a name, logo, term or symbol.

- More effective and efficient to promote a brand rather than individual products
- A brand has the same meaning in any language

**Apple**: Logo changed three times since 1979 — current logo is simple, globally recognisable. Competitive advantage due to global brand recognition. In 2018, Google was most valuable brand (US$300B+), Apple close second.

### 2.3 Standardisation vs Customisation

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Standardisation** | Same marketing strategies worldwide — no differences in strategies across countries |
| **Customisation** | Different strategies for each country — changes based on culture, religion, tastes |

**Why research the market**: Differences in language, religion, tastes, and ethics require understanding before entering new markets.

**Apple Examples:**
- **Standardisation**: Same iPhone in every country; iPhone 5c ad featured people of various nationalities speaking their own language
- **Customisation**: iPhone 6 Plus designed for Asian market (large screen); different pricing by market
- **Combination**: Changes language/actors (standardised) but adapts pricing and some features (customised)

**McDonald's**: Uses a combination of standardised and customised strategies — some countries use the same approach, whereas others need strategies adapted to fit local culture.

### 2.4 Global Pricing Strategies

| Strategy | Description | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Customised pricing** | Different prices for the same product in different markets. Uses cost-plus method covering: transportation, taxes, warehousing, tariffs | iPhone X: Russia $1,390 vs USA $999 |
| **Market customised pricing** | Prices set according to local market conditions and exchange rate fluctuations | Prices adjust with currency changes |
| **Standardised pricing** | Same price anywhere in the world — risky due to different purchasing powers | Rarely used in practice |

**Factors affecting global pricing:**
- Currency exchange rates
- Transportation costs
- Local taxes and tariffs
- Competition in overseas markets
- Local purchasing power

### 2.5 Global Promotional Strategies
- Use variety of mediums: TV, Internet, radio
- Must consider language, religion, culture
- Same promotional message adapted for different cultural contexts
- Global businesses can reach millions with consistent messaging

### 2.6 Competitive Positioning

**Definition**: The formal process of determining how a business differentiates itself from competitors.

**Value proposition** includes three elements:
1. **Operational excellence** — efficiency, reliability
2. **Customer intimacy** — personalised service, understanding needs
3. **Product Leadership** — innovation, quality, features

**Apple**: Historically positioned at high end (quality/design). As developed markets saturated, turned to high-growth emerging economies (China, India). 2014: secured contract with China Mobile (largest carrier) — significant sales increase. Huawei has since overtaken Apple in market share.

## 3. Apple — Global Marketing Case Study

### Global Branding
- Apple logo is one of the most recognised worldwide
- Competitive advantage through global brand recognition
- Simple, consistent branding across all markets

### Standardisation
- Same iPhone found in every country
- Consistent core product and features
- iPhone 5c promotion focused on people from various nationalities

### Customisation
- Consideration of income levels (pricing)
- Tastes and preferences (product features)
- Culture and religion (promotion approach)
- Government regulations and infrastructure (distribution)
- iPhone 6 Plus suggested designed for Asian market (large screen)

### Global Pricing
- Different prices in different markets due to: currency exchange rates, transportation costs, local taxes, purchasing power
- Cost-plus method for new markets covering exportation costs

### Competitive Positioning
- Premium, high-quality positioning globally
- Adapted strategies for emerging economies (China, India)
- Distribution channel expansion (China Mobile contract 2014)
