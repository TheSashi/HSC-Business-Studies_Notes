# Human Resources Management — Complete Notes

## The Role of HRM

Human Resource Management is the **managing of people** to achieve business objectives. It is one of the four key business functions alongside operations, marketing, and finance.

**HRM as a strategic function:**
- Attracts, develops, and retains talented employees
- Aligns workforce capabilities with business goals
- Creates positive workplace culture
- Manages employee relations and compliance

**Link to business performance:**
- Skilled, motivated employees → higher productivity → better operations
- Good customer service training → improved marketing effectiveness
- Efficient staffing → cost control → better financial performance

---

## Relationship Between HRM and Other Functions

| Function | Relationship with HRM |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Operations** | HR recruits and trains production staff. Operations determines skill needs and staffing levels. Productivity depends on workforce quality. |
| **Marketing** | HR recruits marketing team. Marketing effectiveness depends on staff creativity and skills. Customer-facing staff need service training. |
| **Finance** | HR costs (wages, training) are major budget items. Finance sets salary budgets. HR must justify expenditure through productivity gains. |

## Legal Framework for Employment

### Key Legislation

| Law | Purpose |
|-----|---------|
| **Fair Work Act 2009** | National workplace relations system — minimum wages, unfair dismissal, enterprise bargaining |
| **WHS Act 2011** | Work health and safety — employer duty of care, hazard identification, risk management |
| **Anti-Discrimination Act** | Prohibits discrimination based on gender, race, age, disability, sexual orientation |
| **Modern Awards** | Industry-specific minimum pay and conditions |
| **National Employment Standards (NES)** | 11 minimum entitlements (annual leave, sick leave, hours, notice of termination) |

### Employment Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Full-time** | Regular hours (38 hrs/week), full entitlements |
| **Part-time** | Regular hours but fewer than full-time, pro-rata entitlements |
| **Casual** | No guaranteed hours, higher hourly rate (loading), no leave entitlements |
| **Contract** | Fixed-term or project-based, specific terms in contract |

## HRM Strategies

### 1. Recruitment and Selection

The process of attracting and choosing the right candidate.

**Steps in the recruitment process:**

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Job analysis** | Identify duties, responsibilities, skills needed for the role |
| **2. Job description** | Written document outlining the role (title, duties, conditions) |
| **3. Person specification** | Ideal candidate profile (qualifications, experience, skills, attributes) |
| **4. Job advertisement** | Post via job boards (Seek, LinkedIn), company website, social media, newspapers |
| **5. Application review** | Screen resumes, cover letters against selection criteria |
| **6. Selection methods** | Interviews, psychometric testing, skills tests, assessment centres, reference checks |
| **7. Appointment** | Offer letter, contract, negotiate terms |

**Internal vs External Recruitment:**

| | Internal | External |
|--|----------|----------|
| **Pros** | Cheaper, faster, motivates staff, known performance | Fresh ideas, larger pool, new perspectives |
| **Cons** | Limited pool, creates vacancy, inbreeding | Expensive, unknown performance, longer adjustment |

### 2. Training and Development

**Induction training:**
- Introduction to the organisation, policies, procedures, safety
- Usually first week on the job
- Reduces anxiety, speeds up productivity

**On-the-job training:**
- Learning while working (mentoring, coaching, job rotation, shadowing)
- Practical, relevant, immediate application
- Can disrupt production, quality depends on trainer

**Off-the-job training:**
- External courses, workshops, seminars, conferences, TAFE/university
- Expert instruction, dedicated learning environment
- Expensive, time away from work, may not be directly applicable

**Development:**
- Long-term career growth (leadership programs, further education)
- Succession planning — preparing employees for future roles
- Performance management integration — identify skill gaps through appraisals

### 3. Performance Management

Monitoring and evaluating employee performance.

**Methods:**

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)** | Measurable targets (sales targets, production output, customer satisfaction scores) |
| **Performance appraisals** | Formal review meetings (annually/quarterly) — set goals, review achievements, identify development needs |
| **360-degree feedback** | Feedback from multiple sources — supervisor, peers, subordinates, self-assessment |
| **Balanced scorecard** | Measures performance across 4 perspectives: financial, customer, internal processes, learning & growth |

**Purpose of performance management:**
- Identify high performers (reward, promotion)
- Identify underperformers (support, training, warnings)
- Align individual goals with business objectives
- Provide basis for pay decisions
- Document performance for legal protection

### 4. Dismissal and Redundancy

**Dismissal:**
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Termination** | Ending employment for poor performance or misconduct (following due process) |
| **Constructive dismissal** | Employee resigns due to employer's hostile actions (may be claimable as unfair) |
| **Unfair dismissal** | Termination that is harsh, unjust, or unreasonable — employee can claim through Fair Work Commission |

**Redundancy:**
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Voluntary redundancy** | Employee chooses to leave with a redundancy package |
| **Involuntary redundancy** | Position no longer needed (restructuring, technology), employee terminated with minimum notice and pay |

**Legal requirements:** Minimum notice periods, redundancy pay (based on years of service), consultation obligations

### 5. Employee Relations

Managing the relationship between employer and employees.

**Key elements:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Enterprise agreements** | Negotiated between employer and employees (or union) — set pay and conditions above minimum |
| **Awards** | Industry-specific minimum pay and conditions set by Fair Work Commission |
| **Unions** | Organisations representing workers' interests — negotiate pay, conditions, represent in disputes |
| **Dispute resolution** | Process for resolving workplace conflicts — mediation, Fair Work Commission, arbitration |

**Industrial action:**
- **Strike** — employees stop work to pressure employer
- **Lockout** — employer prevents employees from working
- Protected vs unprotected action under Fair Work Act

### 6. Workplace Diversity

**Dimensions of diversity:**
- Gender
- Cultural/ethnic background
- Age
- Disability
- Sexual orientation
- Religion
- Socioeconomic background

**Benefits of diversity:**
- Wider talent pool
- Better decision-making (diverse perspectives)
- Improved creativity and innovation
- Better understanding of diverse customer base
- Enhanced employer reputation

**Challenges:**
- Communication barriers
- Potential for discrimination/bias
- Managing different work styles
- Training costs for cultural awareness
- Resistance to change

### 7. Flexible Work Practices

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Part-time** | Fewer hours than full-time |
| **Job sharing** | Two employees share one full-time role |
| **Telecommuting/Remote work** | Working from home or off-site |
| **Flextime** | Flexible start/finish times (core hours required) |
| **Compressed work week** | Full hours in fewer days (e.g. 4 × 10hr days) |
| **Term-time only** | Working during school terms only |

**Benefits:**
- Improved work-life balance → higher morale and retention
- Access to wider talent pool (parents, people with disabilities)
- Reduced absenteeism
- Lower office costs

**Challenges:**
- Coordination difficulties
- Reduced team cohesion
- Harder to monitor performance
- Not suitable for all roles

## Motivation Theories

### Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

```
        Self-Actualisation
       (reach full potential)
      ────────────────────
       Esteem Needs
     (recognition, status)
    ────────────────────
     Social/Belonging Needs
      (teamwork, friendship)
     ────────────────────
      Safety Needs
   (job security, safe workplace)
  ────────────────────
   Physiological Needs
  (salary, basic conditions)
```

**Application:** Businesses should ensure lower-level needs are met before expecting employees to be motivated by higher-level rewards. Pay and safety first, then belonging, then recognition, then growth opportunities.

### Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory

| Factor | Description | Examples |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Hygiene factors** | Cause dissatisfaction if absent, but don't motivate when present | Salary, working conditions, job security, company policy, supervision |
| **Motivators** | Actually motivate and satisfy employees | Achievement, recognition, responsibility, advancement, personal growth |

**Application:** Fix hygiene factors to prevent dissatisfaction, then use motivators to actually engage and motivate employees.

### McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y

| Theory X (authoritarian) | Theory Y (participative) |
|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Workers are lazy and avoid work | Workers are self-motivated and enjoy work |
| Need close supervision and control | Need autonomy and responsibility |
| Motivated by punishment/rewards | Motivated by intrinsic factors |
| Management style: command and control | Management style: empowerment and trust |

## Organisational Structures

| Structure | Description | Pros | Cons |
|-----------|-------------|------|------|
| **Flat** | Few management layers, wide span of control | Faster communication, empowerment | Manager overload, unclear reporting |
| **Tall** | Many management layers, narrow span of control | Clear career path, close supervision | Slow communication, bureaucracy |
| **Centralised** | Decision-making concentrated at top | Consistency, control | Slow response, disempowerment |
| **Decentralised** | Decision-making pushed to lower levels | Faster response, empowerment | Inconsistency, coordination challenges |
| **Functional** | Grouped by function (marketing, finance, etc.) | Specialisation, efficiency | Silos, poor cross-functional communication |
| **Matrix** | Dual reporting (function + project) | Flexible, cross-functional collaboration | Conflicting priorities, complex reporting |

## HSC Response Framework for HR

### HR Strategy Answer Structure
1. **Define** the strategy — "Recruitment is the process of attracting and selecting suitable candidates..."
2. **Explain** how it works — describe the steps or methods
3. **Apply** to the business — link to specific case study
4. **Evaluate** effectiveness — discuss pros/cons, impact on business performance

### Recommendation Structure
1. **Recommend** — "It is recommended that [business] implement [strategy]"
2. **Define** — explain what the strategy involves
3. **Reason 1** — how it addresses the identified problem
4. **Reason 2** — additional benefit
5. **Link** — restate recommendation with justification
