# Marketing Process — Objectives, Target Markets, SWOT, Market Research & Monitoring

> **HSC Business Studies — Marketing** | Marketing objectives, identifying target markets, SWOT analysis, product life cycle, market research methods, implementing/monitoring/controlling the marketing plan, and financial forecasts.

---

## 1. Marketing Objectives

Marketing objectives are **realistic and measurable goals** to be achieved through the marketing plan. Should be closely aligned to overall business goals but more customer-oriented.

### Three Common Marketing Objectives

| Objective | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Increasing Market Share** | Gaining more customers or a larger portion of the market |
| **Expanding the Product Mix** | Offering a wider variety of products to attract more customers |
| **Maximising Customer Service** | Ensuring positive customer experience and continuous improvement |

### Market Share Formula

**Market Share (%) = (Company Sales ÷ Total Industry Sales) x 100**

- Used to evaluate marketing strategies compared to competitors
- A business can have increasing sales revenue but decreasing market share if competitors are growing faster
- Example: If Apollo Sales = $12M and Industry Sales = $89M → Market Share = 12/89 × 100 = 13.5%

## 2. Identifying Target Markets

**Target market** = a group of present and potential customers to which a business intends to sell.

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Primary Target Market** | Most resources directed here |
| **Secondary Target Market** | Smaller, less important segment |

### Why Identify Target Markets?
- Identify appropriate **promotional strategies** to inform potential customers
- Improve ability to **increase sales**
- Ensure products **meet customer needs and expectations** (product, price, distribution)

---

## 3. Steps in Developing a Marketing Plan

1. **Situational Analysis** — assess current market position (SWOT, PLC)
2. **Market Research** — gather data about customers and market
3. **Establish Market Objectives** — set measurable goals
4. **Identify Target Markets** — define primary and secondary markets
5. **Develop Marketing Strategies** — create the marketing mix (4Ps)
6. **Implement, Monitor and Control** — execute and adjust

## 4. SWOT Analysis

| Category | Internal/External | Description |
|----------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Strengths** | Internal | Positive aspects the business controls |
| **Weaknesses** | Internal | Negative aspects the business can improve |
| **Opportunities** | External | Positive external factors to exploit |
| **Threats** | External | Negative external factors to monitor |

> **Key distinction:** Strengths and Weaknesses are **internal** (controllable). Opportunities and Threats are **external** (uncontrollable).

### Why SWOT Matters
- Businesses must constantly monitor internal forces (strengths, weaknesses) and external forces (opportunities, threats)
- SWOT analysis affects marketing strategies — for example, a threat like increased competition impacts product, pricing, promotion and distribution strategies
- SWOT links directly to achieving marketing objectives (market share, increasing consumers, sales, customer service, product mix)

## 5. Product Life Cycle (PLC)

The **product life cycle** describes the stages a product goes through from when it was first thought of until it finally is removed from the market. Each stage has different implications for marketing strategies.

| Stage | Description | Marketing Strategy Implications |
|-------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| **Introduction** | Product is launched into the market; sales are low as consumers are unaware; heavy promotion needed to build awareness | High spending on promotion (informative advertising); limited distribution; skimming or penetration pricing; focus on building product awareness and trial |
| **Growth** | Sales increase rapidly as consumers adopt the product; competitors may enter the market | Increase distribution; shift promotion to persuasive advertising; may need to adjust price as competitors enter; focus on brand differentiation |
| **Maturity** | Sales growth slows and plateaus; market is saturated; heavy competition | Focus on product differentiation and loyalty programs; competitive pricing; promotional spending to maintain market share; may add product variations or features |
| **Decline** | Sales fall as market shrinks; product may become obsolete due to technology changes or shifting consumer preferences | Reduce promotional spending; may reduce distribution channels; consider product deletion or redevelopment; price reductions to clear remaining stock |

### Product Examples by PLC Stage

| Product | Stage | Reasoning |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| **CDs/DVDs** | Decline | Replaced by digital streaming |
| **Personal Computers** | Maturity | Established market, regular upgrades but no rapid growth |
| **Electric Vehicles** | Growth | Rapidly increasing adoption, new competitors entering |
| **Tablets** | Maturity | Market saturated, sales plateauing |
| **Driverless Cars** | Introduction | New technology, limited market awareness |

### How Marketing Strategies Change Through the PLC
- **Promotion:** shifts from informative (intro) → persuasive (growth) → reminder (maturity) → minimal (decline)
- **Price:** skimming (intro) → competitive (growth/maturity) → reduced (decline)
- **Product:** basic features (intro) → additions/variations (growth/maturity) → streamlined (decline)
- **Distribution:** selective (intro) → intensive (growth/maturity) → reduced (decline)

## 6. Market Research

### Types of Market Research

| Type | Description | Methods |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Primary Research** | New data collected specifically for the business | Surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation, experiments |
| **Secondary Research** | Existing data collected for other purposes | Government statistics, industry reports, newspapers, internet |

### Market Research Process
1. **Define the problem** — what do we need to know?
2. **Collect data** — primary and/or secondary
3. **Analyse data** — identify patterns and insights
4. **Make recommendations** — based on findings
5. **Implement and monitor** — act on recommendations

### Apple Case Study — Market Research
- Conducts regular surveys to assess customer responses to applications and design features
- Customer feedback led to changes in iPhone design (larger screen, thinner phone, improved camera)
- In 2014, Apple released larger screen iPhones in response to consumer demand

## 7. Implementing the Marketing Plan

- **Implementation** is the process of putting marketing strategies into operation
- The implementation stage covers the **how, where and when** it is to be done
- Implementation is as important as developing marketing objectives — even the best plan fails without proper execution

## 8. Monitoring and Controlling the Marketing Plan

### Monitoring
- **Monitoring** means checking and observing the actual progress of the marketing plan
- Information collected during monitoring is used to control the plan

### Controlling
- **Controlling** involves comparing planned performance against actual performance
- Takes corrective action to make sure objectives are attained

### Developing Financial Forecasts

Financial forecasts detail the **costs and revenues** for each strategy. By measuring sales potential and revenue forecasts and comparing these with anticipated expenditures, the business can decide the best way to allocate resources.

**Requires:**
1. **Cost estimate** — projected expenses for marketing activities
2. **Revenue estimate** — projected income from sales

> It is easier for a business to forecast marketing costs than revenue because costs are based on known expenses, while revenue depends on unpredictable market conditions and consumer behaviour.

### Comparing Actual to Planned Results

Three key performance indicators used to measure success:

| KPI | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| **Sales Analysis** | Uses sales data to evaluate a business's current performance and the effectiveness of a marketing strategy |
| **Market Share Analysis** | Evaluates marketing strategies compared to competitors — a business may have increasing sales but decreasing market share if competitors grow faster |
| **Marketing Profitability Analysis** | Breaks down total marketing costs into specific marketing activities to determine which are most profitable |

### Revising the Marketing Strategy

Once analysis results are calculated, a business can assess whether objectives have been met or if changes are needed:

1. **Changes to marketing mix** — modification to the 4Ps (product, price, promotion, place)
2. **New product development** — creating new products to meet changing demand
3. **Product deletion** — removing products that are no longer profitable or relevant

## 9. HSC Exam Response Structures

### Explain Scaffold (for "Explain" questions)
- **A. Define** — What is it?
- **B. Relationship** — How does the issue impact the business?
- **C. Implications** — What does this lead to?
- **D. Link** — What does this mean for the business?

### Analyse Scaffold (for "Analyse" questions)
- **Cause/effect #1** — How is the business impacted? Why?
- **Cause/effect #2** — How is the business impacted? Why? Link to cause #1
- **Implications** — What does this mean for the business long-term?

### Key Verb Meanings

| Verb | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Identify** | Name/State |
| **Outline** | Provide main features |
| **Describe** | Give characteristics |
| **Explain** | Show cause and effect |
| **Analyse** | Break down and examine, show relationship between and draw out implications |
| **Assess** | Make a judgement |
| **Evaluate** | Weigh up and recommend |
