# Task 3 Draft Essay — Evaluate McDonald's Globalisation Through Marketing Strategies

Globalisation has effectively and significantly shaped McDonald's marketing strategies, allowing the corporation to expand its operations across over 40,000 restaurants in more than 100 countries while generating $25.92 billion in global revenue (2024). McDonald's has responded to globalisation by adapting its product range, refining promotional campaigns, and leveraging e-marketing platforms to maintain a competitive position in diverse international markets.

---

**Product Strategy**

Globalisation has required McDonald's to balance standardisation with customisation to meet varying consumer preferences across markets. The corporation maintains a standardised core of approximately 15 global products, including the Big Mac and Chicken McNuggets, which account for roughly 70% of total sales and ensure brand consistency worldwide. However, McDonald's also customises its menu by introducing region-specific items to align with local tastes and cultural norms. For example, the McOz burger caters to Australian consumers, the McAloo Tikki addresses vegetarian preferences in India, and the Teriyaki Burger reflects Japanese flavour profiles. This dual approach demonstrates how globalisation forces multinational corporations to adapt products without diluting their global brand identity. Furthermore, McDonald's has responded to sociocultural influences by removing artificial preservatives and committing to cage-free eggs by 2025, reflecting growing consumer demand for healthier and ethically sourced food. Therefore, product customisation has been central to McDonald's successful globalisation, enabling the corporation to remain relevant across culturally diverse markets while maintaining operational efficiency through standardised supply chain processes.

---

**Promotion Strategy**

Globalisation has also influenced McDonald's promotional strategies, requiring the corporation to communicate its brand message across multiple cultural and linguistic contexts. McDonald's invests approximately $4.5 billion annually in systemwide advertising, making it one of the largest global advertisers. A key example is the FIFA World Cup 2022 campaign, which was McDonald's largest global promotional initiative and resulted in a double-digit increase in delivery sales across participating markets. In addition, McDonald's engages in co-branding partnerships such as the BTS Meal, which was launched simultaneously across over 50 countries in 2021 and leveraged the global popularity of the K-pop group to attract younger demographics. These campaigns demonstrate McDonald's ability to execute large-scale global promotions while maintaining local relevance. Furthermore, McDonald's adapts promotional messaging to align with local languages and cultural contexts, ensuring advertisements resonate with specific target markets. The corporation also invests heavily in social responsibility promotion through Ronald McDonald House Charities, which strengthens brand image and consumer trust across international markets. Therefore, McDonald's promotional strategies demonstrate how globalisation enables multinational corporations to leverage economies of scale in advertising while customising messaging to diverse consumer segments.

---

**E-Marketing Strategy**

Globalisation has accelerated McDonald's adoption of e-marketing platforms to engage consumers across borders and gather valuable market research data. The MyMacca's loyalty program has attracted over 150 million active members globally, with a target of 250 million by 2027, demonstrating the effectiveness of digital platforms in driving customer retention across international markets. The mobile application provides personalised offers, mobile ordering, and delivery tracking, which enhance customer convenience and increase transaction frequency. Additionally, digital ordering kiosks have been installed in over 90% of Australian stores and increase average order value by 15–20% through suggestive selling algorithms. These technological investments reflect how globalisation has enabled McDonald's to standardise digital processes across markets while collecting consumer data to inform localised marketing decisions. Furthermore, McDonald's social media presence, with over 80 million global followers, allows the corporation to engage with consumers in real time and promote products across multiple international platforms simultaneously. Therefore, e-marketing has been instrumental in McDonald's globalisation strategy, enabling the corporation to achieve operational efficiency, strengthen customer relationships, and maintain competitive advantage through technology-driven marketing.

---

In conclusion, globalisation has profoundly influenced McDonald's marketing strategies across product, promotion, and e-marketing. The corporation's ability to balance global standardisation with local customisation, execute large-scale international promotional campaigns, and invest in digital marketing platforms demonstrates effective management responses to the opportunities and challenges presented by globalisation. Consequently, McDonald's maintains its position as the world's leading quick-service restaurant chain, serving 69 million customers daily across more than 100 countries.
