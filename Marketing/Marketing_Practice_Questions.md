## 1. Past HSC Section IV Questions

### 2019 HSC — Question 26 (20 marks)
**"Analyse the relationship between market segmentation and marketing strategies."**

**Response Structure:**
1. **Define** market segmentation and marketing strategies
2. **Explain** how segmentation informs strategy development
3. **Analyse** the relationship — how each type of segmentation (demographic, geographic, psychographic, behavioural) leads to different strategies
4. **Use examples** to illustrate the relationship
5. **Conclusion** — summarise the interdependence

### 2025 Past HSC — Extended Response
**"Explain how target market impacts on people, processes and physical evidence strategies."**

**PEEL Structure for each body paragraph:**

**P — Point:**
- Processes/People/Physical evidence are influenced by the target market because...
- A business designs its strategies to meet the needs of its target market

**E — Evidence:**
- Apple uses processes such as online ordering, pre-orders, roaming payment systems, free iOS upgrades
- Apple's people strategies: "Genius" and "Specialist" job titles, strong sales/customer service training
- Apple's physical evidence: store design reflecting innovation, hands-on testing, prime city locations

**E — Explanation:**
- These strategies improve customer satisfaction by...
- Apple customers expect convenience, speed, and innovation

**L — Link:**
- Therefore, Apple's target market strongly influences its strategies
- Allows Apple to maintain customer satisfaction, loyalty, and competitive advantage

### 2021 HSC — Section IV
**"Analyse how situational analysis (SWOT, product life cycle) may affect marketing strategies."**

**Structure:**
- Introduction: Define situational analysis, state specific parts and strategies covered
- Body 1: ONE part of SWOT (e.g., Threats) → impact on ONE strategy (e.g., Product)
- Body 2: Another SWOT element → another strategy
- Body 3: Product Life Cycle → how ONE strategy changes through PLC stages
- To get 12+ marks: must have at least ONE paragraph on SWOT AND ONE on PLC

**Example body paragraph (Threats → Product):**
Apple has threats such as competition from low-cost Asian providers (Huawei, Xiaomi). Threats are external factors the business cannot control. This has impacted Apple's product strategy — they position as high-quality/luxury with recognisable branding (bitten apple logo) and minimalist white/silver packaging. However, focus on camera improvements has raised questions about innovation, affecting market share (17% vs Samsung's 20% in Q2 2023, though improved to 23% vs 16% by Q4 2024).

**Key exam tips:**
- Use relational verbs (therefore, as a result, consequently, this means)
- Always link back to marketing objectives (market share, sales, customer service)
- Use specific data/examples to support claims
1. **Define** ethical behaviour and government regulations in marketing
2. **Explain** importance of ethics — consumer trust, brand reputation, long-term sustainability
3. **Explain** importance of regulations — CCA 2010, ACCC enforcement, consumer protection
4. **Analyse** consequences of unethical behaviour and non-compliance

## 4. Extended Response Scaffolds & Worked Examples

### 2021 HSC — "Analyse how a situational analysis (SWOT, product life cycle) may affect marketing strategies"

**Analyse** = show relationship between and draw out implications

**To get 12+ marks**: At least ONE paragraph on SWOT AND one on Product Life Cycle

**Structure:**
- **SWOT**: 2 body paragraphs — choose ONE part of SWOT, do at least 2 strengths/2 weaknesses/2 threats/2 opportunities
- **Product Life Cycle**: 1 body paragraph — show how ONE marketing strategy changes through the PLC

**Body Paragraph Example (SWOT — Threats & Product Strategy):**
Apple has numerous threats such as competition in its smartphone market which impacts their Product strategy. Threats are external factors that could impact the business with no control over them. One major threat is increased competition from low-cost Asian providers such as Huawei and Xiaomi, which has impacted their product strategy. The product strategy includes branding, packaging, and after-sales services. To compete, Apple has positioned its product as a high-quality, luxury smartphone — branding on packaging is easily recognisable (the apple with a bite out of it) and linked to reputation of producing high-quality, innovative products. However, there have been issues with whether they've been innovative over the last few years (focus on camera improvements). This impacted market share — lower than Samsung from Q2 2023 (17% vs 20%) until Q4 2024 when it improved (23% vs 16%). Packaging uses minimalist colours (white, silver logos) which is prominent and high-quality/luxurious, meeting customer expectations. Therefore, by using their product strategy of branding and packaging, Apple is able to maintain/increase customers, minimise the threat of competitors, and improve market share.

### Answering an "Assess" Question — Promotional Mix

**Question**: "Assess why a promotional mix of promotion strategies is important in the marketing of goods and services."

**Assess** = make a judgement of value and importance — how effective is the promotional mix in persuading and informing potential and existing customers.

**Structure:**
1. **Introduction**: Define promotional mix, introduce case studies (e.g., Hyundai, Apple, Woolworths)
2. **Evidence 1**: One promotional strategy effective for marketing
3. **Evidence 2**: Another promotional strategy effective for marketing
4. **Evidence 3**: Another promotional strategy effective for marketing
5. **Conclusion**: Restate judgement, summary sentence for each body paragraph, link to overall business goal

**Practice Question**: "Assess why pricing and product strategies are important in marketing goods and services."
- 3 body paragraphs — at least ONE on Product strategies and ONE on Price strategies

## 5. Apple Case Study — Key Points

### Marketing Approaches
- **Sales approach (early iPhone):** Innovative product; educated customers on features
- **Marketing approach (current):** Persuasive marketing backed by research; surveys assess responses; customer feedback drives design changes

### Market Segmentation
- **Demographic:** Young to middle-aged, middle to high income
- **Psychographic:** Tech-savvy, design-conscious, status-seeking
- **Behavioural:** Brand loyal, early adopters, ecosystem users

### Positioning
- Premium, innovative brand
- Sleek design, exclusive retail experience
- Ecosystem integration as differentiation

## 5. Key Exam Tips

1. **Always link to a case study** — don't just describe theory
2. **Use specific examples** — products, campaigns, figures
3. **Know your verbs** — identify (1-2 marks), explain (3-4 marks), evaluate (7-8 marks)
4. **Show interdependence** — connect marketing to other functions
5. **Practice time management** — know how long to spend per mark
