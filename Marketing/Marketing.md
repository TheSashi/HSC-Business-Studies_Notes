# Marketing — Complete Knowledge Base

## 1. Role of Marketing

### 1.1 Definition

> **Marketing** is the total system of interacting activities designed to plan, promote, price and distribute products to present and potential customers.

### 1.2 Strategic Role of Marketing

- A common financial business goal is **profit maximisation**
- The **strategic role of marketing** is to translate this financial goal into reality
- A business develops and implements a **marketing plan** that sets out a series of actions and strategies to attain greater sales and achieve financial goals
- Marketing today places a strong emphasis on viewing the business through the **customer's eyes** — customer-oriented marketing
- Marketing enables a business to:
  - Identify customer needs and wants
  - Develop products that satisfy those needs
  - Communicate value to customers
  - Generate revenue to support the entire business

### 1.3 Marketing Plan

The **marketing plan** is where the business tries to apply marketing strategies based on research they've done on the market. It includes:
- Knowing what they need to sell
- Understanding advantages, disadvantages, opportunities and threats
- Analysing customers, their needs, demands, and demographics
- Understanding where and when to sell for optimal revenue maximisation

### 1.4 Interdependence with Other Business Functions

Marketing is deeply interconnected with every other business function:

#### Marketing & Operations
- Marketing provides **demand forecasts** and customer preference data to operations
- Operations produces the goods that marketing then sells
- Marketing research identifies product features customers want → operations modifies production accordingly
- Example: BYD — operations produces the "Blade Battery", marketing uses this technology to promote vehicle safety and reliability. Marketing provides customer feedback on price and features to operations, which modifies cars to meet those needs.

#### Marketing & Finance
- Finance provides **budgets and funds** for marketing activities
- Marketing generates **revenue** which flows to finance
- Marketing works on analysing and selling products that meet customer needs, generating revenue allowing finance to pay business expenses

#### Marketing & Human Resources
- HR recruits and trains **qualified employees** for marketing
- Marketing needs competent staff to sell products effectively
- If employees aren't trained correctly, they could assess markets wrongly, leading to lower conversion rates and lower company profits

## 2. Marketing Approaches

### 2.1 Production Approach (Production Concept)
- Relies on the view that consumers base purchasing on the **quality of the product**
- Marketing consisted of simply **taking orders and delivering products**
- Assumes consumers will favour products that are available and highly affordable
- Focus is on production efficiency and distribution

### 2.2 Sales Approach (Selling Concept)
- Emphasised selling because of **increased competition**
- To stimulate demand, businesses increased spending on **advertising**
- Assumes consumers will not buy enough unless aggressive promotion is used
- Focus is on short-term sales rather than building relationships
- Example: Apple iPhone in early stages — highly innovative, beyond consumer expectations, so marketing strategies were aimed at **educating customers** on features

### 2.3 Marketing Approach (Marketing Concept)
- Focuses on **finding what the customer wants** (through market research) and then satisfying that need
- Involves adopting a **customer orientation** — all actions should aim at satisfying customer needs
- Marketing must be:
  - **Customer oriented**
  - Supported by **integrated marketing strategies**
  - Aimed at **satisfying customers**
  - **Integrated into the business plan** to achieve business goals
- Shift from informative to **persuasive** marketing as consumers develop expectations

### 2.4 Societal Marketing Concept
- Extends the marketing concept to consider **society's long-term interests**
- Balances company profits, consumer wants, and societal well-being
- Considers environmental and social impact of marketing activities

### 2.5 Customer Orientation

- For businesses adopting customer orientation, the **customer relationship does not end with the sale — it begins there**
- Businesses look for ways to:
  - Improve **after-sale service**
  - Fulfil the needs and wants of customers
- Examples:
  - Car sellers offering **free car servicing**
  - Electrical goods offering **longer warranty periods** and free replacement if faulty

### 2.6 Relationship Marketing
- Ensures **existing customers are satisfied**
- Core goal is **customer loyalty** to generate repeat sales
- Achieved through:
  - **Reward programs** (e.g., loyalty cards)
  - **Customer care**
  - Good **after-sales service**

### 2.7 Types of Markets

A **market** is defined as a group of individuals, organisations or both that:
- Need or want a product (goods/services)
- Have the money (**purchasing power**) to purchase the product
- Are **willing to spend** their money to obtain the product
- Are **socially and legally authorised** to purchase the product

| Market Type | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Resource Market** | Individuals/groups in primary production (mining, agriculture, forestry, fishing) | Farmers, miners |
| **Industrial Market** | Industries/businesses purchasing products to use in production of other products or daily operations | Manufacturers |
| **Intermediate Market** | Wholesalers and retailers who purchase finished products and resell for profit | Distributors, retailers |
| **Consumer Markets** | Individuals who plan to use or consume the products they buy | End consumers |

#### Consumer Market Subdivisions:
- **Mass Markets**: goods/services that appeal to all types of consumers (e.g., basic food items, electricity)
- **Market Segments**: business focuses on only one area of a particular market (e.g., sports car sellers targeting only a particular part of the market)
- **Niche Market**: a smaller section of a market segment (e.g., magazines for teenage girls, outdoor lifestyle)

### 2.8 Marketing Approaches — Apple Case Study

- **Sales approach**: The innovative nature of the Apple iPhone creates a sales approach (introductory stages of product life cycle) because companies like Apple were developing new products with capacities beyond consumer expectations. Marketing strategies were aimed at **educating customers** on features of earlier iPhones.

- **Marketing approach**: Focus of marketing iPhones has become more **persuasive rather than informative**. Customers have developed expectations regarding capacity of current and future designs, leading to greater focus on market research. Customer feedback led to changes in iPhone design such as:
  - Larger screen size
  - Thinner phone
  - Improved camera specification
  - In 2014, Apple released larger screen sized iPhones in response to consumer demand

## 3. Influences on Marketing

### 3.1 Factors Influencing Customer Choice

Marketers closely examine the behaviour of customers to understand what motivates an individual to purchase a particular product. Market researchers ask:
- Who are our customers?
- What do they buy?
- When do they buy?
- How often do they buy?
- Why do they buy?

#### 3.1.1 Psychological Influences
Influences **within an individual** that affect buying behaviour:

| Factor | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Perception** | The perspective of how something is viewed — how consumers interpret marketing messages |
| **Motives** | Internal drives that encourage a person to purchase; marketers must understand what motivates consumers |
| **Attitudes** | A person's learned predisposition to respond favourably or unfavourably to a product |
| **Personality** | Unique psychological characteristics that distinguish a person |
| **Self-image** | How consumers see themselves; they buy products that match their self-concept |
| **Learning** | Changes in behaviour arising from experience |

**Apple example**: In 2013, Apple launched iPhone 5c (5 colours — green, blue, yellow, pink, white). Sales were low because the psychological influence of **quality expectations** was stronger than the love of colours. The 5s (3 metallic colours — silver, gold, grey) was associated with quality and refinement, targeting consumers with preferences for quality and innovation.

#### 3.1.2 Sociocultural Influences
Forces exerted by **other people and groups** that affect customer behaviour:

| Factor | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Social class** | Grouping of people with similar social standing based on income, occupation, education |
| **Culture and subculture** | Shared values, beliefs and customs of a group; multicultural considerations |
| **Family and roles** | Family members influence buying decisions based on their roles |
| **Reference (peer) groups** | Groups an individual identifies with and is influenced by |

**Apple example**: The exploding popularity of social media, online gaming and video streaming led to strong focus on design features supporting these capabilities. Marketing of iPhones heavily promotes camera, screen and processing specifications to differentiate from competitors.

#### 3.1.3 Economic Influences
Enormous impact on both businesses and customers — influence a business's capacity to compete and a customer's willingness and ability to spend.

| Economic Condition | Impact on Marketing |
|---|---|
| **Boom** | Low unemployment, rising income, optimism. Businesses expand production, consumer spending increases. Marketing can emphasise premium products |
| **Recession** | High unemployment, falling income, lack of confidence. Consumer spending decreases, customers seek functional, long-lasting products. Marketing plans should **stress value and usefulness** |

**Apple example**: The GFC led to declining growth of iPhone sales in Europe and USA. Apple developed the 'low-cost' iPhone 5c and continued selling older models. In April 2020, the release of an updated iPhone SE proved timely during the COVID-19 crisis. Apple also released a low-cost "Mini" version in 2020-2021 to target price-conscious customers.

#### 3.1.4 Government Influences

**Government Policies** (indirect influence):
- **Fiscal Policy**: Government budget showing revenue sources (taxes) and spending. Expands or contracts economic activity.
- **Monetary Policy**: Government through the Reserve Bank uses interest rates to control money supply and inflation. Changes in cash rate encourage/discourage consumer spending.
- Examples: Introduction of the GST, increases in income tax

**Government Regulations** (direct and immediate impact):
- Breaches may result in **financial penalties**
- Regulatory forces consist of laws and regulatory bodies
- Examples:
  - **Competition and Consumer Act 2010** (Cwlth)
  - **Fair Trading Act 1987** (NSW)

**Apple example**: In 2014, iPhone 6 and 6 Plus launch was delayed for Chinese customers due to government concerns about privacy and third-party accessibility (iCloud). India's regulations made expansion difficult, but eased in recent years — Apple opened an online store in 2020. India launched a $6.6 billion incentive program to boost local smartphone manufacturing.

### 3.2 Ethical Influences of Marketing

#### 3.2.1 Truth and Accuracy in Advertising

Main unethical marketing practices:

| Practice | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Untruths due to concealed facts** | Information purposefully omitted from advertisements that could harm customer trust |
| **Exaggerated Claims (Puffery)** | Exaggerated praise or flattery for promotional purposes that no reasonable person would take as factual |
| **Vague Statements** | Statements using ambiguous words so consumers assume the advertiser's intended message |
| **Invasion of Privacy** | Tracking web users and using information to target advertisements; consumers can opt out |

#### 3.2.2 Good Taste in Advertising
- Highly **subjective** — some consumers may find an ad offensive while others don't
- Depends on consumers' **values, tastes and preferences**
- There is usually common agreement on what society considers acceptable
- Marketers must be aware of **community sensitivities**

#### 3.2.3 Products That May Damage Health
Self-regulatory advertising codes include:
- **Responsible Children's Marketing Initiative**
- **Quick Service Restaurant Code**: allows fast food companies to advertise certain products in children's programs only if they meet specific nutritional guidelines
- Codes on **alcohol and smoking** advertisements
- Federal government sets restrictions: no advertising during programs for preschool children

#### 3.2.4 Engaging in Fair Competition
Due to competition, some businesses engage in unfair marketing strategies resulting in consumer exploitation:
- Making **false promises**
- Highly **exaggerated, incomplete** product descriptions
- **Manipulative, high-pressure** selling methods

#### 3.2.5 SUGGING — Selling Under the Guise of a Survey
- A sales technique **disguised as market research**
- Although not illegal, raises ethical issues including **invasion of privacy** and **deception**
- Users/customers believe they are filling in a survey when the company is actually trying to sell them products

#### 3.2.6 Ethical Issues — Apple Case Study
Apple has faced major ethical issues including:
- Labour practices in manufacturing (Foxconn factories)
- Environmental concerns with product disposal and manufacturing
- Planned obsolescence of products
- Consumer privacy and data collection

### 3.3 Consumer Law (Legal Influences)

#### 3.3.1 Competition and Consumer Act 2010

Two major purposes:
1. **Protect consumers** against undesirable practices such as misrepresenting products, place of production, and misleading/deceptive advertising
2. **Regulate trade practices** that restrict competition

Enforced by: **ACCC** (Australian Competition and Consumer Commission) and **ASIC** (Australian Securities and Investment Commission)

#### 3.3.2 Deceptive and Misleading Conduct

| Tactic | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Fine print** | Important conditions written in small print, difficult to read |
| **Before and after advertisements** | Misleading comparisons |
| **Tests and surveys** | Unsubstantiated claims |
| **Country of origin** | False claims about where product is made |
| **Packaging** | Size/shape gives misleading impression of contents |
| **Special offers** | Advertising limited-time offers when they're continuously available |

Common misleading and deceptive techniques:
- **Bait and Switch Advertising**: advertising products at reduced prices, and when they run out, directing customers to higher-price items
- **Dishonest advertising**: using deceptive words or claiming product has qualities it doesn't (price reductions, specials, free gift offers must be genuine)

#### 3.3.3 Price Discrimination
- Setting of prices for a product in **separate markets**
- Price difference possible because markets are geographically spread or there is product differentiation
- The CCA **prohibits** price discrimination if it could substantially reduce competition
- Applies to discounts, credits, rebates, services, and payment arrangements

#### 3.3.4 Implied Conditions
- **Unspoken and unwritten** terms of a contract that exist regardless of whether written into the contract
- Include:
  - **Fitness for particular purpose**
  - Illegal to suggest a product has characteristics it does not have

#### 3.3.5 Warranties
- A promise by the business to **repair and replace** faulty products
- Ensures customers the business has confidence in product quality
- Can be used as an **aggressive marketing tool** if it includes superior options to competitors

#### 3.3.6 Refunds and Exchanges
A business is **required by law** to offer a refund:
- If products are **faulty**
- If they **don't match the description**
- If they **fail to do the job** they were supposed to do

A business is **NOT obliged** to give a refund if:
- Customer changes their mind
- Finds a cheaper product elsewhere
- Product is damaged after purchase

Accurate signs regarding refunds and exchanges must be displayed.

#### 3.3.7 ACCC Case Studies

##### Apple — ACCC Enforcement
- **iPhone and iPad misrepresentations** cost Apple Inc. $9 million in penalties
- **Error 53 case**: Apple caught misleading customers about repair rights (court documents alleged sting operation)
- **Consumer guarantee representations**: ACCC took action against Apple over alleged misleading representations about consumers' rights to repairs and replacements
- Apple gave undertaking to ACCC to **improve consumer guarantees policies and practices**

##### Woolworths — ACCC Enforcement
- ACCC took Woolworths to court over **grocery pricing and discount practices**
- Concerns about misleading pricing claims on products

## 4. SWOT Analysis

### 4.1 Situational Analysis
- The **most crucial step** of the marketing plan is management having a precise understanding of the business's current position and a clear picture of where they are heading
- Must be undertaken based on **meaningful market research**

### 4.2 SWOT Framework

| | **Internal** | **External** |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Strengths | Opportunities |
| **Negative** | Weaknesses | Threats |

- **Internal forces**: operate from inside the organisation, largely within its control
- **External forces**: changes in the external environment can dramatically alter the course of the business; should be **constantly monitored**

### 4.3 SWOT — Apple Case Study

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| **Strength** | Strong brand reputation, attracting customers globally |
| **Weakness** | High price, possibly reducing competitiveness in the market |
| **Opportunity** | Partnership with AI, satisfying customer needs as AI becomes more commonly used |
| **Threat** | Increased competition from Asian brands such as Xiaomi and OPPO |

### 4.4 Product Life Cycle

The product life cycle consists of the stages a product passes through:

| Stage | Sales | Profits | Competition | Marketing Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | Low, building slowly | Low/negative (high startup costs) | Little or none | Build awareness, educate consumers, heavy promotion spending |
| **2. Growth** | Rapidly increasing | Increasing | Competitors enter market | Differentiate brand, prove why your brand is best |
| **3. Maturity** | Peak, then levels off | Stable/declining | Intense, market saturated | Defend market share, stress value |
| **4. Decline** | Falling | Declining | Reducing as competitors exit | Reduce spending, consider product deletion |

**Examples of product life cycle stages:**
- CD — **Decline**
- Personal computers — **Maturity**
- Electric vehicles — **Growth**
- Tablet devices — **Maturity**
- Driverless cars — **Introduction**

### 4.5 Steps in Developing a Marketing Plan

1. **Situational Analysis** (SWOT)
2. **Market Research**
3. **Establish market objectives**
4. **Identify target markets**
5. **Develop marketing strategies**
6. **Implementation, monitoring and controlling**

## 5. Market Research

### 5.1 Definition
**Market research** is the process of systematically **collecting, recording and analysing** information concerning a specific marketing problem.

### 5.2 Steps of the Market Research Process

1. **Determining Information Needs**
2. **Collecting data** from primary and secondary sources
3. **Analysing and interpreting data**

### 5.3 Primary vs Secondary Data

| Feature | Primary Data | Secondary Data |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Data gathered by the business itself | Data from other people's primary research |
| **Collection methods** | Surveys, observations, focus groups, experiments | Internet research, government statistics, industry reports, Census data |
| **Advantages** | Specific to business needs, current, controlled | Cheaper, readily available, provides broader context |
| **Disadvantages** | Expensive, time-consuming to collect | May not be specific enough, could be outdated |

### 5.4 Information Gathered from Market Research

- **Needs and wants** of consumers (preferences)
- **Income** of target market and what consumers are willing and able to afford → assists with **determining price**
- **Feedback** on improvements → modifications to products and customer service
- **Competitor analysis**: prices, unique features, after-sales service → assists with **product differentiation**
- **Secondary data**: population characteristics from Census → information about existing and potential customers

### 5.5 Market Research — Apple Case Study

- **Primary research**: Apple emails feedback surveys to customers following a purchase. Customers rate satisfaction level and likelihood of purchasing again.
- **Monthly surveys**: Apple surveys iPhone buyers country-by-country to reveal:
  - What drives customers to buy Apple vs other products (e.g., Samsung Android)
  - Features customers most use
  - Customer demographics
  - Level of satisfaction with different aspects of iPhone
- **Secondary research**: Sales data and market share updates provide valuable insights
- Market research helps Apple to:
  - Constantly improve products
  - Increase competitiveness
  - Differentiate products from brands like Samsung
  - Invest extra cash back into product development, forming a positive cycle

## 6. Marketing Objectives

### 6.1 Definition
**Marketing objectives** are the **realistic and measurable** goals to be achieved through the marketing plan. They should be:
- Closely aligned to overall **business goals**
- More **customer oriented** than general business objectives

### 6.2 Three Common Marketing Objectives

| Objective | Definition | Strategies |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Increasing market share** | Gaining a larger proportion of total industry sales | Promotional campaigns, competitive pricing, product differentiation |
| **2. Expanding the product mix** | Offering a wider variety of products to attract more customers | New product development, product line extensions |
| **3. Maximising customer service** | Ensuring customers have a positive experience and reviewing how to improve | Customer feedback forms, after-sales service, training staff |

### 6.3 Identifying Target Markets

- A **target market** is a group of present and potential customers to which a business intends to sell their product
- **Primary target market**: most resources directed at this market
- **Secondary target market**: usually a smaller and less important market segment

**Why is identifying target markets important?**
- To identify appropriate **promotional strategies** to inform potential customers
- To improve the business's ability to **increase sales** to potential customers
- To identify potential customers first, then ensure they are meeting those customers' needs and expectations (product, price, distribution)

### 6.4 Market Segmentation

| Segment Type | Basis | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Geographic** | Where customers live (location, region, climate, urban/rural) | McDonald's offering different menus in different countries |
| **Demographic** | Age, gender, income, occupation, education, family size, ethnicity | Apple targeting young to middle-aged, middle to high-income individuals |
| **Psychographic** | Lifestyle, values, attitudes, personality, interests | Apple targeting consumers who value innovation, quality, and design |
| **Behavioural** | Buying patterns, brand loyalty, usage rate, benefits sought | Apple's ecosystem loyalty, reward programs |

### 6.5 Target Markets — Apple Case Study

**Demographics:**
- **Age**: Younger to middle-aged consumers
- **Income**: Middle to high-income brackets, willingness to spend on premium products
- **Occupation**: Professionals, managers, executives, students who value technology
- **Geographic**: Strong presence in Americas, Europe, and Greater China

**Psychographics:**
- **Values**: Innovation, quality, design, seamless user experience
- **Lifestyle**: Early adopters of technology, appreciate prestige of Apple products
- **Brand loyalty**: Strong loyalty among customers, drawn to Apple ecosystem

**Product Segment Targets:**
- **iPhone**: Broad audience — young adults, professionals, tech enthusiasts
- **iPad**: Creative professionals, students, individuals seeking tablet experience
- **Mac**: Creative professionals, developers, users valuing powerful desktop computing
- **Apple Watch**: Fitness enthusiasts, young adults, professionals valuing health tracking

## 7. Marketing Mix — The 4Ps

The marketing mix refers to the combination of four key elements:

### 7.1 Product

Involves decisions about:
- Which product to make
- **Quality** of the product
- **Packaging and labelling**
- **Design**
- **Brand name**
- **Guarantee**

Product strategies vary by life cycle stage:
- **Introduction**: Limited product range, focus on establishing brand
- **Growth**: Expand product line, improve quality
- **Maturity**: Differentiate, add features, modify product
- **Decline**: Consider product deletion or repositioning

### 7.2 Price

The major pricing decision is whether to set a price **above, below or approximately even** with competitors' price.

| Strategy | Description | When Used |
|---|---|---|
| **Price skimming** | Setting high initial price, gradually lowering | New/innovative products, introduction stage |
| **Penetration pricing** | Setting low price to gain market share quickly | Entering competitive markets |
| **Competitive pricing** | Pricing at or near competitors' level | Established markets with many competitors |
| **Loss leader** | Selling below cost to attract customers | Retail strategies to drive foot traffic |
| **Price discrimination** | Different prices in different markets/segments | When markets are geographically spread or differentiated |

### 7.3 Promotion

Methods used to **persuade and remind** customers about products:

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **Advertising** | Paid, non-personal communication through media (TV, radio, online, print) |
| **Personal selling** | Direct communication between salesperson and customer |
| **Sales promotion** | Short-term incentives (discounts, coupons, contests) |
| **Publicity/Public relations** | Managing public image through media coverage, events |
| **Direct marketing** | Direct communication with targeted individuals (email, mail) |
| **Digital/Social media marketing** | Online presence through social media, websites, influencers |

### 7.4 Place (Distribution)

Deals with **channels of distribution** — how to get the product to customers:

| Channel | Description |
|---|---|
| **Direct** | Manufacturer → Consumer (e.g., online stores, factory outlets) |
| **One-level** | Manufacturer → Retailer → Consumer |
| **Two-level** | Manufacturer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer |
| **Three-level** | Manufacturer → Agent → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer |

Distribution considerations:
- **Intensive distribution**: Maximum outlets (e.g., soft drinks, newspapers)
- **Selective distribution**: Selected outlets (e.g., clothing brands)
- **Exclusive distribution**: Very few outlets (e.g., luxury goods)

## 8. Monitoring and Controlling the Marketing Plan

### 8.1 Key Definitions

- **Monitoring**: checking and observing the actual progress of the marketing plan
- **Controlling**: comparison of planned performance against actual performance and taking **corrective action** to ensure objectives are attained
- **Implementation**: putting marketing strategies into operation — the **how, where and when**

### 8.2 Developing Financial Forecasts

Details the costs and revenues for each strategy. By measuring sales potential and revenue forecasts and comparing with anticipated expenditures, the business can decide the best way to allocate resources.

Requires:
1. **Cost estimate**
2. **Revenue estimate**

### 8.3 Comparing Actual to Planned Results

Three key performance indicators:

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| **Sales analysis** | Uses sales data to evaluate current performance and effectiveness of marketing strategy |
| **Market share analysis/ratios** | Evaluates marketing strategies compared to competitors |
| **Marketing profitability analysis** | Breaks down total marketing costs into specific marketing activities |

### 8.4 Market Share Calculation

$$\text{Market Share (\%)} = \frac{\text{Company Sales}}{\text{Total Industry Sales}} \times 100$$

### 8.5 Revising the Marketing Strategy

Once analysis results are calculated, a business can assess whether objectives have been met or if changes are needed:

Changes could include:
1. **Changes in marketing mix** — modification to the 4Ps
2. **New product development** — adding new products to the range
3. **Product deletion** — removing underperforming products

### 8.6 Monitoring and Controlling — Worked Example

**Electronic Appliance Ltd:**

| Year | Sales Revenue ($ million) | Market Share (%) |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 28 | 18 |
| 2017 | 25 | 18 |
| 2018 | 33 | 14 |

- Sales revenue can decrease while market share stays the same if the **entire industry shrinks** (total industry sales also fell)
- Market share in 2018 decreased despite higher revenue — the **industry grew faster** than the company

**Apollo Tracksuits Pty Ltd:**

| Year | Apollo Sales ($m) | Industry Sales ($m) | Market Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 5 | 38 | 13.15% |
| 2014 | 7 | 44 | 15.91% |
| 2015 | 10 | 81 | 12.35% |
| 2016 | 9 | 70 | 12.86% |
| 2017 | 12 | 89 | 13.48% |

Product line sales analysis helps identify which products are underperforming vs forecast to make strategic adjustments.

## 9. McDonald's Marketing Case Study (Detailed)

> **Source**: "BUSINESS CASE STUDY — McDonald's" by A.J. Stanshall (2024), ResourceFactory.com.au

### 9.1 Strategic Role of Marketing

- Marketing puts the vision of a business into action
- McDonald's achieves sustained sales growth through strategies in product design, pricing, and promotional campaigns
- In October 2020, McDonald's announced **"Accelerating the Arches"** growth strategy, updated to **"Accelerating the Arches 2.0"** in 2023
- Major focus: maximise marketing with emphasis on **brand and affordability**
- Goal: increase **brand advocacy** — where customers' connection to the brand leads them to recommend it to others
- Shift from **brand engagement** (customers feeling personal connection) to **brand advocacy** (customers recommending the brand)

### 9.2 Factors Influencing Customer Choice — McDonald's

#### Psychological
- McDonald's understands key psychological drivers of consumers
- **Crocs collaboration (2023)**: Tapped into motivations — need to "belong" to a fashion trend, nostalgia and memories of happy childhood using iconic McDonald's characters
- **"Health halo" effect**: When an unhealthy option (fries) is pictured next to a healthy option (salad), consumers perceive the entire menu as healthier and feel less guilty about purchasing

#### Sociocultural
- Socioeconomic status, social class, culture, family, and peer groups influence behaviour
- **China expansion**: Rapid expansion of China's middle class — from ~80 million (2002) to over 500 million (2024) — means substantially more citizens have disposable income for McDonald's

#### Economic
- CEO Chris Kempczinski (2023): "macroeconomic uncertainties will persist, and we expect to continue to face headwinds"
- Major challenge: **rising inflation** (especially in the US) increases cost of inputs (raw materials, labour)
- Cannot always pass costs on through price rises due to competition and customer expectation
- Looks to **efficiency gains** to offset rising costs

#### Government
- Regulations influence consumer decisions
- **UK Calorie Labelling Regulations (2022)**: Mandatory calorie information at all customer touchpoints

### 9.3 Consumer Laws — McDonald's

#### Labelling
- Bound by **Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code**
- Data on ingredients, nutritional and allergen information available on company website
- Bound by state laws: **Food Act 2003 (NSW)**, **Food Regulation Act 2015 (NSW)**
- In NSW, mandatory to label **kilojoule intake** for each food item
- **2015 breach**: Animated menu boards made customers wait up to a minute to view kilojoule info, only screened for ~7 seconds. McDonald's worked with NSW Food Authority to amend digital menu
- **2018 Cancer Council study**: Despite mandatory labelling, no significant reduction in kilojoule content in meals — labelling had **limited impact on consumer behaviour**

#### Advertising
- **Quebec, Canada**: Consumer Protection Act forbids advertising to children under 13
- **2013 class action**: Alleged McDonald's breached this law by timing Happy Meal toy releases with film releases and displaying toys at "eye level" for children
- **Settled 2020**: McDonald's Canada donated CAN$1,000,000 to four charities and changed Happy Meal promotion practices

### 9.4 Ethical Considerations — McDonald's

#### Advertising to Children
- **1997 "McLibel" case**: Found McDonald's UK guilty of exploiting children with advertising
- McDonald's Australia is part of the **Australian Association of National Advertisers** (self-regulatory initiative)
- **2020 breach**: Ruled McDonald's violated agreement with TV ad featuring "naughty" girl rewarded with McDonald's trip — ad aimed at children, only featured less healthy options
- **2023 breach**: "Player of the Week" awards contravened agreement (advertisers must not give children occasional food products or vouchers as awards/prizes)
- **Research (2021)**: McDonald's appears to aim advertising more at children in lower-middle-income countries than higher-income countries

### 9.5 SWOT Analysis — McDonald's

| Category | Details |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Diversified income stream; goodwill/global brand; high-quality standards; detailed growth plan; largely uniform menu with geographic variations; extensive access to capital; large market share/scale; technology |
| **Weaknesses** | Lack of central control over franchised operations (March 2023 IT glitch shut down many stores); perception of unhealthiness; operating in multiple jurisdictions |
| **Opportunities** | Innovation and differentiation; reinvest in restaurants; drive improvements to achieve optimal capacity; anticipate/respond to changing consumer preferences; utilise digital media; improve social/environmental performance; new products |
| **Threats** | Negative publicity; increased governmental environmental protection; supply chain interruptions; commodity price increases; economic conditions/trading volatility; food safety breaches; competition (Burger King, Domino's, Yum! Brands, Starbucks, Wendy's); increasing wage costs; trade union action; increasing legal complexity; extreme weather/natural disasters/terrorism |

### 9.6 Product Life Cycle — McDonald's

- Management sees **burgers** as "mature", **chicken and coffee** as still in "growth" phase
- **French fries example:**
  - **Introduction**: Replaced packets of potato chips at McDonald's in the 1940s
  - **Growth**: 1940s–1990, rapidly expanded across US and internationally, became menu favourite
  - **Maturity (1990)**: Phil Sokolof campaigned against saturated fat in beef tallow used for fries. McDonald's changed recipe — met with customer resistance, share price fell
  - **Post-Maturity/Renewal**: Further recipe changes to reduce saturated fat + **innovation and extension** strategy — "Loaded fries", "Twisted fries", "Waffle fries", "Cheesy Bacon fries"

### 9.7 Market Research — McDonald's

- Traditional: surveys, questionnaires, sales data
- Modern: apps and technology provide **real-time market information** — what items sell, at what time, where, to whom
- CMO Tariq Hassan: "we are not short on data... the question becomes, which data is most important and how do you use that data?"
- **AI-powered dynamic pricing**: Models adjust menu prices at different stores, ascertain at what level consumers "resist" price increases
- CEO Chris Kempczinski (Jan 2024): Loyalty program growth linked to smarter, personalised pricing and digital offers
- **Senior manager Jill McDonald (2023)**: "As we continue to learn more about our loyalty customers... we'll leverage machine learning at scale... looking at thousands of customer cohorts, where we used to have less than ten"

### 9.8 Market Objectives & Target Markets

- Multiple target markets simultaneously
- Segments customers using geographic, cultural, demographic, psychographic, and behavioural factors
- With digital ordering data, moving towards **"ultimate" segmentation** — each "market" consisting of one person
- Under **Accelerating the Arches 2.0**:
  1. **Maximise marketing** — encourage brand advocacy through Food, Integrity, Value, Affinity
  2. **Commit to the core** — renewed focus on chicken, burgers, coffee
  3. **Double down on 4 D's** — Digital, Delivery, Drive-Thru, Development

### 9.9 Market Segmentation — McDonald's

#### Demographic
- **Age**: Happy Meals (young children) vs McCafé (older customers)
- **Race/Ethnicity**: US campaigns directed at Black, Hispanic, Asian consumers
- **Religion**: Halal and Kosher foods in certain locations; no beef/pork in India (Hindu belief); Filet-o-Fish created in 1962 for Catholic consumers (no meat on Fridays)
- **Family size**: Family Bundles in Australia

#### Psychographic
- Divides customers by interests, attitudes, values, lifestyles
- Moving away from traditional demographic models towards **data-driven, hyper-personalised segmentation** using loyalty scheme data

#### Behavioural
- Split customers by loyalty, purchase frequency, average transaction value
- Shifted from paper loyalty cards (buy 5 coffees, get 1 free) to **app-only system in 2019**
- App purchases earn **rewards points** convertible to free products
- Data analysis of past behaviour lets company **predict future patterns** for targeted marketing

#### Geographic
- Adapts marketing mix for different locations
- Examples: Malaysian soup, Pizza Pies (Asia), McAloo Tikki Burger (India), Double Beef Prosperity Burger (Chinese New Year markets)

### 9.10 Product/Service Differentiation & Positioning

#### Differentiation
- Informal eating outlet market contains ~10 million restaurants
- McDonald's differentiates through **speed of service** since inception

#### Positioning
- **Negative perception**: 2004 *Super Size Me* documentary and "McLibel" trial — accusations of high-calorie, high-fat food, large portions (largest drinks = 42 ounces / 1.2 litres)
- **Response**: Phased out Super-Sized option, added healthier options
- Aims to be seen as a **value option** for as many consumers as possible and a **good corporate citizen**

### 9.11 Product Strategies — McDonald's

- **COVID-19 pandemic** forced simplified menu → discovered "core" products (burgers, chicken, coffee) make up **~70% of sales** across top 6 markets
- Future growth centred on the "core"

#### Branding
- More people recognise the "golden arches" than the Christian cross (*Fast-food Nation*)
- Hundreds of trademarks filed: logos, words (MCMUFFIN), phrases (BIG RED SHOE), designs (Drive-Thru sign shape), letters (QPC = Quarter Pounder)
- Products are brands in their own right: **Big Mac valued at over US$1 billion**; McDonald's has **17 "billion-dollar brands"**

#### Packaging
- Packaging reinforces brand messaging and maintains consistent brand identity
- Helps operations — crew can easily identify correct package for correct item
- **Late 2020**: Launched new worldwide packaging — modern, fresh, simple bold graphics with a sense of fun

### 9.12 Pricing Strategies — McDonald's

- Stated aim: **deliver value** to customers
- Uses **AI-powered pricing models** to test customer price resistance

#### Loss Leaders
- **Loose Change Menu** (Australia): Items from AU$1.00
- Low-price items (e.g., $2 hamburger) attract customers who also purchase higher-margin items (coffee)

#### Price Points / "Value Ladder"
- Multiple price points to accommodate varying levels of affordability
- Loose Change Menu (lowest) → Standard menu → Gourmet burgers (Chicken Deluxe)
- Appeals to widest possible range of customers

#### Price Bundling
- **McValue Meals**: Big Mac Value Meal ~$13.35 = 14% saving vs buying individually (Big Mac $7.55 + medium fries $3.70 + medium drink $4.30 = $15.55)

### 9.13 Promotion Strategies — McDonald's

#### Advertising
- One of the most prolific advertisers in the world; **3rd largest ad spender in Australia**
- Spends ~**US$4 billion per year** on promotion worldwide
- Uses TV, radio, billboards, social media; sponsors sports teams and leagues
- Regional approach via local "co-ops" — stores in same area contribute to advertising fund
- Shift from advertising to **engaging** (CMO Morgan Flatley)
- Pivoted from direct sales activation to **brand building campaigns**

#### Publicity
- Benefits from positive news stories about new products
- Negative publicity: *Super Size Me*, McLibel, China meat scandal, *McMillion$* documentary, free food to Israeli soldiers (2024)
- Engages **public relations specialists** (e.g., This is Mango)
- Example: 2023 news release on McDonald's Caboolture store + Clean Up Australia Day

#### Sales Promotions
- **2022 FIFA World Cup**: Largest global marketing campaign ever — McDelivery in 75 countries, 10 languages, 4 dialects
- Featured Jason Sudeikis and TikTok star Khaby Lame
- Example: Yellow/red card in match → Canadian fans got promotional code for free items
- Reported **double-digit increases in delivery sales** in top ten markets

### 9.14 Place/Distribution — McDonald's

- **Distribution channel**: Producer → Retailer → Customer
- **Selective distribution**: ~42,000 restaurants worldwide
- **Delivery**: 55,000 delivery orders being prepared/delivered at any moment; **US$16 billion** in delivery sales (2023)
- Focus on encouraging more customers to use **Drive-Thru**

### 9.15 Global Marketing — McDonald's

- **"One McDonald's Way"**: Successful campaigns in one market are quickly customised and rolled out in multiple markets
- **Global branding**: Relatively similar experience worldwide; food, service, and brand internationally recognised
- **Olympic sponsor since 1976**

#### Standardisation
- ~2.5 million Big Macs sold daily globally
- ~15 core menu items appear in almost every store worldwide
- Leverages **economies of scale** for inputs and marketing
- **McCafé standardisation**: Plans to make McCafé the exclusive global coffee brand; reducing 100+ types of coffee equipment to just 5 global suppliers

#### Customisation
- Acknowledges unique cultural tastes while maintaining global consistency
- Examples: beer on menu in European countries (France, Germany, Portugal); Aloo wrap in India; Teriyaki Mac Burger in Japan

## 11. HSC Response Frameworks for Marketing Questions

### 11.1 Key Verbs and What They Require

| Verb | What to Do | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Identify** | Name/list without explanation | Simple list or point |
| **Define** | Give a precise meaning | One clear sentence |
| **Describe** | Give characteristics and features | Definition + characteristics + features |
| **Explain** | Show how/why, cause and effect | Define → Relationship between components → Implications → Link |
| **Discuss** | Present arguments for and against | Arguments for → Arguments against → Judgement |
| **Analyse** | Break down into components and examine | Identify components → Examine relationships → Draw conclusions |
| **Evaluate/Assess** | Make a judgement based on criteria | Arguments for → Arguments against → Weighed judgement → Recommendation |
| **Justify** | Support a decision with evidence | State position → Provide reasons/evidence → Conclude |

### 11.2 PEEL Paragraph Structure

- **P** — Point (state your argument/thesis)
- **E** — Explain (define and elaborate)
- **E** — Evidence (case study example, statistics, specific details)
- **L** — Link (connect back to the question)

### 11.3 "Explain" Scaffold

```
A. Define — What is it?
B. Relationship between components — How does the issue impact the business?
C. Implications — What does this lead to?
D. Link — Therefore...
```


### 11.6 Sample Response — Marketing Approaches

> **Question**: Explain how Apple uses its marketing approaches to ensure customer expectations are met.

**Paragraph 1 (Sales Approach)**:
Apple uses a marketing approach, which is a strategy focused on identifying and satisfying customer needs and wants through research and communication. In the early stages of the iPhone, although the product was highly innovative, Apple still focused on understanding how consumers would respond to new technology and tailored its marketing to address this. The company recognised that customers were unfamiliar with such advanced features, so it designed marketing campaigns that both informed and persuaded consumers while still aligning with their emerging needs. This meant Apple was not just pushing sales, but ensuring customers understood how the product would benefit them. As a result, this led to strong customer interest and adoption of the iPhone. Therefore, Apple met customer expectations by introducing innovative products while ensuring marketing was aligned with consumer understanding and needs.

**Paragraph 2 (Marketing Approach)**:
Apple continues to use a marketing approach by consistently focusing on customer feedback and market research to guide product development. As consumer expectations increased, Apple shifted towards more persuasive marketing, backed by data collected from surveys and user responses to features and applications. This information directly impacts business decisions, as seen in changes to iPhone design such as larger screens, thinner builds, and improved camera quality. For example, the release of larger iPhones in 2014 reflected clear consumer demand. This leads to higher customer satisfaction, as products are designed specifically to meet evolving preferences. Therefore, Apple ensures customer expectations are met by continuously adapting its products based on ongoing market research and consumer feedback.

### 11.7 Sample Response — Discuss the Strategic Role of Marketing

> **Question**: Discuss the strategic role of marketing.

Marketing is the process of increasing customer revenue generation through promotions, product, place and price. This is achieved through effective market research and understanding your target audience to provide their demands effectively and efficiently, leading to an increase in sales which leads to profit maximisation overall affecting the business positively. Although marketing allows the business to potentially increase sales and overall revenue, there is a large amount of funds required to fulfil this which a small business will not be able to afford and may end up causing a loss if the market is not assessed correctly as marketing to people who do not need the product in effect will lead to a loss of money. ## 13. Quick Reference — Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Marketing** | Total system of interacting activities to plan, promote, price and distribute products |
| **Marketing plan** | Sets out actions and strategies to attain greater sales and achieve financial goals |
| **Marketing objectives** | Realistic and measurable goals to be achieved through the marketing plan |
| **Target market** | Group of present and potential customers to which a business intends to sell |
| **Market segmentation** | Dividing a market into distinct groups with similar characteristics |
| **Marketing mix** | Combination of the 4Ps — Product, Price, Promotion, Place |
| **SWOT analysis** | Identification of internal strengths/weaknesses and external opportunities/threats |
| **Market research** | Systematic collection, recording and analysis of information about a marketing problem |
| **Primary data** | Data gathered first-hand by the business |
| **Secondary data** | Data from other people's primary research |
| **Market share** | Proportion of total industry sales captured by a business |
| **Product life cycle** | Stages a product passes through: introduction, growth, maturity, decline |
| **Consumer orientation** | Business approach where customer relationship begins with the sale |
| **Relationship marketing** | Marketing focused on customer loyalty and repeat sales |
| **Puffery** | Exaggerated claims no reasonable person would take as factual |
| **SUGGING** | Selling Under the Guise of a Survey |
| **Price discrimination** | Setting different prices for the same product in different markets |
| **ACCC** | Australian Competition and Consumer Commission — enforces CCA 2010 |
