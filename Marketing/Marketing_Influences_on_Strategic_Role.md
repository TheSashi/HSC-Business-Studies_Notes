# Marketing Influences on the Strategic Role of Marketing — Model Answers

## Question: Analyse the Impact of Influences on Achieving the Strategic Role of Marketing

### Key Term: Analyse
> Define, cause, implication, long-term implication — show the relationship between influences and the strategic role of marketing

---

## Strategic Roles of Marketing

| Strategic Role | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Profit Maximisation** | Increasing revenue and reducing costs to maximise profit |
| **Increase Market Share** | Growing the proportion of sales in the market |
| **Enter New Markets** | Expanding into new geographic or demographic markets |

---

## Influences on Marketing (Choose 3 — one per body paragraph)

### Categories of Influences

1. **Consumer Law** (Competition and Consumer Act 2010)
   - Deceptive and misleading advertising
   - Price discrimination
   - Implied conditions
   - Warranties

2. **Ethical Issues**
   - Truth, accuracy and good taste in advertising
   - Products that may damage health
   - Engaging in fair competition
   - Sugging (selling under guise of research)

3. **Factors Influencing Consumer Choice**
   - Psychological
   - Sociocultural
   - Economic
   - Government

## Model Answer: Apple Inc.

### Introduction

> *Apple Inc. is one of the largest technology companies in the world, built on product innovation and brand loyalty to drive its marketing strategy. Apple's strategic roles of marketing centre on profit maximisation, increasing market share, and entering new markets through premium product positioning and global expansion. This response will analyse how economic factors that influence consumer choice, price discrimination under the Competition and Consumer Act 2010, and ethical issues surrounding truth and accuracy in advertising affect Apple's ability to achieve these strategic roles.*

---

### Body Paragraph 1: Implied Conditions under the CCA

**Definition:** Implied conditions are conditions required under law to be met for every product sold, even if not written down (e.g., fitness for particular purpose).

**Cause/Effect #1 — Fitness for Particular Purpose:**
- Apple must ensure products perform their intended function (e.g., iPhone must make/receive calls)
- If unable to perform → legal issue → customer entitled to refund or replacement
- Costs Apple in lost revenue and replacement stock
- Damages brand reputation → customers less likely to buy again or recommend

**Cause/Effect #2 — Product Matching Claims:**
- Product must match packaging and advertising claims
- Apple had issues claiming iPhones had more storage than actual (OS taking up space)
- Breached implied conditions under CCA → potential fines for misleading customers
- Further damages consumer trust

**Implication (Long-term):**
- Breaching CCA costs money (refunds, replacements, fines)
- Reputational damage → customers reluctant to purchase or recommend
- Erodes customer base over time
- Undermines ability to increase sales and achieve profit maximisation

**Point out issue:** Apple must follow implied conditions under CCA to maintain and grow sales

---

### Body Paragraph 2: Economic Factors Influencing Consumer Choice

**Definition:** Conditions such as consumer income levels, inflation, interest rates, and the general state of the economy that shape spending willingness and ability.

**Cause/Effect #1 — Economic Downturn:**
- During recession or high inflation → consumers have less disposable income
- Apple products priced at premium vs Samsung/Xiaomi → particularly vulnerable
- Example: Cost of living pressures 2022–2023 → consumers shifted to cheaper alternatives or held onto existing phones
- Apple reported slower iPhone sales growth in several markets

**Cause/Effect #2 — Economic Growth:**
- During growth and rising wages → consumers more willing to spend on premium goods
- Apple benefits as brand positioning appeals to aspirational buyers
- Apple products seen as status symbols

**Implication (Long-term):**
- Apple's premium pricing makes market share growth **dependent on broader economic conditions** the company cannot control
- To protect market share during downturns → may need more affordable product lines
- Example: iPhone SE range and older model discounts
- Without adapting → risks losing market share to competitors serving price-sensitive consumers
- Limits ability to enter new markets where average incomes are lower

---

### Body Paragraph 3: Ethical Issues — Truth and Accuracy in Advertising

**Definition:** Ethical advertising requires businesses to present products honestly, without exaggerating capabilities or concealing limitations.

**Cause/Effect #1 — Overstating Product Capabilities:**
- Apple's marketing of battery life on iPhones and MacBooks questioned by consumers and reviewers
- Real-world performance often fell short of advertising claims
- Raises ethical concerns about whether consumers receive accurate information

**Cause/Effect #2 — Impact on New Markets:**
- In new markets (developing countries) → consumers less familiar with brand
- Rely heavily on advertising claims to judge premium-priced products
- If advertising seen as misleading → word spreads quickly via social media and online reviews
- Slower adoption rates in new territories

**Implication (Long-term):**
- Persistent questions about advertising accuracy attract regulatory scrutiny
- Advertising standards bodies (Australia, UK, India) can force ads to be pulled or corrected
- Generates negative publicity
- For Apple — whose strategy depends on perception of quality and trustworthiness — even small ethical lapses have outsized consequences
- Must maintain strict standards of truth and accuracy to successfully enter new markets

---

## Conclusion

> *Implied conditions under the CCA force Apple to meet minimum product standards or face financial penalties and reputational harm that reduce sales and limit profit maximisation. Economic factors outside Apple's control directly shape consumer purchasing power, making the company's premium pricing strategy a barrier to growing market share during downturns. Ethical issues around truth and accuracy in advertising affect Apple's credibility, which is especially damaging when the company is trying to build trust in new markets. These influences show that Apple's strategic roles of profit maximisation, increasing market share, and entering new markets are not achieved through marketing alone — they are shaped through legal, economic, and ethical forces that require constant attention and adaptation.*

## Analyse Scaffold (Template)

Use this structure for any "analyse" question:

1. **Definition** of the concept
2. **Cause/Effect #1** — How (observable changes) + Why (link to topic-specific goal)
3. **Cause/Effect #2** — Link to cause #1
4. **Implications (Long-term)** — Link to broader objective/business goals
5. **Point out issue** — What should the business do?
