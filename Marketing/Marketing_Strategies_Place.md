# Place (Distribution) Strategies

> **HSC Business Studies — Marketing Topic** | Distribution channels, channel choice, and physical distribution.

---

## Definition

**Place** is NOT about location — it is about distribution channels and channel choices. These are the activities that make products available to customers when and where they want to purchase them.

---

## Distribution/Marketing Channels

The routes taken to get the product from factory to customer:

1. **Producer → Customer** (direct)
2. **Producer → Retailer → Customer**
3. **Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Customer**
4. **Producer → Agent → Wholesaler → Retailer → Customer**

Most large businesses use at least 2 methods for flexibility and convenience.

**Apple example:** Uses producer→consumer (Apple website, Apple stores) AND producer→retailer→consumer (JB Hi-Fi, Optus, Telstra).

---

## Innovative Distribution Methods

Retailing activity conducted away from traditional stores:
1. Door-to-door selling
2. Mail order catalogues
3. Party plan merchandise
4. Vending machines
5. Telemarketing
6. Internet marketing (e-commerce)

---

## Channel Choice (Market Coverage)

How a business chooses the channel best suited to its product, depending on market coverage — the number of outlets a firm chooses for its products.

### Intensive Distribution
- Saturates the market with products in as many outlets as possible
- Example: Coca-Cola and Pepsi — supermarkets, vending machines, corner stores, restaurants, sporting games, canteens

### Selective Distribution
- Uses only a moderate proportion of all possible outlets
- Example: Apple — can buy from Apple stores and selected retailers (JB Hi-Fi, Telstra), but not available everywhere

### Exclusive Distribution
- Uses only one retail outlet for a product in a large geographic area
- Example: Ferrari, Tiffany jewellery — available only from specific, very limited outlets

---

## Physical Distribution Issues

Activities concerned with efficient movement of products from producer to customer:

1. **Transport** — method depends on type of product being transported
2. **Warehousing** — storage of goods at various points in the distribution channel
3. **Inventory control systems** — maintain quantities and varieties of products appropriate for the market

---

## Apple — Place Case Study

- Apple stores in prime city locations (selective distribution)
- Online store for direct-to-consumer sales
- Authorised resellers (JB Hi-Fi, Harvey Norman, carriers)
- Selective, not intensive — maintains premium brand image by limiting outlets

---

## Key Exam Points

- **Distinguish** between intensive, selective, and exclusive distribution
- **Explain** how channel choice relates to target market and brand positioning
- **Analyse** how place strategies interact with other elements of the marketing mix
- Link place to **customer convenience** and **competitive advantage**
