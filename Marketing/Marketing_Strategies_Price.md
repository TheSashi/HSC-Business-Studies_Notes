# Pricing Methods and Strategies

> **HSC Business Studies — Marketing Topic** | Pricing methods, strategies, and Apple case study applications.

---

## Pricing Methods

Pricing refers to the amount of money a customer is prepared to offer in exchange for a product. Business pricing decisions are influenced by internal and external factors.

### 1. Cost-Based Pricing

The business determines the cost of producing one unit, then adds a markup (percentage) to cover additional costs and profit margin.

**Formula:** Cost + (Cost x Markup percentage) = Price

**Disadvantages:**
- Difficult to accurately determine an appropriate markup percentage
- Product is priced after production costs are incurred without considering other marketing mix elements or market conditions

### 2. Market-Based Pricing

Sets prices according to the interaction between supply and demand — whatever the market is prepared to pay. Prices constantly change with supply/demand fluctuations, making it difficult to apply.

### 3. Competition-Based Pricing

Price covers costs and is comparable to competitors' prices. Used when there is high competition from businesses producing similar products.

Three approaches:
- **Below competitors** — entering a new market
- **Equal to competitors** — following the price leader
- **Above competitors** — positioning product as superior

## Pricing Strategies

### Price Skimming
- Used when a business has produced a new, innovative product (leading edge technology)
- Charges a higher price before competitors start producing/selling the product
- Allows business to recoup R&D costs
- Example: Smart TVs, new iPhones

### Price Penetration
- Charges the lowest possible price while still covering costs
- Aims to quickly achieve large market share ("mass market pricing")
- Typically used when there is increased competition OR business is at establishment stage
- Not used long-term as it impacts revenue and profit generation

### Loss Leader
- Gets customers into the business by selling some products below cost
- Customers think they have saved money and may purchase other products at full price
- Example: Supermarket specials on milk/bread to attract shoppers

### Price Points
- Setting prices at certain levels that are considered acceptable by consumers
- Example: $9.99 instead of $10 — psychologically feels cheaper
- Common in retail and fast food

### Prestige Pricing
- Setting prices deliberately high to create an image of quality and exclusivity
- Higher price = consumers perceive the product as superior
- Example: Rolex, Louis Vuitton, Apple products

## Apple — Pricing Case Study

Apple uses a combination of pricing strategies:

- **Price skimming** — new iPhones launched at premium prices (iPhone 16 Pro Max ~$2,199), then prices drop as newer models release
- **Prestige pricing** — premium pricing reinforces Apple's luxury brand image
- **Price points** — product range at different price tiers (iPhone 16 ~$1,399, iPhone 16 Pro ~$1,799, Pro Max ~$2,199)
- **Selective price reductions** — older models sold at lower prices to capture price-sensitive segments

Apple's pricing supports its differentiation strategy — the high price signals quality, innovation, and exclusivity to its target market.
