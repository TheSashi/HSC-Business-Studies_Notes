# Role of Marketing & Marketing Approaches

> **HSC Business Studies — Marketing** | Strategic role, marketing plan, three approaches, customer orientation, relationship marketing, types of markets, and interdependence with other business functions.

---

## 1. Definition

**Marketing** is the total system of interacting activities designed to plan, promote, price and distribute products to present and potential customers.

---

## 2. Strategic Role of Marketing

- A common financial business goal is **profit maximisation**
- The **strategic role of marketing** is to translate this financial goal into reality
- Marketing today places strong emphasis on **customer-oriented marketing** — viewing the business through the customer's eyes

### Marketing Plan
The business applies marketing strategies based on market research:
- Knowing what they need to sell
- Understanding advantages, disadvantages, opportunities and threats
- Analysing customers, their needs, demands, and demographics
- Understanding where and when to sell for optimal revenue maximisation

---

## 3. Marketing Approaches

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Production Approach** | Relies on the view that consumers base their purchasing on the quality of the product. Marketing consisted of simply taking the orders and delivering the products |
| **Sales Approach** | Emphasised selling because of increased competition. Therefore to stimulate demand for their goods and services, businesses increased their spending on advertising |
| **Marketing Approach** | Focuses on finding what the customer wants (through market research) and then satisfying that need. Involves adopting a customer orientation with the belief that all actions should be aimed at satisfying customer needs |

### Marketing Approach Requirements
- **Customer oriented** — all actions aimed at satisfying customer needs
- **Supported by integrated marketing strategies**
- **Aimed at satisfying customers**
- **Integrated into the business plan** to achieve goals

---

## 4. Customer Orientation

- The customer relationship **does not end with the sale — it begins there**
- Businesses look for ways to improve **after-sale service** and fulfil customer needs/wants
- **Examples:** Free car servicing with new car purchase; longer warranty periods and free replacement for faulty electrical goods

### Relationship Marketing
- Ensures **existing customers are satisfied**
- Core: **customer loyalty** to generate repeat sales
- Achieved through: reward programs, customer care, good after-sales service

---

## 5. Types of Markets

A **market** = group of individuals/organisations that:
- Need or want a product (goods and services)
- Have the money (purchasing power) to purchase the product
- Are willing to spend their money to obtain the product
- Are socially and legally authorised to purchase the product

| Market Type | Description | Examples |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **Resource Market** | Primary production (mining, agriculture, forestry, fishing) | Farmers, miners |
| **Industrial Market** | Industries/businesses purchasing products for production or operations | Factories buying raw materials |
| **Intermediate Market** | Wholesalers and retailers who purchase finished products and resell for profit | Coles, Woolworths |
| **Consumer Market** | Individuals who plan to use/consume products | End consumers |

### Consumer Market Segments

| Segment | Description | Examples |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Mass Market** | Products appealing to all types of consumers | Basic food items, electricity |
| **Market Segment** | Business chooses to focus on one area of a particular market | Sports cars |
| **Niche Market** | Smaller section of a market segment | Teenage girl magazines, outdoor lifestyle |

## 6. Interdependence with Other Business Functions

Marketing does not operate in isolation — it relies on and interacts with other business functions.

### Operations
- Operations manages the production of goods and services
- Marketing provides demand forecasts so operations knows how much to produce
- Operations produces the goods that marketing sells
- Marketing research identifies the features customers want, which operations then builds into products

### Finance
- Finance has a strong relationship with marketing as the department is vital for providing funds and budgets
- The marketing function works on analysing and selling products that meet customer needs and generate revenue
- This revenue allows finance to pay off business expenses including marketing other departments and third party expenses

### Human Resources (HR)
- HR recruits qualified employees and trains them on the resources the business uses
- This provides marketing with performance employees who are passionate and hard working
- If employees aren't trained correctly they could assess wrong, leading to lower conversion rates and lower company profits
- Marketing and HR are very closely linked and both rely on each other for optimal performance

### Describe Scaffold — Interdependence
**A. Definition:** Human resources is the department which recruits qualified employees and trains them, providing marketing with performance employees who allow marketing to sell the product effectively.

**B. Characteristics/Features:** This is through multiple training programs and understanding their target audience, as the employees are the ones who interact with customers.

### Explain Scaffold — Interdependence
**A. Define:** Finance has a strong relationship with marketing as the department is vital for providing them with funds and budgets.

**B. Relationship:** The marketing function works on analysing and selling products that meet customer needs, generating revenue that allows finance to pay expenses.

**C. Implications:** This collaboration leads to higher sales and a stronger market position.

**D. Link:** The reinvestment of these funds allows the business to achieve its long-term goal of growth.

## 7. Apple Case Study — Marketing Approaches

### Sales Approach (Early iPhone)
- The innovative nature of the Apple iPhone created a sales approach to marketing (introductory stages of product life cycle)
- Companies like Apple were developing new products with capacities beyond consumer expectations
- Marketing strategies were aimed at educating customers on the features of earlier iPhones

### Marketing Approach (Current)
- Focus of marketing iPhones has become more persuasive rather than informative
- Customers have developed expectations regarding capacity of current and future designs
- Greater focus on market research and making products that meet the needs and wants of consumers
- Apple conducts regular surveys to assess responses to applications and design features
- Customer feedback has led to changes in iPhone design: larger screen size, thinner phone, improved camera specification
- In 2014 Apple released the larger screen sized iPhones in response to consumer demand

## 8. BYD Case Study — Interdependence

BYD (Chinese electric vehicle manufacturer) demonstrates strong interdependence between business functions:

### Operations ↔ Marketing
- Operations designs and manufactures electric vehicles using innovative technology
- Marketing uses this technology to promote vehicle safety and reliability
- Marketing provides customer feedback on price and features to operations, which modifies the cars to meet those needs
- When operations delivers quality, affordable EVs, marketing can sell them in more international markets

### Finance ↔ Marketing
- As marketing identifies new trends, operations adapts production to create updated versions
- This collaboration leads to higher sales and a stronger market position
- The business uses resulting profit to reinvest in factories and research, creating a cycle of continuous improvement

### Reinvestment Cycle
- The reinvestment of funds allows BYD to achieve its long-term goal of growth in the electric vehicle industry
