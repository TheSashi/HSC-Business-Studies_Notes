# Role of Operations & Strategic Direction

> **HSC Business Studies — Operations** | Definition, transformation, value adding, customer focus, cost leadership, and goods/services differentiation.

---

## 1. Definition

**Operations** = the business process that involves **transformation** and **value adding**.

- **Transformation:** Changing raw materials and resources into outputs (goods/services)
- **Value Adding:** Creation of extra or added value as inputs are transformed into outputs
  - Manufacturing: making changes and/or adding inputs to form final product
  - Services: through knowledge, skills, and expertise

---

## 2. Operations and Customer Focus

Contemporary business places the customer foremost. Customers want firms to:
- Waste **minimal resources** in production
- Reflect **fair value** for labour used
- Operate at **low cost** to maximise affordability
- Integrate **environmental awareness** and ecologically sustainable practices
- Reflect **changes in customer needs** over time

---

## 3. Strategic Role of Operations

Operations managers contribute to the **strategic direction** of the organisation. Overarching goal: **maximise profit** through increasing revenue or minimising expenses.

### Cost Leadership
- Aiming for the **lowest costs** or most price competitive while remaining profitable
- **Economies of Scale:** Cost advantages from increased operations
  - Lower cost per unit via global sourcing or bulk buying
  - Efficiencies from improved technology/machinery
- Strategies: eliminating waste, standardised products, high volume, automated production, bulk buying

### Goods and Service Differentiation
Distinguishing goods/services from competitors.

**Goods Differentiation:**
- Varying actual product features (basic → complex varieties)
- Varying product quality
- Varying augmented features (extra warranty, free delivery, additional features)

**Services Differentiation:**
- Varying amount of time spent on service
- Varying level of expertise
- Varying qualifications and experience of provider
- Varying quality of materials/technology used

### Cross Branding
- Strategic alliances for differentiation
- **Examples:** Woolworths and Caltex, Coles and Shell

---

## 4. Goods vs Services

| Feature | Goods | Services |
|---------|-------|----------|
| **Tangibility** | Tangible (can touch/see) | Intangible (cannot touch) |
| **Storage** | Can be stored | Cannot be stored |
| **Production** | Produced before sale | Produced at time of sale |
| **Quality** | Consistent | Variable |
| **Examples** | Cars, phones, food | Teaching, hairdressing |

---

## 5. Performance Objectives

Performance objectives are the goals the operations function is trying to achieve.

| Objective | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Quality** | Quality of design (consumer understanding) + Quality of conformance (meeting specifications) |
| **Speed** | Time for production to respond to market changes; reduced wait/lead/processing times |
| **Dependability** | Consistency and reliability of products |
| **Flexibility** | How quickly processes can adjust to market changes |
| **Customisation** | Individualised products for specific customer needs (mass customisation) |
| **Cost** | Minimisation of expenses; conducting processes as cheaply as possible |

### Apple Case Study — Performance Objectives

| Objective | Apple Example |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Quality** | "We have never been about selling the most. We're about selling the best" — Tim Cook |
| **Speed** | Must quickly respond to demand after new iPhone launch |
| **Dependability** | iPhone X delayed due to OLED display issues from Samsung supplier |
| **Flexibility** | iPhone 6/6 Plus launched with larger screens in response to competitor designs |
| **Cost** | iPhone SE with cheaper components; moved production to India to eliminate 20% customs duty; vertical integration (purchased Intel's 5G modem division) |
