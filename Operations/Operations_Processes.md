# Operations Processes — Inputs, Transformation & Outputs

> **HSC Business Studies — Operations** | Inputs (transformed/transforming resources), transformation process (4Vs), technology, task design, layout, sequencing, and outputs.

---

## 1. Inputs

Inputs are split into **Transformed** and **Transforming** resources.

### Transformed Resources (inputs that are changed)
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Materials** | Raw materials converted into finished products |
| **Information** | Data processed into useful knowledge |
| **Customers** | People who are transformed (e.g., haircut, education) |

### Transforming Resources (inputs that do the changing)
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Human Resources** | Labour, skills, knowledge of employees |
| **Facilities** | Machinery, equipment, buildings, technology |

---

## 2. Transformation Process — The 4Vs

The 4Vs are key influences on the transformation process.

### Volume
- How much product is made
- **Volume flexibility:** How quickly the process can adjust to demand changes
- **Responsiveness:** Managing lead times (time from order to fulfilment)
- High volume → efficiency, automation, lower unit costs
- Low volume → manual work, skilled labour, higher costs

### Variety
- **Mix flexibility:** Range of goods/services delivered
- Greater variety → more complex operations processes needed
- High variety → flexible machinery, advanced technology, detailed planning
- Low variety → simpler, more efficient production

### Variation in Demand
- Impacts significantly on transformation resources
- Increase in demand requires: more supplies, human resources, energy, machinery, technology
- **Predicting demand:** Operations relies on marketing for accurate sales forecasts

### Visibility (Customer Contact)
- Customer contact and feedback directly affects transformation
- **Direct contact:** Surveys, interviews, warranty claims, letters, verbal contact
- **Indirect contact:** Sales figures, observation, customer reviews

---

## 3. Technology

Technology is a key input used in the transformation stage.

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Leading Edge** | Brand new innovative technology | AI, robotics, 3D printing |
| **Established** | Proven technology widely used | Computers, standard machinery |
| **Appropriate** | Best suited to the business's needs and context | Simple tools for developing countries |

---

## 4. Task Design

**Task design** = how work is organised and performed.

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Job Design** | One worker completes entire task (high skill, low efficiency) |
| **Division of Labour** | Task broken into specialist parts (high efficiency, lower skill per worker) |
| **Team-Based** | Groups of workers collaborate on tasks |
| **Job Enlargement** | Expanding the range of tasks a worker performs |
| **Job Enrichment** | Adding more responsibility and autonomy |

---

## 5. Layout

**Layout** = physical arrangement of resources in the transformation process.

| Type | Description | Best For |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Product Layout** | Resources arranged in sequence of production | High volume, standardised products |
| **Process Layout** | Similar resources grouped together | High variety, customised products |
| **Fixed-Position Layout** | Product stays in one place; resources move to it | Large, bulky products (ships, buildings) |
| **Cellular Layout** | Grouped into cells, each producing a family of products | Medium variety, medium volume |

---

## 6. Sequencing and Scheduling

### Sequencing
- The **order** in which activities in the operation processes occur
- Determines priority of tasks

### Scheduling
- The **length of time** activities take within operations
- Allocating time to each task
- Tools: Gantt charts, critical path analysis

---

## 7. Monitoring, Controlling and Improvement

### Monitoring
- Measuring **actual performance** against planned performance
- Based on **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**

### Controlling
- Taking corrective action when performance deviates from plan
- Adjusting processes to maintain quality and efficiency

### Improvement
- **Continuous Improvement (Kaizen):** Ongoing incremental improvements to processes
- **Total Quality Management (TQM):** Holistic approach; quality is everyone's responsibility

---

## 8. Outputs

Outputs are the results of the business's efforts:
1. **Final good and/or service**
2. **Customer service**
3. **Warranties**

> The operations manager needs to be able to measure outputs against objectives.
