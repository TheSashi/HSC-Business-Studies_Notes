# Operations Management — Comprehensive Notes

---

## 1. ROLE OF OPERATIONS

Operations refers to the business process that involves **transformation** and **value adding**.

- **Transformation**: Changing raw materials and resources into outputs (goods/services).
- **Value Adding**: The creation of extra or added value as inputs are transformed into outputs.
  - In manufacturing: value added by making changes and/or adding more inputs to form the final product.
  - In services: value added through knowledge, skills, and expertise.

### Operations and Customer Focus

Contemporary business places the customer and their needs foremost. Customers want firms to:

- Waste minimal resources in production
- Reflect fair value for any labour used
- Operate at low cost to maximise affordability
- Integrate environmental awareness and ecologically sustainable practices
- Reflect changes in customer needs over time

### Strategic Role of Operations

Operations managers contribute to the **strategic direction** of the organisation. The overarching goal is typically to maximise profit through increasing revenue or minimising expenses.

#### Cost Leadership
- Aiming to have the **lowest costs** or be the most price competitive while remaining profitable.
- Achieved through:
  - **Economies of Scale**: Cost advantages from increased operations — lower cost per unit via global sourcing or bulk buying; efficiencies from improved technology/machinery.
  - Eliminating waste, producing standardised products, high volume output, automated production systems, bulk buying inputs.

#### Goods and Service Differentiation
- Distinguishing goods/services from competitors.
- **Goods differentiation**: Varying product features, quality, or augmented features (extra warranty, free delivery, additional features).
- **Services differentiation**: Varying time spent, level of expertise, qualifications/experience of provider, quality of materials/technology.
- **Cross branding/strategic alliances**: e.g., Woolworths + Caltex, Coles + Shell.

### Goods and Services in Different Industries

| Category | Examples | Operations Considerations |
|----------|----------|--------------------------|
| Standardised goods | Mass produced on assembly line | Focus on efficiency, automation, economies of scale |
| Customised goods | Varied to customer needs | Flexible processes, skilled labour, higher cost |
| Perishable goods | Food, flowers | Short lead times, cold storage, quality/safety, fast distribution |
| Non-perishable goods | Electronics, canned food | Manage quality from sourcing to distribution, responsive to demand |
| Intermediate goods | Screws used in furniture | Processed once, then used in final product |
| Standardised services | Fast food | Consistency, speed |
| Customised services | Accountants, medical, legal | Expertise, personalisation |
| Self-service | Online banking, self-checkouts | Customer-initiated, reduces labour costs |

## 2. INPUTS → TRANSFORMATION → OUTPUTS

### Inputs

Inputs are split into **Transformed** and **Transforming** resources.

#### Transformed Resources (inputs that ARE changed)
- **Materials and intermediate goods**: Physical items used to create products (raw materials like wood, metal; intermediate goods like computer chips).
- **Information**: Internal and external data/knowledge guiding decision-making and process improvement.
- **Customers**: Their needs, preferences, and feedback shape operations. Customers' state/experience is transformed by the service (e.g., haircut, medical treatment).

#### Transforming Resources (inputs that CARRY OUT the change)
- **Human Resources**: Skills, knowledge, and efforts of employees. Well-designed HR management policies improve operations performance. Key aspects:
  - Skill and training → accuracy, productivity
  - Motivation and engagement → quality outputs, better customer service
  - Teamwork and communication → reduced delays, improved workflow
- **Facilities**: Plant (factory/office) and machinery. Major decisions include:
  - Design layout of facilities
  - Number of facilities to use
  - Location and capacity

### Outputs
- **Final goods and/or services**
- **Customer service**: How well a business meets/exceeds customer expectations. Central to ensuring the right goods/service is delivered at the right place at the right time.
- **Warranties**: Claims made against goods with defects from transformation issues. Measuring warranty claims is a good way to assess effectiveness of operations.

## 3. THE 4Vs OF TRANSFORMATION

The 4Vs describe characteristics of the transformation process that influence how operations are structured.

### Volume
- How much product is made.
- **High volume**: Focuses on efficiency, standardisation, automation, lower unit costs.
- **Low volume**: More manual work, skilled labour, higher per-unit costs.
- **Volume flexibility**: How quickly the process can adjust to increases/decreases in demand.
- **Responsiveness to changes**: Essential for managing lead times (time from order to fulfilment).

### Variety
- Also known as product range or mix flexibility.
- **High variety**: More complex processes, diverse inputs, flexible machinery, skilled employees, higher costs.
- **Low variety**: Simpler, more efficient production.
- The greater the variety, the more operation processes needed to allow for variation.

### Variation (in Demand)
- Changes in customer demand significantly impact transformation resources.
- **Increased demand** requires: more supplies, more HR, more energy, more machinery/technology use.
- **Decreased demand** leads to: wasted resources, excess inventory, underutilised facilities.
- Accurate forecasting is vital (operations depends on marketing for sales forecasts).

### Visibility (Customer Contact)
- Customer contact and feedback directly affect the transformation process.
- **Direct contact**: Surveys, interviews, warranty claims, letters, blogs, verbal contact.
- **Indirect contact**: Review of sales figures, observation of decision-making, customer reviews.
- **High visibility**: Requires skilled service staff, responsiveness, quality customer interaction.
- **Low visibility**: Prioritises speed, consistency, cost control.

### 4Vs Summary Table

| V | Impact on Operations | Business Implication |
|---|---------------------|---------------------|
| Volume | Determines output levels and production structure | Plan capacity, resources, responsiveness |
| Variety | Affects flexibility and process complexity | Choose right product mix without overcomplicating |
| Variation | Affects inventory, scheduling, supplier coordination | Accurate forecasting, flexible planning |
| Visibility | Shapes product/service design and quality focus | Effective feedback systems, maintain satisfaction |

## 4. TECHNOLOGY AND LAYOUT

### Technology

Technology is the design, construction, and/or application of innovative devices, methods, and machinery upon operation processes.

**Classification:**
- **Leading Edge**: Brand new innovative technology not yet fully tested (e.g., facial recognition when first introduced).
- **Established**: Technology that has been tested, used, and proven in industry (e.g., self-service checkouts).

**Uses in Operations:**
1. Administration: GANTT charts, software packages, word processing
2. Operations processes: Large machines, robotics, Computer Aided Design (CAD)

**Impact on Operations:**
- More efficient — produces more products than labour → reduces lead time
- After initial cost, saves money by substituting labour for capital → achieve cost leadership
- Produces more consistent quality than humans, less waste/defects → product differentiation and cost leadership
- Faster rate, fewer errors, consistent quality
- Long-term labour cost savings despite higher upfront investment

### Imitation vs Innovation
- **Imitation**: Creating products that already exist but at lower cost (reverse engineering).
- **Innovation**: Creating new products, leading the market; operations and supply chain shaped around the need for innovation.

### Task Design

Task design involves classifying job activities in ways that make it easy for an employee to successfully perform and complete the task.

**Steps in Task Design:**
1. Define what needs to be done (general statement)
2. Analyse the general job into specific duties
3. Allocate a degree of difficulty and a time element
4. Match tasks to existing state/federal awards
5. Articulate tasks via job descriptors and a pay scale

### Layout

#### Process Layout
- Machines and equipment grouped together **by function** they perform.
- For **intermittent production**: high variety, low volume; different sequence of production for each product.
- Examples: Hospitals (areas for particular medical care), printeries, banks.
- Flexible but can be slower.

#### Product Layout
- Used for **mass production** of consistent quality products.
- **Assembly line** is the most common — aims for best combination of personnel and machine use.
- Faster and more efficient but less flexible.
- Example: Foxconn factories mass-producing iPhones.

#### Fixed Position Layout
- For industries like **construction** where large equipment stays in the same place.
- Materials, workers, and equipment come to the one work area.
- More efficient to bring materials to site rather than moving the product.

| Layout Type | Best For | Key Feature | Example |
|-------------|----------|-------------|---------|
| Process | High variety, low volume | Grouped by function | Hospital, bank |
| Product | High volume, consistent quality | Assembly line sequence | Car factory, Foxconn |
| Fixed Position | Large immovable products | Workers/equipment come to site | Construction |

## 5. MONITORING, CONTROLLING AND IMPROVEMENT

### Monitoring
- The process of **measuring actual performance against planned performance**.
- Typically arranged around **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**:
  - Lead times/wait times
  - Inventory turnover
  - Defect rates, repair rates, warranty claims
  - Capacity and volume rates
  - Direct/indirect cost analysis
  - Process flow rates

### Controlling
- Occurs **after KPIs are assessed** against predetermined targets.
- Investigates reasons for variations from targets.
- Important part of operations management, tends to focus around **quality**.
- Corrective actions: retraining staff, changing materials, improving inspection processes, stricter quality checks.

### Improvement
- **Systematic reduction** of inefficiencies, wastage, poor work processes, and elimination of bottlenecks.
- Improvements sought in:
  - **Time**: Minimisation of bottlenecks
  - **Process flows**
  - **Quality**
  - **Cost**: Per unit costs, review of expenses
  - **Efficiency**: Reduction of waste, greater output per unit of input

#### Continuous Improvement
- Ongoing commitment to achieving perfection.
- For success, staff must be involved.
- Helps business obtain and maintain competitive advantage.

## 6. PERFORMANCE OBJECTIVES

Performance objectives are the goals that the operations function is trying to achieve.

### Quality
- **Quality of Design**: Arises from understanding consumers and their preferences.
- **Quality of Conformance**: How well the product meets the standard of prescribed design with certain specifications; how consistently products achieve compliance.

### Speed
- Time it takes for production/operations processes to respond to market changes.
- Aims to satisfy customer demands as quickly as possible.
- Goals: reduced wait times, shorter lead times, faster processing times.

### Dependability
- How **consistent and reliable** a business's products are.
- Fewer warranty claims and complaints = higher dependability.

### Flexibility
- How quickly operation processes can **adjust to changes** in the market.
- Quicker processing time → greater likelihood of quick adjustment.
- Includes: mix flexibility, volume flexibility, new product flexibility.

### Customisation
- Creation of **individualised products** to meet specific customer needs.
- **Mass customisation**: Allows a standard, mass-produced item to be modified to a specific customer requirement.

### Cost
- **Minimisation of expenses** so operations processes are conducted as cheaply as possible.
- All KPIs based around minimisation of cost.

### Performance Objectives Summary

| Objective | Focus | Key Benefit |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| Quality | Design + conformance standards | Customer satisfaction, brand reputation |
| Speed | Fast response to demand | Reduced lead times, competitive advantage |
| Dependability | Consistency and reliability | Fewer defects, trust |
| Flexibility | Adjusting to market changes | Responsiveness, adaptability |
| Customisation | Individualised products | Customer loyalty, differentiation |
| Cost | Minimising expenses | Profit maximisation, price competitiveness |

## 7. INFLUENCES ON OPERATIONS

### 7.1 Globalisation

**Definition**: The removal of barriers of trade between nations.

**Characteristics:**
- Increasing integration between national economies
- High degree of transfer of capital, labour, intellectual capital, ideas, financial resources, and technology

**Impact on Operations:**
- Large businesses orient practices towards global markets
- Operations structured around a series of **global production facilities**
- Consumers seek global brands and standardised products (e.g., McDonald's, Apple, Samsung)

**Opportunities:**
- Access to global supply chain (global web)
- Economies of scale through standardised production

**Threats:**
- Intensified competition from global competitors
- Foreign competitors entering domestic market easily

### 7.2 Technology (as an Influence)

- Technology is the design, construction, and application of innovative devices, methods, and machinery.
- Enables higher productivity through increased efficiency
- Reduces production costs through reduced reliance on human labour
- Produces products faster with fewer errors and less waste
- Consistent quality output
- Long-term labour cost savings

### 7.3 Quality Expectations

**Definition**: The totality of features and characteristics of goods and services that bears its ability to satisfy stated or implied needs.

**Quality Expectations — Goods:**
1. Quality of Design
2. Fitness for Purpose (product does what it was bought to do)
3. Durability (how long it lasts)

**Quality Expectations — Services:**
1. Professionalism of service provider
2. Reliability of service provider
3. Level of customisation

**Impact**: Directly impacts customer satisfaction, brand reputation, and profitability. Failure leads to customer dissatisfaction, returns, warranty claims, loss of market share.

### 7.4 Cost-Based Competition

- Derived from determining break-even point and applying strategies to create cost advantages.
- Reducing cost is the main way to maximise profitability.
- Methods: eliminate waste, standardised products, high volume output, automated production, economies of scale, bulk buying.

### 7.5 Government Policies and Legal Regulations

**Government Policies:**
- Environmental policies (carbon reduction)
- WH&S standards (Workplace Health and Safety)
- Employment relations
- Taxation
- Trade and industry policies
- Labelling requirements (country of origin)

**Legal Regulations (Compliance):**
- WHS Act 2012 (Commonwealth)
- Training and Development requirements
- Fair Work and Anti-Discrimination laws (Racial Discrimination Act 1975, Anti-Discrimination Act 1977 NSW, Fair Work Act 2009)
- Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999
- Competition and Consumer Act 2010
- Food Safety Act 2003 (NSW)

**Compliance costs**: Expenses associated with meeting legal requirements.

### 7.6 Environmental Sustainability

- Business operations should consume resources today without compromising access for future generations.
- Integrate long-term sustainable view into business planning.

**Business approaches:**
- Minimising waste
- Recycling
- Reducing carbon footprint
- Greenhouse abatement measures

**Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR):**
- Open and accountable business actions based on respect for people, community, and environment.
- **Triple bottom line**: Financial returns + social responsibility + environmental sustainability.

**Legal Compliance vs Ethical Responsibility:**
- **Legal compliance**: Following the letter of the law (prescribed standards)
- **Ethical responsibility**: Following the intention and 'spirit' of the law; broader social, community, environmental concerns.
- How businesses decide what is ethical: consulting special interest groups, following international guidelines (ILO, Human Rights organisations).

## 8. SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT (SCM)

**Definition**: The integration and management of the flow of supplies throughout the inputs, transformation processes, and outputs to best meet the needs of customers.

### Sourcing
- Purchasing of inputs for transformation processes.
- **Factors influencing choice of suppliers:**
  1. Consumer demand
  2. Quality of inputs required
  3. Flexibility and timeliness of supply
  4. Cost of supplier

### The Global Web
- Network of suppliers based on:
  - Lowest overall cost
  - Lowest risk
  - Maximum certainty in quality and timing of supplies

### Trends in SCM

1. **Supplier Rationalisation**: Assessing suppliers, determining which can be better utilised, eliminating ineffective ones. Less contracting, less wastage/duplication, improved timeliness.
2. **Backwards Vertical Integration**: Purchasing suppliers through mergers and acquisitions.
3. **Cost Minimisation**: Using offshore suppliers of lower cost.
4. **Flexible/Responsive Supply Chain**: Ordering supplies only when needed, using them optimally.

### E-Commerce in SCM
- Buying/selling goods and services via the internet.
- **E-procurement** systems manage supply chains.
- **B2B** (business to business): Automatic supply when stock falls to predetermined level.
- **B2C** (business to customer): Direct selling to consumers.

### Global Sourcing
- Purchasing supplies/services without being constrained by location.
- **Advantages**: Lower material costs, reduced lead times (materials closer to production), access to materials unavailable domestically.
- **Disadvantages**: Could lead to reduced quality, negatively impacts domestic businesses.

### Logistics
1. **Distribution**: Getting goods/services to the customer.
2. **Transportation**: Physical movement of inventories — type of product and cost determine method.
3. **Storage**: Secure place to hold stock until required.
   - **Warehousing**: Facility for storage, protection, and distribution of stock.
   - **Distribution Centres**: Strategically located to minimise time to supply retail outlets.
4. **Materials Handling and Packaging**: Standards and methods for movement and storage of goods; protects from damage, ensures safe/efficient movement.

## 9. INVENTORY MANAGEMENT

**Definition**: Inventory (stock) refers to the amount of raw materials, work-in-progress, and finished goods a business has on hand at any point in time.

### Holding Stock (Just in Case — JIC)

**Advantages:**
1. Consumer demand can be met when stock available
2. Alternative products can be offered if a line runs out
3. Reduces lead times between order and delivery
4. Opportunity to generate immediate revenue
5. Stock can be distributed to distribution centres
6. Promote use of products in non-traditional/new markets
7. Older stock sold at reduced prices
8. Stocks are an asset (shown on Balance Sheet)
9. Bulk production may reduce costs

**Disadvantages:**
1. Holding costs: storage charges, spoilage, insurance, theft, handling expenses
2. Invested capital/labour/energy could be used elsewhere
3. Cost of obsolescence (no demand for product)

### Just in Time (JIT)
- Lean production method: only make enough products to meet demand.
- **Advantages**: Reduced storage/warehousing costs, reduced theft/damage risk, stock shouldn't become obsolete.
- **Disadvantages**: Increase in lead time due to variation in demand, issues getting stock from suppliers in time, risk of production shutdown if supplies delayed.

### Inventory Valuation Methods

#### FIFO (First In, First Out)
- Stock bought **first** is sold **first**.
- When costs are **increasing**: FIFO → higher COGS, lower profit (use to minimise tax).
- When costs are **decreasing**: FIFO → lower COGS, higher profit (use to maximise reported profit).

#### LIFO (Last In, First Out)
- Stock bought **last** is sold **first**.
- When costs are **increasing**: LIFO → lower COGS, higher profit (use to maximise reported profit).
- When costs are **decreasing**: LIFO → higher COGS, lower profit (use to minimise tax).

#### Weighted Average
- Calculates the average cost of all inventory available for sale.
- Smooths out price fluctuations.

#### EOQ (Economic Order Quantity)
- Mathematical formula to determine the **optimal order quantity** that minimises total inventory costs (ordering costs + holding costs).
- Balances the cost of ordering too frequently vs. holding too much stock.

### Inventory Valuation Strategy Summary

| Goal | Costs Increasing | Costs Decreasing |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Minimise profit (reduce tax) | LIFO | FIFO |
| Maximise profit (attract investors) | FIFO | LIFO |

**Important distinction:**
- If asked about **inventory management strategy** → discuss Holding Stock (JIC) or JIT.
- If asked about **inventory valuation method** → discuss FIFO and LIFO.

## 10. QUALITY MANAGEMENT

**Definition**: Processes a business undertakes to ensure consistency, reliability, safety, and fitness of purpose of product.

### Quality Control
- Reduces problems and defects by using **inspection** at various points in production.
- Steps: Define quality standards → Set predetermined targets → Take corrective action if targets not met.

### Quality Assurance (QA)
- Use of a **system** to ensure set standards are achieved in production.
- Key aspects:
  - **Fitness for purpose**: Product does what it is supposed to do.
  - **Right the first time**: Avoid wasting time, energy, and resources.
- International standards: **ISO 9000** accreditation ensures products meet the same standards globally.

### Quality Improvement

#### Continuous Improvement
- Belief that over time, processes will be made more efficient and effective.
- Staff involvement is essential for success.
- Helps obtain and maintain competitive advantage.

#### Total Quality Management (TQM)
- Focuses on managing the **total business** to deliver quality to customers.
- **Holistic approach**: Quality becomes both a commitment and the responsibility of **every employee**.
- Success depends on commitment from everyone in the business.

#### Six Sigma
- Data-driven methodology to **eliminate defects** and reduce variability in processes.
- Aims for near-perfect quality (3.4 defects per million opportunities).
- Uses DMAIC framework: Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control.

#### Kaizen
- Japanese philosophy of **continuous, incremental improvement**.
- Involves all employees from top management to frontline workers.
- Small, ongoing changes rather than large-scale transformations.
- Focus on eliminating waste (muda) in all forms.

### Quality Circles
- Small groups of employees who meet regularly to identify, analyse, and solve work-related problems.
- Encourage participation and ownership of quality at all levels.

### Quality Management Comparison

| Approach | Focus | Scope |
|----------|-------|-------|
| Quality Control | Inspection and defect detection | Production stage |
| Quality Assurance | Systems to prevent defects | Entire production system |
| TQM | Whole business commitment to quality | All employees, all functions |
| Six Sigma | Data-driven defect elimination | Process variation reduction |
| Kaizen | Continuous incremental improvement | Every level of organisation |
| Quality Circles | Employee problem-solving groups | Specific work areas |

## 11. OUTSOURCING

**Definition**: The use of **external providers** to perform business activities. External specialists can perform services at lower cost and with greater effectiveness than in-house.

Can be both **domestic** and **global**.

**Functions that can be outsourced:**
- Production, marketing/advertising/market research, packaging, customer service, maintenance.

### Advantages
1. Simplification of operations
2. Efficiency and cost savings
3. Increased process capability
4. Increased accountability (contractual obligations)
5. Access to skills/resources lacking within the business
6. Capacity to focus on core business and key competencies
7. Improvements to in-house performance
8. Other strategic benefits

### Disadvantages
1. Communication and language barriers
2. Loss of control of standards and information security
3. Hierarchies and coordination challenges
4. Organisational change and redesign
5. Loss of corporate memory and vulnerability (information risk to competitors)
6. Time and cost of IT systems

## 13. OVERCOMING RESISTANCE TO CHANGE

Resistance to change arises from two principal sources:

### Financial Costs of Change
1. **Purchasing new equipment**: High initial cost, but long-term benefits outweigh.
2. **Redundancy payouts**: Money paid to people made redundant as skills no longer needed.
3. **Retraining**: Required due to new technology or restructuring.
4. **Reorganising plant layout**: Physical costs of rearranging facilities.

### Psychological/Emotional Resistance (Inertia)
- **Inertia**: Feeling of uncertainty or fear of the unknown when change is imminent.
- Effects chain: Workers less motivated → less productivity → impacts quality → increased lead time and defects → customers may no longer buy from business.

### Change Management Strategies
1. **Identify source(s) of change** and assess need to accommodate through adjustments to business processes.
2. **Lower resistance** through communicating with employees about the need to change; get widespread support.
3. Use **change agents** (influential people who champion the change).
4. Apply **change models** (structured frameworks for managing transition).

## 14. NEW PRODUCT DESIGN AND DEVELOPMENT

### Approaches to Product Design
1. **Consumer approach**: Preferences and desires of consumers determine which products are designed and developed.
2. **Technology/Innovation approach**: Changes and innovations in technology enable new, appealing products with greater functionality.

### Steps in Product Design
1. Market research, product concept, and specification development
2. Product design and prototype development with quality parameters decided
3. Prototype testing and assessment
4. Product refinement and production processes refined
5. Production, product launch, distribution, and product line extension

### Important Considerations
- **Supply chain management**: New products may require inputs from new suppliers; impact on timing and volume of supplies.
- **Quality**: Must maintain standard of quality customers expect.
- **Capacity**: May need to increase use/range of resources or invest in new technology/machinery.
- **Costs**: Involved in producing new products.

### Service Design and Development
- Customer/client is the starting point.
- **Explicit service**: Tangible aspect (time, expertise, skill, effort).
- **Implicit service**: Intangible feeling (psychological wellbeing, feeling of being looked after).
- Cost determined after explicit and implicit aspects considered.

## 15. SEQUENCING AND SCHEDULING

### Sequencing
- The **order** in which activities in the operation processes occur.

### Scheduling
- The **length of time** activities take within the operations process.

### GANTT Charts
- Outlines: activities to be performed, order of performance, expected duration of each activity.
- **Advantages:**
  - Visual representation of project timeline
  - Shows which activities can be performed **concurrently**
  - Helps identify bottlenecks and resource allocation needs
  - Easy to understand and communicate to team

### Critical Path Analysis (CPA)
- Shows what needs to be done, how long tasks take, and the necessary order.
- **Critical path**: The **shortest time** to complete all tasks.
- Identifies which tasks are critical (cannot be delayed without delaying entire project) and which have **float** (can be delayed).
- Helps operations managers allocate resources efficiently to critical tasks.

## 16. HSC RESPONSE FRAMEWORKS

### Describe (Features and Characteristics)
- Definition of key concept/answer the question
- Provide characteristics and features (at least 2)
- Use linking language ("Furthermore", "Additionally")

### Explain (Cause and Effect)
1. **Answer the question** in a sentence
2. **How does this happen?** (causes)
3. **Why does this happen/why is this important?** (effects)
4. **Link to topic-specific goal** (what does this mean for the business?)
5. **Link to long-term business goal** (profit maximisation, sustainable growth)

### Distinguish (Differences)
- Definition 1
- Definition 2
- Differences (use connective words: "whereas", "however", "on the other hand")
- Link back to the question

### Outline (Sketch in General Terms)
- Answer the question
- Provide additional details (definition, description)
- Keep it concise but informative

### Assess (Make a Judgement)
1. **Judgement sentence** (state position with scope)
2. **How** is it impacting on the process?
3. **Effect** on the business
4. **What does it mean** for the business (implication)?
5. **Restate judgement** (considered position)

### Analyse (Show Relationship and Draw Out Implications)
- Define key concept
- How does X impact Y?
- Effect: link to performance objective
- Implication: what does it mean for the business?

### Discuss (Issues + Advantages + Disadvantages)
1. Identify an issue
2. Advantages (prove by linking to topic-specific goal, give the 'how')
3. Disadvantages (prove by linking to topic-specific goal, give the 'how')
4. Link to topic and business goals

## Apple Case Study — Key Examples

### Inputs
- **Transformed**: Touch screens, memory chips, circuitry, speakers, audio chips; information for design; customer preferences/feedback (e.g., in-cell display on iPhone 5)
- **Transforming**: Human resources and factories in China (Foxconn); large-scale factories achieving economies of scale

### 4Vs
- **Volume**: High-volume production at Foxconn mega factories; forecasting demand with customer surveys and consumer panels
- **Variety**: Wide product range (iPhone, iPad, MacBook, Apple Watch, AirPods, software, services); requires different production lines and skilled workers
- **Variation**: Smartphone market subject to rapid changes; over-ordering risks obsolescence, under-ordering delays production
- **Visibility**: Customer expectations drive design (thinner phone, better camera, longer battery life); any quality/ethical issues quickly affect reputation

### Quality Issues
- 5 million iPhones returned to Foxconn due to defects (2013) — lack of worker training, $1.6bn cost
- Quality Assurance teams troubleshoot before launch
- Defective Apple Maps (2012), problematic facial recognition on iPhone X (2018)

### Outsourcing to Foxconn
- **Advantages**: Lower production cost, technical expertise, economies of scale, Apple focuses on core activities, proximity to Asian market
- **Disadvantages**: Loss of quality control, security risks, dependency on partner, communication/cultural barriers, reputation affected by partner actions

### Supply Chain
- Components sourced from USA, Korea, Taiwan, Europe, China, Inner Mongolia
- 785 suppliers in 31 countries (349 in China), ~200 account for 97% of supply chain
- Multiple suppliers for same component to reduce delays
- COVID-19: factory lockdowns delayed iPhone 12 (2020), semiconductor chip shortage (2021)
- Tim Cook streamlined inefficiencies, reduced suppliers/warehouses, uses inventory tracking

### Cost Objectives
- iPhone SE with cheaper components
- In-house development of graphics/audio chips
- Switched from Samsung to TSMC for memory/processors (2014)
- Moved iPhone SE production from China to India (2017) — eliminated 20% customs duty
- Vertical integration: Purchased Intel's 5G modem division (2019)
