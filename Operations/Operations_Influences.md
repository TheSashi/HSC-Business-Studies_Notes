# Influences on Operations

> **HSC Business Studies — Operations** | Globalisation, technology, quality expectations, cost-based competition, legal requirements, and environmental sustainability.

---

## 1. Globalisation

**Globalisation** = removal of barriers of trade between nations.

Characterised by:
- Increasing integration between national economies
- High degree of transfer of capital, labour, intellectual capital, ideas, financial resources and technology

### Impact on Operations
- Large businesses orient practices towards **global markets**
- Consumers seek **global brands** and **standardised products** (McDonald's, Apple, Samsung)
- Operations structured around **global production facilities**
- **Global web** of suppliers based on lowest cost, lowest risk, maximum certainty

### Opportunities
- Access to global supply chain
- Economies of scale through standardised production
- Lower production costs through global sourcing

### Threats
- Increased competition from foreign businesses
- Currency fluctuation risks
- Supply chain vulnerability

---

## 2. Technology

Technology transforms operations through:

| Impact Area | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Automation** | Replacing human labour with machines/robots |
| **Information Systems** | ERP, MRP, CRM systems for managing operations |
| **Communication** | Faster, more efficient coordination |
| **Product Design** | CAD/CAM, 3D printing, rapid prototyping |
| **Quality Control** | Automated inspection, sensors, data analytics |

---

## 3. Quality Expectations

- Consumers increasingly demand **high quality** products
- Global competition raises quality standards
- **ISO certifications** and international standards
- Quality is now a **competitive necessity**, not a differentiator

---

## 4. Cost-Based Competition

Derived from determining **break-even point** and applying strategies to create cost advantages.

### Break-Even Analysis

**Formula:** Break-Even (units) = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price - Variable Cost per unit)

### Cost Reduction Strategies
- Economies of scale
- Global sourcing
- Outsourcing
- Technology and automation
- Lean production (eliminating waste)

---

## 5. Legal Requirements

| Law | Impact on Operations |
|-----|---------------------|
| **WHS Act** | Workplace health and safety standards |
| **Environmental Protection** | Waste management, emissions, sustainability |
| **Consumer Protection** | Product safety, labelling, warranties |
| **Industrial Relations** | Fair Work Act, awards, conditions |
| **Trade Practices** | Competition law, anti-competitive behaviour |

---

## 6. Environmental Sustainability

Operations must consider:
- **Waste reduction** — minimise materials used
- **Recycling** — reuse materials where possible
- **Energy efficiency** — reduce energy consumption
- **Sustainable sourcing** — use environmentally responsible suppliers
- **Carbon footprint** — reduce greenhouse gas emissions

> Businesses increasingly adopt **triple bottom line** — people, planet, profit.

---

## 7. Cost-Based, Legal and Environmental Sustainability — Key Exam Points

When answering questions about influences:
1. **Identify** the influence (globalisation, technology, etc.)
2. **Explain** how it affects the operations function
3. **Provide examples** from case studies
4. **Link** to business objectives (profit maximisation, competitive advantage)
