# Operations Strategies

> **HSC Business Studies — Operations** | Quality management, inventory management, supply chain management, outsourcing, new product design, overcoming resistance to change, and global factors.

---

## 1. Quality Management

Processes to ensure consistency, reliability, safety and fitness of purpose.

### Quality Control
- Reduces problems and defects using **inspection** at various production points
- Define quality standards → set predetermined targets → take corrective action

### Quality Assurance
- System ensuring set standards are achieved in production
- **Fitness for purpose** — product does what it's supposed to
- **Right the first time** — avoid wasting time, energy, resources
- **ISO 9000** — international standards for quality accreditation

### Quality Improvement

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Continuous Improvement (Kaizen)** | Ongoing incremental improvements; staff involvement essential |
| **Total Quality Management (TQM)** | Holistic approach; quality is commitment and responsibility of every employee |

## 2. Inventory Management

**Inventory/stock** = raw materials, work-in-progress and finished goods on hand.

### Holding Stock — Advantages
- Meet consumer demand when stock available
- Offer alternative products if line runs out
- Reduces lead times
- Immediate revenue generation
- Bulk production reduces costs
- Stock is an asset on Balance Sheet

### Holding Stock — Disadvantages
- Storage costs, spoilage, insurance, theft, handling
- Capital tied up could be used elsewhere
- Cost of obsolescence (no demand)

### Inventory Valuation Methods

| Method | Description | Effect (Rising Prices) |
|--------|-------------|----------------------|
| **FIFO** (First In First Out) | Oldest stock sold first | Higher profit (lower COGS) |
| **LIFO** (Last In First Out) | Newest stock sold first | Lower profit (higher COGS) |

### Just-in-Time (JIT)
- Inventory arrives **just when needed** for production
- Minimises holding costs
- Risk: supply chain disruptions can halt production

## 3. Supply Chain Management (SCM)

Integration and management of flow of supplies through inputs, transformation and outputs.

### Sourcing Factors
1. Consumer demand
2. Quality of inputs required
3. Flexibility and timeliness of supply
4. Cost of supplier

### Trends in SCM

| Trend | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Supplier Rationalisation** | Assess and reduce number of suppliers; less contracting, wastage, duplication |
| **Backwards Vertical Integration** | Purchasing suppliers through mergers/acquisitions |
| **Cost Minimisation** | Using offshore suppliers for lower costs |
| **Flexible/Responsive SCM** | Order supplies only when needed; use optimally |

### E-Commerce in SCM
- **E-procurement** systems manage supply chain
- **B2B:** Automatic supply when stock falls to predetermined level
- **B2C:** Direct business-to-customer sales

### Global Sourcing
- Purchasing supplies/services without location constraints
- **Advantages:** Lower costs, reduced lead times, access to unavailable domestic materials
- **Disadvantages:** Potential quality reduction, negative impact on domestic businesses

### Logistics
- **Distribution:** Getting goods/services to customer
- **Transportation:** Physical movement of inventories
- **Warehousing:** Storage of inventory

## 4. Outsourcing

Using external providers to perform business activities.

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|-----------|-------------|
| Cost reduction | Less control over quality |
| Access to specialists | Dependency on external provider |
| Focus on core business | Potential job losses |
| Flexibility | Communication challenges |

## 5. New Product Design and Development

### Approaches

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Consumer Approach** | Product development driven by customer needs and market research |
| **Technology Approach** | Product development driven by new technology capabilities |

### Product Design Process
1. **Idea generation** — brainstorm concepts
2. **Screening** — evaluate feasibility
3. **Concept testing** — test with target market
4. **Business analysis** — cost/benefit analysis
5. **Product development** — create prototype
6. **Test marketing** — trial in limited market
7. **Commercialisation** — full launch

## 6. Overcoming Resistance to Change

Resistance arises from two sources:

### Financial Costs
- Cost of new equipment/technology
- Training costs
- Potential downtime during transition

### Psychological and Emotional
- Fear of job loss
- Comfort with current processes
- Lack of understanding of benefits

### Strategies to Overcome Resistance
- **Communication** — explain reasons for change
- **Training** — provide skills for new processes
- **Involvement** — include employees in decision-making
- **Gradual implementation** — phase in changes slowly
- **Support** — provide resources and assistance
