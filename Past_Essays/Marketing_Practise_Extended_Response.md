## Completed Essay — Analyse the Impact of Influences on Achieving the Strategic Role of Marketing

### Introduction

Apple Inc. is one of the largest technology companies in the world, built on product innovation and brand loyalty to drive its marketing strategy. Apple's strategic roles of marketing centre on profit maximisation, increasing market share, and entering new markets through premium product positioning and global expansion. This response will analyse how economic factors that influence consumer choice, price discrimination under the Competition and Consumer Act 2010, and ethical issues surrounding truth and accuracy in advertising affect Apple's ability to achieve these strategic roles.

### Body Paragraph 1 — Implied Conditions under the CCA

Implied conditions under the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 can impact on Apple's achievement of their strategic role of marketing through increasing sales. Implied conditions are conditions that are required under law to be met for every product sold even if it is not written down, such as fitness for particular purpose.

Under the implied condition of fitness for a particular purpose, Apple must ensure that its products can perform the functions that they were made for. For example, the iPhone should be able to make and receive messages and calls. If the iPhone is unable to perform its prime function, this becomes a legal issue and the customer is entitled to receive a refund or replacement. This costs Apple directly in lost revenue and replacement stock. Beyond the immediate financial hit, it damages their brand reputation, which means customers are less likely to buy from them again or recommend Apple products to others.

Apple is also required under implied conditions to ensure that their product matches what they have stated on the packaging and in their advertisements. Apple had an issue with claims that their iPhones had more storage than they actually had, because the operating system took up a large portion of the advertised space. This breached the implied conditions under the CCA and meant that they could face fines for misleading their customers, which would further damage consumer trust.

Since Apple has breached the implied conditions of the CCA and has been required to refund or replace products and pay fines, this has cost them money. Combined with the reputational damage, customers become reluctant to purchase Apple products or recommend them to potential new buyers which is a huge way sales occur. Over time, this erodes their customer base and undermines their ability to increase sales and achieve profit maximisation. Apple must follow the implied conditions under the CCA so that they can maintain and grow their sales to support their broader marketing objectives.

### Body Paragraph 2 — Economic Factors Influencing Consumer Choice

Economic factors that influence consumer choice can affect Apple's ability to achieve their strategic role of increasing market share. Economic factors refer to conditions such as consumer income levels, inflation, interest rates, and the general state of the economy that shape how much people are willing and able to spend.

When economic conditions worsen, such as during a recession or period of high inflation, consumers have less disposable income. Apple's products are priced at a premium compared to competitors like Samsung or Xiaomi, which means they are particularly vulnerable during downturns. For example, during the cost of living pressures in 2022 and 2023, many consumers shifted towards cheaper alternatives or held onto their existing phones for longer instead of upgrading. Apple reported slower iPhone sales growth in several markets during this period, showing how sensitive their revenue is to consumer spending power. When fewer people can afford Apple products, the company struggles to grow its market share against Android competitors who offer devices at lower price points.

During periods of economic growth and rising wages, though, consumers are more willing to spend on premium goods. Apple benefits from this because their brand positioning appeals to aspirational buyers who see Apple products as a status symbol. The relationship works both ways: a strong economy pulls consumers towards Apple, while a weak economy pushes them towards cheaper options that still meet their basic needs.

The long term implication is that Apple's reliance on premium pricing makes its goal of increasing market share dependent on broader economic conditions that the company cannot control. If Apple wants to protect its market share during downturns, it may need to introduce more affordable product lines, something it has started doing with the iPhone SE range and older model discounts. Without adapting to economic pressures, Apple risks losing market share to competitors who can serve price sensitive consumers more effectively, which limits their ability to enter new markets where average incomes are lower.

### Body Paragraph 3 — Ethical Issues, Truth and Accuracy in Advertising

Ethical issues relating to truth and accuracy in advertising can impact on Apple's achievement of their strategic role of marketing through entering new markets. Ethical advertising requires that businesses present their products honestly, without exaggerating capabilities or concealing limitations.

Apple has faced criticism for how it advertises certain product features. For instance, Apple's marketing of battery life on iPhones and MacBooks has been questioned by consumers and tech reviewers who found that real world performance often fell short of what was claimed in advertisements. When a company overstates how long a battery lasts, even if it is not technically illegal, it raises ethical concerns about whether consumers are being given accurate information to make purchasing decisions. This matters even more when Apple tries to enter new markets in developing countries, where consumers may be less familiar with the brand and rely heavily on advertising claims to judge whether a premium priced product is worth the cost.

If Apple's advertising is seen as misleading or exaggerated, word spreads quickly, especially through social media and online reviews. In new markets where Apple does not yet have strong brand loyalty, a reputation for overstating product capabilities can slow adoption rates. Consumers in these markets may choose established competitors they trust instead, which undermines Apple's goal of expanding into new territories.

Over time, persistent questions about advertising accuracy can also attract regulatory scrutiny. Advertising standards bodies in countries like Australia, the UK, and India have the power to force companies to pull ads or issue corrections, which generates negative publicity. For Apple, whose entire marketing strategy depends on a perception of quality and trustworthiness, even small ethical issues in advertising can have outsized consequences for their ability to grow internationally. Maintaining strict standards of truth and accuracy in their advertising is necessary for Apple to successfully enter new markets and sustain long term growth.

### Conclusion

Implied conditions under the CCA force Apple to meet minimum product standards or face financial penalties and reputational harm that reduce sales and limit profit maximisation. Economic factors outside Apple's control directly shape consumer purchasing power, making the company's premium pricing strategy a barrier to growing market share during downturns. Ethical issues around truth and accuracy in advertising affect Apple's credibility, which is especially damaging when the company is trying to build trust in new markets. These influences show that Apple's strategic roles of profit maximisation, increasing market share, and entering new markets are not achieved through marketing alone. They are shaped through legal, economic, and ethical forces that require constant attention and adaptation.
