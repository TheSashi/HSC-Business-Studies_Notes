In contrast to McDonald's, which recorded an expense ratio of approximately 30%, reflecting labour-intensive and store-based operations, Apple's lower expense ratio of 14.93% in 2025 indicates greater cost efficiency despite high spending on research and development and global services. While these expenses may support solvency, the higher expense ratio adds pressure on profitability. Therefore, Apple must continue improving cost control to ensure efficiency does not erode future profits.

### Efficiency of Collection from Customers - Accounts Receivable Turnover (Days)

Apple's accounts receivable efficiency has worsened between 2024 and 2025, as shown in the accounts receivable turnover. In 2024, Apple's accounts receivable balance was $33,410 million, with revenue of $391,035 million. This resulted in accounts receivable being collected approximately 32 days on average, only being above the ideal of 30 days. In 2025, accounts receivable increased to $39,777 million, while revenue rose to $416,161 million, worsening the collection period time to approximately 35 days. This increase indicates that Apple is taking longer to collect cash from customers and shareholders. The rise in receivables may be linked to expanded global sales and more flexible credit terms to attract more customers, particularly within the services and international markets. As a result Apple's cash is tied up for longer periods, reducing short term cash flow efficiency. Although Apple remains highly profitable, slower receivables collection may place pressure on liquidity if the trend continues. Therefore, Apple's cash flow efficiency has worsened slightly and required immediate management attention.

### Recommendation - Improve Accounts Receivable Turnover (Days)

It is recommended that Apple improves its financial performance and position by implementing factoring and discounts for early payments to manage its accounts receivable more efficiently and effectively. Factoring is a cash flow management strategy where a business sells its accounts receivable to a debt collection agency in exchange for immediate cash inflow. In 2025, Apple's accounts receivable increased from $33,410 million to $39,777 million, and the accounts receivable period worsened from approximately 31 days to 35 days, indicating slower cash collection. Factoring would allow Apple to receive cash sooner rather than waiting for customers to pay, improving liquidity. Discounts for early payments involve offering customers a small reduction in their accounts if they pay before the due date. This strategy would encourage faster settlement of accounts, although reducing Apple's profit margin it will give an immediate cash flow injection helping if there is a need for liquidity by reducing the time for cash to arrive. Although both strategies involve a cost to working capital (as they do not receive the full amount back), the faster inflow of cash improves liquidity, reduces the risk of late payments, and decreases reliance on short-term borrowing. Therefore, the combined use of factoring and early payment discounts would effectively improve Apple's cash flow, liquidity and overall financial stability.

### Conclusion

Overall, Apple's financial performance improved between 2024 and 2025 due to stronger profitability driven by revenue growth. However, efficiency and cash-flow management worsened, as shown by the expense ratio and slower accounts receivable turnover. By implementing factoring, Apple can improve cash flow efficiency, strengthen liquidity, and support long term financial stability.

## Attempt 2 (Refined)

### Executive Summary

Apple Inc. is a large global technology company that designs, manufactures and sells consumer electronics, software and digital services worldwide. This report assesses Apple's financial performance over the 2024 and 2025 financial years using the net profit ratio, expense ratio, and accounts receivable turnover (days). It also recommends the use of factoring as a cash flow management financial strategy to improve Apple's financial performance and position.

### Profitability - Net Profit Ratio

Apple's profitability improved between 2024 recording 23.97% and 2025 recording 26.92%, as shown by the net profit ratio, which measures how much profit is retained from revenue after expenses such as COGS and operational costs.

In 2024, Apple recorded a net profit of $93,736 million from revenue of $391,035 million, resulting in a net profit ratio of 23.97%, meaning Apple retained approximately 24 cents for every $1 of revenue. In 2025, net profit increased to $112,010 million while revenue rose to $416,161 million, improving the net profit ratio to 26.92%, or nearly 27 cents per dollar of revenue.

This improvement was driven by strong growth in services revenue, which increased from $96,169 million in 2024 to $109,158 million in 2025. Services such as Apple Music, iCloud, Apple TV+, and the App Store generate higher margins than physical products due to lower production costs and distribution costs. Although operating expenses increased, they rose more slowly than revenue, allowing Apple to retain a greater share of profit.

Compared to Samsung, which operates with a net profit ratio of approximately 10%, Apple's higher net profit ratio of 26.92% demonstrates significantly stronger profitability due to its focus on services rather than just products. As a result, Apple's improved profitability supports reinvestment, expansion, and long term growth.

### Efficiency - Expense Ratio

Apple's efficiency slightly worsened between 2024 and 2025 from 14.7% to 14.93%, as indicated by a minor increase in the expense ratio, which measures the proportion of revenue a business spends on operating expenses.

In 2024, Apple's total operating expenses were $57,467 million against revenue of $391,035 million, resulting in an expense ratio of 14.7%, meaning Apple spent around 15 cents for every $1 of revenue on operating costs. In 2025, operating expenses increased to $62,151 million, while revenue rose to $416,161 million, worsening the expense ratio to 14.93%.

The rise in expenses was mainly due to higher research and development costs, which increased from $31,370 million to $34,550 million, as well as selling marketing, general and administrative expenses, which rose from $26,097 million to $27,601 million. These expenses reflect Apple's continued investment in innovation and product development to drive future revenue growth.

In comparison to Samsung, which operates with a higher expense ratio due to its manufacturing focused business model, Apple's lower expense ratio indicates relatively strong cost efficiency. While the increase in expenses places some pressure on short-term profitability, the focus on research and development supports long term growth by improving product quality and competitiveness. Therefore, Apple must continue managing costs carefully to ensure efficiency does not limit future profitability.

### Efficiency of Collection from Customers - Accounts Receivable Turnover (Days)

Apple's accounts receivable efficiency has worsened between 2024 and 2025, as shown by an increase in the accounts receivable turnover period from approximately 32 days in 2024 to 35 days in 2025.

In 2024, Apple's accounts receivable balance was $33,410 million, with revenue of $391,035 million, resulting in an average collection period of around 32 days, which is slightly above/worse than the ideal benchmark of 30 days. In 2025, accounts receivable increased to $39,777 million, while revenue rose to $416,161 million, worsening the collection period to approximately 35 days.

This increase indicates that Apple is taking longer to collect cash from customers, likely due to expanded global sales and more flexible credit terms, particularly within services and international markets. As a result, Apple's cash is tied up for longer periods, reducing short-term cash flow efficiency, which impacts on short term liquidity.

Although Apple remains highly profitable, slower receivables collection may place increased pressure on liquidity if the trend continues. Therefore, Apple's cash flow efficiency has worsened and requires immediate management attention.

### Recommendation - Improve Accounts Receivable Turnover (Days)

It is recommended that Apple improve its financial performance and position by implementing factoring to manage its accounts receivable turnover (days) more efficiently.

Factoring is a cash flow management strategy where a business sells its accounts receivable to a debt collection agency in exchange for an immediate cash inflow. In 2025, Apple's accounts receivable increased from $33,410 million(2024), to $39,777 million, and the collection period worsened from approximately 32 days to 35 days, indicating slower cash collection. By using factoring, Apple would receive cash sooner rather than waiting for customers to pay, improving short term cash flow.

In addition, factoring transfers the responsibility of collecting outstanding debts to the debt collection agency. This reduces the time and resources Apple's staff spend chasing customer payments, allowing employees to focus on revenue generating activities such as sales growth, service expansion, and customer engagement. This also allows Apple to payout any short term loans if needed and acts and an emergency fund as well. Although factoring involves a fee and reduces the total amount received, the faster inflow of cash and improved operational efficiency outweigh the cost.

Therefore, factoring is an effective strategy to improve Apple's cash flow efficiency, revenue generation, and overall financial stability.

### Conclusion

Overall, Apple's financial performance improved between 2024 and 2025 due to stronger profitability driven by revenue growth. However, efficiency and cash-flow management worsened, as shown by the expense ratio and slower accounts receivable turnover. By implementing factoring, Apple can improve cash flow efficiency, strengthen liquidity, and support long term financial stability.
