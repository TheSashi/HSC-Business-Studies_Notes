# Assess How Influences on Marketing Impact the Marketing Process

> 2023 Marketing Section IV Extended Response. Apple case study. 5 body paragraphs.

---

## Introduction

Apple Inc. is one of the largest technology companies in the world, built on product innovation and brand loyalty to drive its marketing strategy. The marketing process consists of six stages: situational analysis, market research, identifying target markets, establishing marketing objectives, developing marketing strategies, and implementation, monitoring and controlling. This response will assess how economic factors, price discrimination under the Competition and Consumer Act 2010, deceptive and misleading advertising, ethical issues surrounding truth and accuracy in advertising, and implied conditions impact on different stages of Apple's marketing process.

## Body Paragraph 1 — Economic Factors → Market Research

Economic factors that influence consumer choice have a significant impact on Apple's marketing process stage of market research. Economic factors refer to conditions such as consumer income levels, inflation, interest rates, and the general state of the economy that shape how much people are willing and able to spend. During a recession or period of high inflation, consumers have less disposable income and become more cautious with spending, which means a business must collect data on consumer spending patterns, price sensitivity and income levels to make informed marketing decisions. For example, during the cost of living pressures in 2022 and 2023, Apple's market research revealed that consumers were shifting towards cheaper alternatives or holding onto their existing phones for longer instead of upgrading. This meant Apple needed to conduct research that accounted for these economic conditions so they could adjust their marketing strategies accordingly. Without this research Apple would not have identified the shift in consumer behaviour and would have lost market share to competitors like Samsung and Xiaomi who offer devices at lower price points. During periods of economic growth however, rising incomes and consumer confidence mean market research can focus on premium product demand and expansion opportunities. Therefore, economic influences have a substantial impact on market research because they determine what information a business needs to collect and how it interprets consumer data to remain competitive in changing economic conditions.

## Body Paragraph 2 — Price Discrimination → Identifying Target Markets

Price discrimination under the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 has a moderate impact on Apple's marketing process stage of identifying target markets. Price discrimination refers to the setting of different prices for a product in separate markets, which is prohibited under the CCA if it could substantially reduce competition. This impacts how a business identifies and segments target markets because the business must ensure that its pricing strategies across different geographic or demographic segments do not breach these provisions. For example, Apple segments its target market geographically, offering products across the Americas, Europe, and Greater China, and segments demographically by targeting young to middle-aged, middle to high-income consumers. If Apple were to charge significantly different prices across these segments without commercial justification, it could face ACCC enforcement action. This means that when identifying target markets, businesses must carefully consider whether price differentiation between segments could be considered illegal price discrimination. Although Apple can still segment based on demographics, geography, psychographics and behaviour, the pricing across those segments must not substantially reduce competition. Therefore, price discrimination law has a moderate impact on identifying target markets because while it does not prevent segmentation itself, it constrains the flexibility of pricing strategies that businesses can apply to different market segments.

## Body Paragraph 3 — Deceptive and Misleading Advertising → Developing Marketing Strategies

Consumer law regarding deceptive and misleading conduct has a significant impact on Apple's marketing process stage of developing marketing strategies. Deceptive and misleading advertising, which includes tactics such as bait and switch advertising, fine print, false country of origin claims and misleading packaging, is prohibited under the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 enforced by the ACCC. This directly constrains how businesses develop their promotional strategies within the marketing mix, as all advertising must be truthful and substantiated. For example, Apple was fined $9 million by the ACCC for misrepresentations regarding iPhone and iPad consumer guarantee rights. This enforcement action forced Apple to revise its marketing strategies around after-sales service and product guarantees, ensuring all promotional material accurately reflected consumers' legal rights under Australian Consumer Law. As a result, businesses must invest significant resources into legal review of marketing strategies before implementation, which can limit the creativity and aggressiveness of promotional campaigns. Therefore, deceptive and misleading advertising laws have a substantial impact on developing marketing strategies because they establish clear boundaries that businesses cannot cross, shaping how they communicate value to consumers while still allowing them to differentiate from competitors through truthful promotion.

## Body Paragraph 4 — Truth, Accuracy and Good Taste → Developing Marketing Strategies

Ethical issues relating to truth, accuracy and good taste in advertising also have a significant impact on Apple's marketing process stage of developing marketing strategies. Ethical advertising requires that businesses present their products honestly, without exaggerating capabilities or concealing limitations, and that advertisements are appropriate for the target audience. This further constrains how businesses develop their promotional strategies because even if advertising is technically legal, it may still be considered unethical if it misleads consumers. For example, Apple's marketing of battery life on iPhones and MacBooks has been questioned by consumers and tech reviewers who found that real world performance often fell short of what was claimed in advertisements. When a company overstates how long a battery lasts, even if it is not technically illegal, it raises ethical concerns about whether consumers are being given accurate information to make purchasing decisions. If Apple's advertising is seen as exaggerated, word spreads quickly through social media and online reviews, which damages brand trust and can slow adoption rates. Over time, persistent questions about advertising accuracy can also attract regulatory scrutiny from advertising standards bodies. Therefore, ethical issues around truth, accuracy and good taste have a significant impact on developing marketing strategies because they require businesses to balance persuasive promotion with honest communication to maintain long term customer relationships and brand credibility.

## Body Paragraph 5 — Implied Conditions → Implementation, Monitoring and Controlling

Implied conditions under the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 have a significant impact on Apple's marketing process stage of implementation, monitoring and controlling. Implied conditions are unspoken and unwritten terms of a contract that exist regardless of whether they are written into the agreement, such as fitness for particular purpose, meaning a product must be suitable for the purpose it was sold for. This impacts how a business monitors and controls its marketing activities because the business must ensure that the products it delivers match the promises made during promotion. For example, Apple markets its iPhones as having specific water resistance ratings and battery performance. Under the implied condition of fitness for particular purpose, these products must perform as advertised. If the iPhone is unable to perform its prime functions such as making calls or sending messages, this becomes a legal issue and the customer is entitled to a refund or replacement. Apple also had an issue with claims that their iPhones had more storage than they actually had, because the operating system took up a large portion of the advertised space. This breached the implied conditions under the CCA and meant Apple could face fines for misleading their customers. Since Apple has faced ACCC action over consumer guarantee representations, they were forced to improve their policies and practices around product fitness and repair rights. This means that during implementation, monitoring and controlling, businesses must establish quality assurance processes and customer feedback mechanisms to ensure ongoing compliance. Therefore, implied conditions have a substantial impact on this marketing process stage because they require continuous monitoring of product quality and customer satisfaction to ensure the business is meeting its legal obligations and maintaining consumer trust.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, economic factors significantly impact market research by shaping what consumer data needs to be collected during changing economic conditions. Price discrimination moderately impacts identifying target markets by constraining how Apple can price differently across segments. Deceptive and misleading advertising laws and ethical issues around truth, accuracy and good taste both significantly impact developing marketing strategies by establishing legal and ethical boundaries for promotion. Implied conditions significantly impact implementation, monitoring and controlling by requiring continuous compliance with product fitness standards. These influences demonstrate that the marketing process does not operate in isolation but is continuously shaped by economic, legal, and ethical forces that require constant attention and adaptation.
