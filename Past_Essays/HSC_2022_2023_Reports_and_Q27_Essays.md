# HSC Business Studies: 2022 and 2023 Answers

Prepared from the two most recent Business Studies exam papers in Downloads, 2022 and 2023, using the Business Studies Obsidian vault notes.

## 2022 HSC Business Studies

### Question 25: Business Report

Prompt: Bee Sticky Pty Ltd is an established Australian manufacturing business that produces high quality, premium organic honey. It is expanding into global markets and will lease warehouses in Malaysia and the USA. Its strengths are its high quality organic honey, approval for consumption and medical uses, and Australian flora origins. Its weaknesses include product breakage during transportation and limited experience with foreign payment systems. Opportunities include overseas demand for high quality honey, falling bee populations overseas, and collaborations with upmarket restaurants and chefs. Threats include established foreign competitors and fluctuations in the Australian dollar. The report must describe branding and product strategies, recommend a channel choice, and analyse global financial management strategies.

#### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Bee Sticky Pty Ltd is an established Australian producer of premium organic honey made from Australian flora, with products approved for consumption and medical uses. The business plans to expand into Malaysia and the USA and lease warehouses in both markets. This report recommends consistent global branding and market appropriate product strategies, selective distribution through chosen specialist food retailers and restaurant partners, and financial controls including foreign exchange hedging, secure international payment and cash flow budgeting. Together, these strategies address transport breakage, overseas competition, unfamiliar payment systems and Australian dollar fluctuations while supporting profitable expansion.

#### BRANDING AND PRODUCT STRATEGIES

Bee Sticky should adopt global branding by using the same name and logo in Malaysia and the USA. The brand should consistently communicate premium organic honey made from Australian flora. This clear identity would help consumers recognise Bee Sticky and distinguish it from established competitors. Promoting one brand is also more efficient than marketing each product separately. Before launch, the business should check that its name and visual identity are suitable in each market.

Bee Sticky should standardise its core honey so customers receive consistent quality, while adapting packaging and labelling to local preferences and rules. Since transport breakage is a stated weakness, it should test protective packaging for long distance shipping. This could reduce damaged stock and refunds while preserving the premium presentation. Labels should accurately display the Australian flora origin, organic status and product information.

Bee Sticky could offer consumer jars for retailers and larger food service packs for restaurant and chef partners. Falling bee populations overseas may make a reliable imported supply attractive, allowing Bee Sticky to position Australian flora honey as an alternative. It must verify food safety, organic certification and labelling requirements in each market, and confirm whether medical use claims are approved locally. Australian approval alone should not be assumed to apply overseas.

#### RECOMMENDED CHANNEL CHOICE

Bee Sticky should use selective distribution through chosen specialist gourmet retailers and upmarket restaurant partners. This moderate outlet coverage suits its premium positioning and targets customers seeking high quality honey. It also uses the opportunity to collaborate with restaurants and chefs, while selected outlets can help protect the product’s image against established competitors.

A one level channel from Bee Sticky to specialist retailers and then consumers avoids unnecessary intermediaries, while restaurant accounts can be supplied directly from its warehouses. Local stock can support faster delivery and closer handling checks. The planned warehouses can act as local hubs to store stock and fulfil orders for selected accounts. Although selective distribution may build awareness more slowly than intensive distribution, it gives Bee Sticky greater control while it tests demand in two new markets.

#### GLOBAL FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES

Bee Sticky faces foreign exchange risk because the Australian dollar fluctuates and overseas sales or warehouse expenses may be paid in US dollars or Malaysian ringgit. Currency movements can reduce the Australian dollar value of export receipts or increase operating costs. A forward exchange contract can lock an agreed rate for predictable future transactions, making cash flow and pricing more certain. If the Australian dollar appreciates, Bee Sticky’s honey may become more expensive to overseas buyers, while foreign receipts convert into fewer Australian dollars. It should review exchange rates when setting prices and updating budgets. However, Bee Sticky could miss out if rates later move in its favour, so it should hedge known commitments rather than speculate on all sales.

Because Bee Sticky has limited experience with foreign payment systems, it should request a letter of credit for new overseas customers. The buyer’s bank guarantees payment when agreed conditions and documents are met, reducing the risk of shipping honey and receiving no payment. This is safer than clean payment, where goods are sent before funds arrive. Letters of credit involve bank fees and administration, so Bee Sticky should compare those costs with the value and volume of each shipment. The payment security is valuable when dealing with unfamiliar customers.

Bee Sticky should prepare cash flow budgets for Malaysia and the USA, including warehouse rent, inventory, freight, insurance, customs charges, taxes and customer receipts. Comparing actual results with forecasts helps identify shortfalls early and supports reliable supply. Where possible, local currency revenue can pay local expenses, matching currency inflows and outflows to reduce conversion risk. Export prices should also account for freight, warehousing, tariffs and exchange rates so that sales produce an adequate margin.

#### CONCLUSION

Bee Sticky can expand more successfully by protecting its premium Australian brand while adapting packaging and labels for each market, using selective distribution to reach suitable customers, and controlling currency, payment and cash flow risks. These strategies directly address its transportation and financial weaknesses while using overseas demand and restaurant partnerships to compete in Malaysia and the USA.

### Question 27: Essay

Prompt: Explain the importance to a business of having operations processes that can maintain a competitive advantage.

Operations processes transform inputs into outputs and add value through the way a business designs, produces and delivers its goods or services. They are highly important to Apple because effective processes allow it to pursue product differentiation through innovative, reliable products while also controlling costs and delivery. If Apple’s operations fail to meet customer expectations or supply products dependably, its premium reputation and competitive advantage can weaken. Apple therefore needs processes that coordinate its global suppliers, technology, production and quality control.

Apple’s inputs are central to maintaining this advantage. The operations case study identifies a supply chain of about 785 suppliers across 31 countries, giving Apple access to specialised components and large scale production. Effective sourcing can help Apple secure the materials and expertise needed to produce products such as iPhones, iPads and Macs, while spreading supply across international markets. This supports quality and product variety, and supplier competition can help control input costs. However, a large global network also creates complexity. COVID 19 delayed the iPhone 12 and the 2021 chip shortage constrained production, showing how disruption to inputs can reduce dependability and sales. Apple must therefore manage supplier relationships and inventory carefully so that its operations continue to deliver products when customers expect them.

The transformation process is equally important because it changes components and information into finished Apple products. Apple’s outsourcing of production to Foxconn provides access to manufacturing expertise and economies of scale, helping the company produce high volumes at lower cost than it may achieve alone. This can support cost leadership while allowing Apple to focus on design and product development. The process also carries risks, including reduced direct quality control, security concerns and communication barriers. Apple must set clear production standards and monitor suppliers so that cost savings do not undermine the quality associated with its brand. Its movement of some production from China to India and its development of in house chips also show how Apple can adjust its transformation process to improve control, cost and resilience.

Technology and process improvement help Apple differentiate its outputs from competing products. By developing its own chips and improving product design, Apple can control important product features and create devices that work within its broader product ecosystem. This supports differentiation because customers may value performance, ease of use and compatibility, rather than choosing only on price. Technology can also improve efficiency and consistency during production. However, leading edge technology is expensive and can be untested, so Apple must ensure that product development, testing and manufacturing are coordinated. The value of technology depends on whether it produces outputs that customers consider useful and reliable, not simply on whether the technology is new.

Quality management is vital to Apple because its customers expect durable, well designed and reliable products at premium prices. Quality checks throughout the transformation process can identify defective components before products reach customers, reducing returns, repair costs and damage to Apple’s reputation. The Apple Maps problems in 2012 and the facial recognition failure associated with the iPhone X in 2018 show that failures in product design or testing can disappoint users even when a product is innovative. These examples demonstrate why Apple must test software and hardware in realistic conditions, respond to customer feedback and improve later products. Reliable outputs strengthen customer satisfaction and loyalty, which makes it harder for competitors to take Apple’s customers.

Overall, well managed operations processes are important to Apple’s competitive advantage because they coordinate reliable inputs, efficient transformation and high quality outputs. Apple’s global sourcing and outsourcing can reduce costs and provide expertise, while technology and quality management support product differentiation. These processes need continual monitoring because shortages, supplier failures or product defects can quickly weaken dependability and reputation. Apple can maintain its advantage only when each stage of its operations process consistently creates value for customers.

## 2023 HSC Business Studies

### Question 25: Business Report

Prompt: Future Century Gaming Pty Ltd operates an online gaming platform for players aged 18 to 40 in Australia and has done so since 2012. It is considering expansion into emerging global markets but has difficulty recruiting and retaining skilled, qualified game designers and developers in Australia. It is considering international labour markets to overcome staff shortages and will finance the expansion through external sources. The report must outline global recruitment and retention challenges, compare debt and equity, and recommend one source of finance.

#### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Future Century Gaming Pty Ltd has operated an Australian online gaming platform for players aged 18 to 40 since 2012. Its planned global expansion is constrained by difficulty recruiting and retaining skilled game designers and developers in Australia. This report outlines global recruitment challenges, compares external debt and equity finance, and recommends private equity from a suitable investor. This source avoids fixed repayments while funding market entry and workforce growth.

#### GLOBAL RECRUITMENT AND RETENTION CHALLENGES

Future Century Gaming must compare the full cost of recruiting in Australia with international labour markets. Wage levels and employment benefits may differ between countries, but lower salaries alone do not guarantee lower total costs. Recruitment agencies, relocation, induction, training, exchange rates, employment regulation and remote work technology can add significant expenses. The company should also budget for time zone coordination and secure systems for collaborative game development. If it chooses a market based only on wages, it may face costs from weak communication, staff turnover or work that does not meet the platform’s quality requirements.

The availability and quality of skills are equally important. Future Century Gaming needs qualified game designers and developers with the right mix of creative ability, programming knowledge and relevant industry experience. It should assess each labour market through qualifications, portfolios, references and practical assessments rather than assuming a large population contains suitable talent. Competition from gaming and technology businesses may make skilled workers difficult to attract. Recruiting employees who lack required skills could increase training costs and delay product development, while skilled employees who receive little support may leave.

Labour supply and retention also vary between countries. Future Century Gaming should investigate how many suitably skilled employees are available, whether those workers are concentrated in particular cities, and how strongly other employers compete for them. It must consider local labour regulations, political stability, language and cultural differences, and infrastructure for online collaboration. Reliable internet and compatible equipment are important for maintaining communication and project schedules. Once recruited, international staff need clear induction, ongoing training, career development, fair rewards and effective communication with Australian employees. If workers feel disconnected from the team, they may leave, increasing recruitment costs and interrupting development work.

#### COMPARISON OF DEBT AND EQUITY FINANCE

Debt finance is money borrowed from an external lender, such as a bank, and must be repaid with interest. Its main advantage is that Future Century Gaming’s existing owners retain ownership and decision making control. Interest expenses may also be tax deductible. However, loan repayments are compulsory regardless of whether the global expansion immediately generates revenue. This creates a cash flow risk if market entry is slower than expected or recruitment costs rise. Lenders may also require security, and an increase in interest rates can make debt more expensive.

Equity finance is funding provided in exchange for ownership shares. As Future Century Gaming is a proprietary company, it could seek private equity from selected investors rather than offer shares to the public. Equity does not require fixed interest payments or repayment of the investment, and dividends are not guaranteed. This can preserve cash for hiring, training and market entry. In return, existing owners dilute their share of ownership and may have to share some control with investors. Finding an investor can also take time, and the company must choose a partner whose expectations fit its long term objectives.

#### RECOMMENDATION: PRIVATE EQUITY

It is recommended that Future Century Gaming seek private equity from a carefully selected investor with experience in technology or gaming. This is an external equity source suited to a growth project where the timing and size of returns from emerging markets are uncertain. Unlike a loan, the investment would not require scheduled interest and principal repayments, giving the company more flexibility to fund recruitment, retain skilled staff and develop its platform before overseas revenue is established. A knowledgeable investor may also provide useful industry contacts or commercial advice, although this should not be assumed unless agreed.

Private equity will dilute the founders’ ownership, so Future Century Gaming should negotiate the shareholding and decision making arrangements before accepting investment. Even with that trade off, equity is preferable to taking on substantial debt because it reduces immediate cash flow pressure during expansion. The company can therefore devote more resources to attracting and retaining the specialist employees on whom its global growth depends.

#### CONCLUSION

Future Century Gaming should assess international labour markets using total employment costs, the availability and quality of skills, and the ability to retain a geographically diverse workforce. It should compare debt’s ownership benefits with the repayment risk against equity’s lower cash flow burden and ownership dilution. Private equity from a suitable investor is the strongest funding choice because it supports expansion without fixed debt servicing while the company builds its global workforce and customer base.

### Question 27: Essay

Prompt: To what extent do influences on operations affect business success?

Influences on operations affect business success to a great extent because they shape production costs, product quality, supply reliability and a business’s ability to meet customer expectations. However, an influence does not determine success by itself. The outcome depends on how effectively management responds to it. Apple demonstrates this through its responses to globalisation, technological change, quality expectations and cost based competition. These influences have created major opportunities for Apple, but supply disruptions and product failures show that they can also threaten success.

Globalisation has strongly affected Apple’s operations by enabling international sourcing, production and access to large markets. Apple’s supply chain includes about 785 suppliers across 31 countries, while outsourcing production to Foxconn gives it access to manufacturing expertise and economies of scale. This can lower unit costs and allow Apple to produce high volumes of iPhones and other devices. However, dependence on a global network exposes Apple to disruption. COVID 19 delayed the iPhone 12 and the 2021 chip shortage restricted production, threatening dependability and sales. Apple’s efforts to reduce suppliers and warehouses show that management can simplify coordination, but it must balance efficiency with the risk of relying too heavily on a limited number of suppliers. Globalisation therefore affects Apple’s success to a great extent, both by creating cost advantages and by increasing operational risk.

Technological change also has a major influence because Apple competes in a market where product features and production capabilities change rapidly. Apple’s development of in house chips and investment in product design allow it to control important components and differentiate its devices. In operations, technology can increase efficiency, improve consistency and support higher productivity. This helps Apple offer products that customers may view as innovative and integrated. However, leading edge technology requires substantial investment and may not work as intended. Apple Maps experienced problems in 2012, demonstrating that new technology can harm customer confidence if it is released before it performs reliably. Technology contributes to Apple’s success only when research, testing and production processes turn innovation into a dependable customer benefit.

Quality expectations influence Apple because its premium positioning leads customers to expect reliable, durable and well designed products. If Apple meets these expectations, satisfied customers are more likely to remain loyal, supporting repeat sales and allowing Apple to differentiate itself from lower priced competitors. If it fails, customers may face defects or unreliable features, damaging Apple’s reputation and increasing repair and support costs. The facial recognition failure associated with the iPhone X in 2018 illustrates how a weakness in a new product can undermine confidence despite the appeal of its technology. Apple must therefore monitor suppliers, test components and software, and use customer feedback to improve subsequent products. Quality expectations have a substantial effect on success because Apple’s brand depends on the experience its operations deliver, not only on its advertising.

Cost based competition further pressures Apple to keep operations efficient without destroying the quality that supports its premium brand. The company has used lower cost components in the iPhone SE, developed chips in house and shifted some production from China to India. These choices can reduce costs, diversify production and protect margins, helping Apple remain competitive as costs and supply conditions change. At the same time, excessive cost cutting could reduce component quality or create new supply problems. Apple must compare the savings with their effects on reliability, speed and customer satisfaction. Overall, cost pressure is an important influence, but it benefits Apple only when efficiencies support its broader value proposition rather than weakening its products.

To a great extent, globalisation, technology, quality expectations and cost based competition shape Apple’s operational performance and therefore its business success. Apple gains from global scale, innovation and cost control, but shortages and product failures demonstrate the consequences of weak coordination or insufficient testing. Success depends on Apple identifying these influences early and adapting its operations while protecting quality and dependability. The influences are powerful, but Apple’s response determines whether they become opportunities or threats.
